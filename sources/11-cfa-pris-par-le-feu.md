## Les passages

### Dans l'auto

> If you have nowhere else to shelter, stay in your car. You are better off in a car than out in the open
> when the fire front arrives.

> Park the car: away from bushes, trees and other cars as they can catch alight easily / behind a barrier
> like a brick wall or a rocky outcrop / in a cleared area such as on gravel, an oval, or a burnt out
> paddock. / facing the oncoming fire front.

> Turn headlights and hazard lights on so you are visible to other drivers and emergency services. Close
> windows and air vents. Turn engine off when fire is close by. Passengers get down below the window line,
> as close to the floor as possible, to protect from radiant heat and toxic gases.

> Shelter under a dry blanket made of pure wool. Wool can protect you from radiant heat. Do not wet
> blankets as the extreme heat will give you steam burns.

> Cover your mouth with a cloth or P2 mask so you're not breathing in as much smoke or fumes. Drink water
> so you don't get dehydrated. Even if you are not thirsty.

> Once the fire has passed, check if it's safe to get out of the car by holding up your hand and feeling
> the air. If it is hotter than when skin is badly sunburned, it's still too hot to get out from under the
> blanket. [...] Once you're out of the car, stay covered up in dry woollen blankets and move to a safer
> place, like burnt ground.

> They died getting out of their cars trying to outrun the fire through dense smoke. The six people who
> sheltered in their cars survived. (feu de Lara, 1969 : 17 morts)

> Keep a kit with essential supplies in your car during Summer. We recommend including: Woollen blankets
> [...] Water [...] P2 masks

### Dans la maison

> Turn off air conditioners and evaporative coolers that could suck in embers and catch fire.

> Wear loose-fitting clothes made from natural fibres like pure wool, heavy cotton drill or denim. Put on
> sturdy leather boots [...] Close curtains or blinds to protect yourself from radiant heat.

> Make sure you are in a space with two exits to the outside at all times. This could be two doors, or a
> door and a window. Do not shelter in areas without access to the outside, such as a basement, bathroom or
> bedrooms. Regularly peek through curtains or blinds to see what is happening with the fire.

> Check for embers in the roof space. [...] Put out spot fires from embers if you can.

> Close the door to the room that is on fire. Get away from the fire as it moves through the house,
> closing doors behind you. [...] Keep down low to minimise breathing in toxic smoke.

> Don't stay inside if there is burnt ground or better shelter outside.

> Burning embers landing around your property for hours before or after the main fire front has passed

Fiche travailleurs : « Fill sinks or baths with water » ; « Move outside to burnt ground as soon as you
safely can. »

### À découvert

> Try to find burnt ground, a ploughed paddock or reserve, a body of water, a large, solid object like a
> brick wall or concrete water tank.

> Put separation distance between yourself and the fire [...] Put separation distance between yourself and
> any vegetation [...] Cover up any exposed skin with natural fibres, such as pure wool, denim or drill
> cotton

## Ce que le chapitre 11 en tire

| Fait | Où |
| --- | --- |
| Rattrapé en auto : s'arrêter où rien ne brûle, l'avant vers le feu, phares et feux d'urgence allumés, vitres et entrées d'air fermées, moteur coupé quand le feu est proche, au plancher sous la laine sèche, un linge sur la bouche, boire | étapes 18 et 19 |
| Ne pas mouiller la couverture : la vapeur brûle | section 8, point 6 |
| Sortir de l'auto quand l'air ne brûle plus comme un gros coup de soleil, sous la laine, vers le terrain brûlé | étape 20 |
| Sortir de l'auto pour courir tue (Lara, 1969) | section 8, point 6 |
| Une couverture de laine par personne dans l'auto | section 4, étape 3, section 7 |
| Au bâtiment : climatiseur arrêté, rideaux fermés, coton ou laine et bottes, pièce à deux sorties, pas au sous-sol, éteindre les braises, fermer la porte de la pièce qui brûle, sortir vers le terrain brûlé | étapes 21 à 23 |
| Les braises tombent pendant des heures après le front | étape 26 |
| À découvert : distance du feu et de la végétation, terrain brûlé, eau, gros rocher, peau couverte de laine ou de coton | étape 24 |

**Confrontations tranchées par cette page :**
- Rester dans l'auto ou en sortir : on reste (Lara, 1969), et on sort seulement quand l'air a refroidi.
- Moteur : coupé quand le feu est proche. Le chapitre disait déjà de ne pas laisser tourner le moteur à l'arrêt
  dans la fumée ; la phrase sur le monoxyde « additionné » du véhicule, sans source, est coupée.
- Couverture mouillée ou sèche : sèche. Le §9 de l'ancien texte disait « laine ou coton mouillés sur la peau » ;
  corrigé.
- La pièce d'air filtré (petite, sans porte extérieure) contre la pièce à deux sorties : la première sert contre
  la fumée, la seconde pendant le passage du front (étape 22).
- Fermer les rideaux (abri) contre les décrocher (départ, CAL FIRE) : deux situations ; au départ personne n'est
  là pour éteindre un rideau qui prend.
