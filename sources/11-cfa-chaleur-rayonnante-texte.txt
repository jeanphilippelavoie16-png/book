Radiant heat
5 min read
Radiant heat is extremely dangerous. It can kill you well before the flames from a bushfire reach you. The safest option is always to leave early. This page will help you learn how to survive radiant heat if you can't leave.
Leaving early is ALWAYS your safest option.
Summary
- The best way to avoid radiant heat is to leave early on extreme fire risk days.
- Distance is the best protection from radiant heat.
- Cover up from head to toe with clothes, gloves, hat and goggles to protect your skin and eyes.
- Take shelter in a building or behind a solid object or wall.
What is radiant heat?
Radiant heat is the intense heat that comes from a bushfire. Radiant heat is the biggest killer in bushfires—you can die from organ failure long before the flames reach you.
Radiant heat:
- is blocked by solid objects
- travels in straight lines (it can't go around corners)
- travels through glass
- can cause injury or even death.

Every summer, bushfires threaten properties and lives, but did you know it's not the flames that kill most bushfire victims? No, it's the radiant heat.

Radiant heat is what you feel when you sit next to a campfire. If a campfire heats up to two kilowatts per square metre, you'll feel that it's too hot and will want to move back from the fire. If you don't this amount of radiant heat is enough to cause burns and blisters in as little as 40 seconds.
At 12 kilowatts, it can cause some materials like dry timber to ignite.
A bushfire can reach 100 kilowatts, and the effects can be truly catastrophic.
For humans, radiant heat can cause burns from 100 metres away and cause a dangerous increase in body temperature.
Radiant heat can cause the rapid onset of heatstroke. Heatstroke damages your brain, meaning you won't be able to concentrate to make good decisions as the fire arrives. Other impacts include severe damage of internal organs and death.
There are some things you can do if you are caught in a fire, cover your skin with long-sleeved, natural-fibre clothing like wool.
It's also useful to know that radiant heat only travels in straight lines and can't bend around corners. So, sheltering behind or inside solid structures may help protect you. But be aware, radiant heat will travel straight through glass.
The best defence against radiant heat is a simple one.
If you're not anywhere near a bushfire, its radiant heat can't hurt you.

How to survive radiant heat
Know when to leave
Leaving early on extreme fire risk days is always your safest option. Know what to do if you get caught out.
Late evacuation is extremely dangerous and can expose you to extreme levels of radiant heat. You could be seriously injured or die.
Distance
If you can't leave, the best way to survive radiant heat is to get as far away from the fire as possible. The closer you are to a fire, the more dangerous radiant heat will be. The further away you are, the less dangerous radiant heat is.
How to block radiant heat
1. Cover up

Protect yourself from radiant heat
What to wear for protection from radiant heat and to increase your chances of survival:
- Wide-brimmed hat to protect head.
- Goggles to shield eyes.
- Mask or scarf/handkerchief to protect face and filter smoke.
- Long-sleeved, collared shirt with the collar up to protect neck.
- Long pants made from natural fibre like cotton or denim. Not stretch denim which contains synthetics that can melt onto your skin.
- Tough leather gardening gloves. Not rubber or synthetic.
- Sturdy boots and wool or cotton socks.
2. Take shelter
Radiant heat is the intense heat that comes from a fire. It can kill you. The best protection against radiant heat is distance.
A solid object, such as a brick wall or boulder can provide some protection from radiant heat.
If you can't escape the fire, take shelter:
- in a building
- behind a solid wall,
- behind a water tank or
- behind a boulder or another solid object.
Do not hide in front of a window as heat can travel through glass.
Sheltering in a car is a last resort option
Sheltering in a car is extremely dangerous. However, if you are not able to get to safety or shelter in a building, shelter in your car.
If you're forced to shelter in your car, your chance of survival is very low, but better than being in the open. To protect yourself from radiant heat:
- Get down below window level as radiant heat will pass through the glass windows.
- Cover yourself with woollen blankets to protect from radiant heat. Use pure wool blankets as synthetic materials will
melt.
