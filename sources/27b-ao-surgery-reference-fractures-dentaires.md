## Extraits intégraux

**1. Pulpe exposée (« complicated crown fracture ») :**

> « A fracture which involves enamel and dentin, exposing the pulp. [...] Another term for
> enamel-dentin-pulp fracture is "complicated crown fracture". »

> « Clinical findings: The crown fracture shows exposed pulp tissue that may present itself in the
> centre as **a small red spot or a regular bleeding structure**. »

**2. Fracture couronne-racine, définition :**

> « the fracture by definition crosses the seal of the gingival crevice, thereby involving the
> coronal part as well as the intraalveolar part of the tooth »

> « Clinical findings: **The coronal fragment is frequently in-situ and attached to the
> gingiva.** »

**3. Fracture couronne-racine, traitements :**

> « Removal of a superficial coronal crown-root fragment and subsequent restoration of exposed
> dentin above the gingival level. This is only indicated in fractures without pulpal exposure. »

> « When an implant solution is planned, after removal of the crown **the root fragment may be
> left in situ** just below the alveolar crest (coronectomy) in order to avoid alveolar
> resorption, thus maintaining the volume of the alveolar process »

> « All of the treatment modalities (except extraction) are technique sensitive and do not need to
> be performed in the acute phase. Instead, the coronal fragment can be temporarily bonded to the
> cervical portion of the tooth with a composite or resin. [...] Prognosis will not be influenced
> by delay of treatment within a time frame of 1 to 2 weeks. »

**4. Fracture de racine, attelle :**

> « **Almost all root fractures can be treated successfully by timely repositioning and
> splinting.** If the coronal fragment is missing, and if there are no serious reasons for
> retaining the apical fragment (which might help maintenance of alveolar volume) it should be
> extracted. »

> « The tooth is repositioned with light axial digital pressure until its normal position has been
> reestablished. »

> « Resin material [...] is applied to provide a splint. This material allows a certain
> flexibility of the splint and is easy to remove. In the case of a root fracture **fixation should
> remain for 4 weeks.** »

> « Patients are encouraged to restrict themselves to a semi-solid diet and avoid clenching [...]
> for a period of 1 or 2 weeks posttrauma. »
