# Alaska Department of Fish and Game — L'ours après le tir, et le site d'abattage

Deux pages du même organisme, regroupées parce que le chapitre s'en sert pour la même chose :
l'ours blessé, et ce qui arrive autour d'une carcasse laissée la nuit.

- **Organisme :** Alaska Department of Fish and Game (ADF&G), organisme gouvernemental
- **Page 1 :** « Bear Baiting: Shot Placement » (éducation des chasseurs) —
  https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/index.cfm?adfg=bearbaiting.shooting
- **Page 2 :** « Bear Safety for Hunters » — https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/index.cfm?adfg=hunting.bearsafety
- **Date de consultation :** 2026-09-23 (pages téléchargées, texte extrait)
- **Langue :** anglais
- **Chapitre qui s'en sert :** 35d (§5-B « l'ours », §7, §8)

---

## Page 1 — extrait intégral (paragraphe après le tir)

> « Patience is also a virtue after you've made your shot. If the bear runs out of sight, it is
> advisable to wait at least 30 minutes before tracking the bear, even if you have hit the bear in
> the lungs. If you follow a wounded bear too soon, its adrenaline will likely kick in and it may
> escape any efforts you make to track it. The further a bear runs from the bait site, the more
> difficult it will be to find. For this reason, you should get very familiar with your area
> beforehand; know where the bear trails lead and where the main game trails are located. Also
> identify any areas of heavy cover such as a swamp or marsh; a wounded bear will likely head for
> such a feature. Carry fluorescent trail marking tape with you, and affix pieces as you follow the
> blood trail. Rain can quickly eliminate a blood trail, so having a marked trail may assist in
> reconstructing the travel of the wounded bear. […] When approaching a downed bear, be very
> careful. Always approach from behind and above the animal and be ready for a follow-up shot. »

## Page 2 — extraits intégraux (le site d'abattage)

> « Be alert — Bears may be drawn to the kill site. Move the meat away from brushy areas as soon as
> you can. »

> « Mark the area — Use flagging tape to mark the entrails and the meat cache and likely approaches
> to it. When you come back for your second load of meat, you will know where your attention needs
> to be directed. […] Approach flagged areas cautiously and be on the lookout for bears. »

> « Avoid leaving a blood trail back to your camp — Pack, do not drag, all the legally required
> salvaged meat and parts to the area where you will hang and store your meat. »

## Ce que le chapitre en tire

- **Ours touché aux poumons : au moins 30 minutes** avant de suivre (tableau des attentes).
- **L'ours blessé va au couvert épais** : marécage, tourbière, aulnaie (§5-B, « où elle va »).
- **La pluie efface une piste de sang** : on marque à mesure, pas au retour (§5-B).
- **Approche par l'arrière et par le haut, arme prête** (§8, renvoi ch. 36 et 48).
- **Une carcasse restée la nuit attire l'ours** ; on revient vers elle comme vers un endroit
  occupé (§7, §8).
