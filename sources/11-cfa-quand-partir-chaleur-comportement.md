## Les passages

### Quand partir

> leave the night before or by 10 am on days rated as Extreme or Catastrophic.

> Leaving early does not mean: [...] starting to pack your car when you smell smoke / trying to secure
> your home when you can see fire

> going to your planned safe destination with supplies for three days

> Don't wait until you can smell smoke or see flames.

> Many people who try to defend their property end up feeling overwhelmed and try to flee at the last
> minute. This action can be deadly.

> Thick smoke can make it hard to see, and your headlights won't help much. You may need to drive very
> slowly. Trees might fall on the roads

### La chaleur rayonnante (transcription de la vidéo et texte)

> Radiant heat is the biggest killer in bushfires—you can die from organ failure long before the flames
> reach you.

> For humans, radiant heat can cause burns from 100 metres away and cause a dangerous increase in body
> temperature. Radiant heat can cause the rapid onset of heatstroke.

> travels through glass

### Le comportement du feu

> Typically bushfires become less intense at night when the temperature drops and humidity increases.

> A fire travels much faster uphill and slower as it goes downhill. The wind can also be funnelled through
> a valley, changing the direction of a fire.

> When this happens, the side of the fire can suddenly become the new fire front, much wider and more
> dangerous than the original one.

> The 'crackle' test is a practical way to assess if moisture content of vegetation is low. As you walk
> across leaf and bark litter, the sharper the crackle sound, the drier the fuel.
