## Extraits intégraux

### Salage

> « Salt the hide: Spread the hide, hair side down, on a flat surface. Sprinkle fresh, clean salt
> over the flesh side of the hide, using a pound of salt for each pound of hide. Be sure to
> sprinkle salt on all parts of the flesh side; rub the salt into the cut edges, neck, legs and
> wrinkles. Remember, any unsalted spot is unprotected. The salting procedure should be repeated
> after the first application of salt becomes saturated with moisture, usually in two to three
> days. »

> « Tilt the pile slightly so liquid from the hides drains away from the pile and doesn't collect
> on the bottom hide. »

> « Dry the hide: Position the hide so that fluid can easily drain from it. The salt will serve to
> remove most of the moisture from the hide in 10 to 14 days. »

> « Do not keep untanned hides or skins over the summer because they may deteriorate and be
> damaged by insects. »

### Épilation à la chaux

> « To dehair, mix 4 to 5 quarts hydrated lime with 5 gallons of water. Make sure the hide is
> completely immersed and no air is trapped in the hide. Soak the hide until the hair slides off
> easily with a push of your hand (6 to 10 days). »

> « After the hide has been dehaired, soak it in clean water for four or five hours, then scud the
> skin again. »

> « Fill a container with 10 gallons of water and stir in 1 ounce of United States Pharmacopoeia
> (USP) lactic acid […] (If you cannot get lactic acid, substitute 1 pint of vinegar for each ounce
> of lactic acid.) Soak the hide in this solution for 24 hours to stop the action of the lime. »

### Sel et alun

> « Prepare a salt-alum tanning solution by dissolving 1 pound of ammonia alum or potash alum in
> 1 gallon of water. Dissolve 4 ounces of washing soda (crystallized sodium carbonate) and 8 ounces
> of salt in 1/2 gallon of water. Pour the soda-salt solution very slowly into the alum solution
> while stirring vigorously. The soda-salt solution must be poured slowly enough to prevent an
> overflow of foam from the container. Amounts may need to be increased proportionately for larger
> skins. »

> « Immerse the skin in the tanning solution for 2 to 5 days, depending upon its thickness. Two
> days should be sufficient for a rabbit skin, while a deer hide may require up to 5 days. Since a
> hide cannot easily be over-tanned using this process, it would be better to leave the hide in
> solution too long rather than not long enough. »

> « Because alum affects some furs, it may be best to first test a scrap piece of the fur in the
> tanning solution. »

> « When properly carried out, it produces skins with stretch and flexibility. However,
> alum-tanned skins often come out stiff and hard and must be worked repeatedly or sometimes
> retanned. »

> « Rinse the hide clean in a gallon of water containing about 1 ounce of Borax. »

### Tannage au cerveau

> « Prepare the tanning solution by combining 1 pound of pig or horse brain with 2 gallons of warm
> water. For best results, use untreated water such as rainwater. […] Water treated with chlorine
> may reduce the effectiveness of tanning solution. »

> « While the hide is still damp and pliable from preparation and cleaning procedures, immerse it
> in the brain tanning solution. Soak the hide overnight. »

> « The hide should be worked by pushing and stretching it in a stroking motion until it dries. »

> « The final step for brain tanning is smoking the hide. Brain tanned hides are most durable if
> they are smoked for several hours in a smokehouse. However, be careful not to heat the hide too
> much. Use dry, semi-rotten wood to produce lots of smoke and low heat. »

### Assouplissement

> « Success in producing a soft skin lies in repeated working, which must be done while the skin
> is drying out, not after it is dry. »
