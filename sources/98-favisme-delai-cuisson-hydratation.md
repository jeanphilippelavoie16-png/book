# Favisme : délai des signes, fève crue ou cuite, ce que les liquides protègent

Trois pages enregistrées le 27 septembre 2026 pour lever les drapeaux 2 à 4 du ch. 98 (§7 et §8,
bloc 5). Aucune page gouvernementale canadienne ou française ne traite du favisme en détail : on
prend une revue universitaire en libre accès (CC BY), la fiche d'une organisation nationale de
maladies rares (NORD, États-Unis) et une série de cas de néphrologie (CC BY 4.0).

  doi:10.3390/nu15020343 ; licence CC BY 4.0. Revue de tous les cas publiés, classés par âge.

  intégral pris par Europe PMC :
  https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9864644/fullTextXML
- **Consultée le :** 27 septembre 2026 (HTTP 200)
- **Copie :** `sources/98-europepmc-favisme-ages.xml`

### Extraits intégraux

Délai, nourrissons :

> « The time between the ingestion of the triggering substances and the onset of symptoms ranges
> from a minimum of 24 hours to a maximum of 5 days, and more days are needed for hospitalization.
> Furthermore, the two reported deaths underline the fact that favic attacks are more commonly
> severe in children due to the high ratio between ingested causative agents and body mass »

Délai, enfants (sevrés à 11 ans) :

> « The time elapsed between the intake of fava beans and the onset of symptoms varies from 1 to 3
> days. »

Délai, préadolescents et adolescents :

> « The time elapsed between fava bean ingestion and the onset of symptoms varies within a narrow
> range of 1 or 2 days; in two cases, the onset of the hemolytic attack followed after a week or
> more of consecutive fava bean ingestion. »

Délai, adultes (deux cas) :

> « The authors described two cases of women with attacks of favism due to fava bean intake, two
> days before and one day before symptom onset, respectively. »

Cas d'enfant, fève crue et cuite, rein :

> « The patient was oliguric until the third day of hospitalization when diuresis started to
> improve rapidly. After one week, he was discharged in good condition. The onset of symptoms
> occurred one day after a meal of broad beans, cooked and raw. »

Cas d'enfant soigné par les liquides seuls :

> « As the child refused a blood transfusion, the only treatment was intravenous hydration. During
> the first two days, there was a drop in hemoglobin, but on the third day (the fifth after the
> beans were ingested), the laboratory parameters started to improve. »

Fève crue, cuite, mûre, verte :

> « The type of fava beans eaten also plays a role in the likelihood of symptom onset: raw fava
> beans are more likely to induce the crisis, than cooked, frozen, or canned ones; in addition, the
> amount of glucosides is directly proportional to degree of ripeness, so the more unripe the fava
> beans, the less the amount of vicine and convicine, and thus the lower the risk of the crisis
> onset. »

Cas déclenchés par des fèves **cuites ou sèches** (tableaux et texte) : « Boiled dried fava beans »
(nourrisson de 4 mois) ; « Cooked homegrown fava beans » (enfant de 9 ans) ; « two meals of boiled
fava beans » (femme de 44 ans) ; « cooked dried fava beans five days before delivery » (nouveau-né).

Quantité et répétition (conclusions) :

> « the onset of symptoms is not always concomitant with the first ever fava bean ingestion, but it
> is also true that it usually appears when the amount of fava beans ingested is greater than the
> amount previously eaten, or when fava beans are eaten continuously for several days. »

Contamination croisée :

> « a case described the harmful exacerbation of symptoms due to the ingestion of pumpkin seeds
> contaminated with broad beans. »

## 2. NORD — *Glucose-6-Phosphate Dehydrogenase Deficiency*

- **Organisme :** National Organization for Rare Disorders (États-Unis), fiche de maladie
  (dernière mise à jour 29 juin 2017 ; préparée avec Ilan Youngster, MD, Assaf Harofeh Medical
  Center, Israël)
- **URL :** https://rarediseases.org/rare-diseases/glucose-6-phosphate-dehydrogenase-deficiency/
- **Consultée le :** 27 septembre 2026 (HTTP 200)
- **Copie :** `sources/98-nord-deficit-g6pd-texte.txt` (texte de l'article extrait de la page ; la
  page brute pesait 5 Mo de scripts)

### Extraits intégraux

> « The onset of symptoms is within 2-3 days after exposure to the trigger (even less with fava
> beans). A hemolytic anemia episode may be preceded by behavioral changes such as irritability or
> lethargy. Most episodes, even severe ones, are usually self-limiting and resolve on their own. »

> « Acute hemolytic anemia from eating fava beans (favism) can be rapid. Favism can occur at any
> age, but occurs more often and more severely in children. A child may have a slightly elevated
> temperature within 24-48 hours and can become irritable and unruly, or subdued and lethargic.
> Nausea, abdominal pain and diarrhea may develop. Vomiting only occurs rarely. Within 6 to 24
> hours, urine may become noticeably dark and can appear red, brown or even black. »

> « In severe cases, evidence may be seen of hypovolemic shock, in which blood and fluid loss is so
> severe that the heart cannot pump enough blood to the body, or, less likely, heart failure. »

> « Some adults may need short-term treatment with fluids to prevent hemodynamic shock (in which
> there is inadequate supply of blood to the organs) or, in severe cases where the rate of
> hemolysis is very rapid, even blood transfusions. Blood transfusions are more likely to be
> indicated in children than adults, and in children with favism can prove life-saving. »

> « Most individuals with G6PD deficiency do not develop symptoms after eating fava beans and
> individuals who do develop symptoms will not always do so. »

### Extraits intégraux

> « Pigment-induced kidney injury commonly arises from the deposition of either myoglobin or
> hemoglobin, which are the end results of rhabdomyolysis and hemolysis, respectively.
> Rhabdomyolysis or hemolysis leads to a sudden release of a high amount of potentially toxic heme
> pigment (myoglobin or hemoglobin) into the systemic circulation, which after glomerular
> filtration causes renal injury and renal failure »

> « The pathophysiology of pigment-induced AKI is multifaceted, involving vasoconstriction, direct
> injury to proximal tubular epithelial cells, and obstruction of the distal tubules due to pigment
> cast formation. Vasoconstriction reduces blood flow to the outer medulla, leading to ischemia.
> Heme pigment precipitates with the Tamm-Horsfall protein, a process facilitated by acidic urine »

> « There is no specific treatment for established pigment-induced AKI. The goal of treatment is
> the prevention of pigment-induced AKI in high-risk patients. Hydration with isotonic saline is
> the only established preventive measure for pigment-induced AKI. »

## Lecture pour le livre

- **Délai :** les signes viennent le plus souvent **le lendemain ou le surlendemain** du repas (1 à
  3 jours chez l'enfant, 1 ou 2 jours chez l'adolescent et l'adulte) ; l'urine foncée peut paraître
  dès 6 à 24 heures ; chez le nourrisson, jusqu'à 5 jours. Des fèves mangées plusieurs jours de
  suite peuvent déclencher la crise après une semaine.
- **Boire :** l'hémoglobine libérée passe par le rein et peut le boucher ; les liquides sont la
  seule protection du rein qu'on connaisse (à l'hôpital, eau salée en perfusion), et ils soutiennent
  la circulation. La plupart des crises, même fortes, passent d'elles-mêmes.
- **Fève crue ou cuite :** la **crue** déclenche plus souvent que la cuite, surgelée ou en conserve ;
  la cuite et la sèche déclenchent aussi (cas documentés). Plus la fève est mûre, plus elle porte
  de vicine et de convicine : la gourgane verte, moins mûre, en porte moins que la sèche — mais
  mangée crue, elle perd l'avantage de la cuisson.
