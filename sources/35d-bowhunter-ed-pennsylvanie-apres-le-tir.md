## Shot Reactions — extrait intégral

> « Observe the reaction of the animal to the shot:
> Did the animal kick out its back legs before running away?
> Did the animal appear to stagger as it walked away?
> Did the animal fall or struggle with any brush or other obstacle in its path?
> Did you see the animal collapse or hear what sounded like an animal falling?
> Any of these reactions could be an indication that the arrow hit the vitals of the animal and
> that it will quickly expire.
> Conversely, two indications that the shot might have missed are if the animal did not respond to
> the shot at all or simply walked or trotted a short distance away.
> An animal that hunches up and runs or trots away may be hit in the abdominal cavity. »

> « In every situation, always pay attention to the exact location of the animal when the shot was
> made, the direction the animal traveled, and the exact location of the animal at its last
> sighting after the shot. »

## When to Start Tracking — extrait intégral

> « Regardless of the animal's reaction to the shot, a hunter should not immediately attempt to
> recover the animal.
> If the shot appears to hit the vital organs, the hunter should wait 30 minutes to 1 hour before
> trailing the animal.
> When there appears to be a hit in a non-vital area, it is best to wait at least 6 hours before
> beginning to track the animal.
> An injured animal will often want to lie down to rest and recover. Taking time before pursuing
> the shot game will often lead to finding the animal where it decided to lie down. »

## Tracking — extrait intégral

> « If you find the arrow, look for blood on the arrow, and note the color. Bright red or frothy
> pink blood are indicators of a vital hit. Dark blood, bile, or other stomach contents may
> indicate a strike to the abdominal cavity. Minimal amounts of blood on the arrow—or none at
> all—may indicate a nick to the animal. »

> « Note that, in the fall, as the leaves have changed colors, it may be necessary to rub what
> appears to be blood on a leaf to confirm that the red color on a leaf is actually blood. »

> « If you are able to locate the blood trail, mark the location of blood as you find it. It is
> recommended that you use toilet paper or other biodegradable, high-visibility material. »

## Loss of Blood Trail — extrait intégral

> « There can be multiple reasons why, such as:
> Internal organs, or the arrow plugs or obscures the entry and/or exit wounds.
> The shot hit high in the body and blood is pooling in the chest and/or abdominal cavity.
> The animal may have bled out but is still traveling.
> If you lose the blood trail:
> Begin a systematic search by making ever widening circles.
> Use a grid search pattern where you make ever widening paths with right turns.
> Use maps or GPS to make sure you are checking the entire area.
> Look for the white underbelly of a downed deer. »

## Approaching Downed Game — extrait intégral

> « A downed deer or other large animal should be approached carefully from above and behind the
> head.
> If the animal appears to be dead, wait a short distance away for a few minutes. Watch for any
> rise and fall of the chest cavity.
> Notice whether the eyes are closed—the eyes of a dead animal are usually open.
> You can be certain that the animal is dead if the eye doesn't blink when touched with a stick. »

## Ce que le chapitre en tire

- **Les réactions au coup** et leur lecture (§5-B, tableau).
- **30 minutes à 1 heure** (organes vitaux) ; **6 heures au moins** (hors organes vitaux, cavité
  abdominale) : les deux planchers officiels du tableau des attentes.
- **La bête blessée cherche à se coucher** : c'est ce que l'attente exploite (§3).
- **Sang foncé, bile, contenu d'estomac = cavité abdominale** (tableau du sang).
- **Frotter la tache pour savoir si c'est du sang** : la version sans peroxyde (§9).
- **Trois raisons pour lesquelles le sang s'arrête** (§5-B, « quand le sang s'arrête »).
- **Grille à virages à droite, cercles qui s'élargissent** (§5-B, §9).
- **L'œil touché au bâton** (35d, étape 42 ; le ch. 36, étape 2, ne donne pas ce test).
