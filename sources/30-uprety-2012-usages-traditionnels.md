## Extraits intégraux de l'article

> « We report on a total of 546 medicinal plant taxa used by Aboriginal peoples of the Canadian
> boreal forest (Additional file 1). »

> « Among the most commonly used plants were: Abies balsamea (L.) Mill., Achillea millefolium L.,
> Acorus calamus L., Aralia nudicaulis L., Betula papyrifera Marsh., Cornus sericea L., Heracleum
> maximum Bartram, Juniperus communis L., Larix laricina (Du Roi) K. Koch, Menta arvensis L., Nuphar
> lutea (L.) Sm, Picea glauca (Moench) Voss, Picea mariana (Mill.) BSP, Populus balsamifera L.,
> Populus tremuloides Michx, Rhododendron groenlandicum (Oeder) K.A. Kron & W.S. Judd., Salix sp.,
> Sorbus americana Marsh, and Thuja occidentalis L. »

> « The majority of formulations were prepared as decoction or infusion (Additional file 1). »

> « Medicinal plant use should be carried out under the supervision of a knowledgeable person [81],
> usually an elder [102], as some plants might be poisonous (even lethal, e.g., Cicuta, Taxus,
> Veratrum), or could cause adverse reactions when taken in combination with other plants or with
> western medicine. »

## Extraits intégraux du fichier additionnel 1 — les espèces du tableau 1 du chapitre

(Les numéros entre crochets sont les références de l'article ; le nom de la nation est celui de la
source.)

**Abies balsamea — sapin baumier**
> « Gum: Applied over cuts, burns, impetigo, sores, scabs, abscesses, ulcers, grip, insect bites,
> boils, swellings, infections and cuts as a healer [44; Mi'kmaq: 60, 62; Ojibwa: 84, 87;
> Algonquians: 63; Algonquin: 68, 69; Atikamekw: 73; Malecite: 65; Innu: 72; Cree: 80; Dene: 17]. »
> « Branches and needles: […] Boiled and steam used to treat flu [Dene: 13], cold and sore throat
> [Cree: 81]. […] Tea from tops or combs to cure colic [Mi'kmaq: 43]. »

**Picea glauca — épinette blanche**
> « Twigs: Tea used as a general medicine [Algonquians: 63]. Steeped to make an antiscorbutic tea
> [Mi'kmaq: 60]. Tips of branches used to brew a tea to "heal the inside". »

**Picea mariana — épinette noire**
> « Twigs: Boiled to make a broth for cough [Algonquin: 63] […] Tips used to make a tea for "healing
> the inside" [Algonquin: 69]. Tea used against fever [Montagnais: 71]. »
> « Gum: Applied as a salve for skin infections, cuts, boils, rashes, burns, sores, and chapped or
> craked skin [Dene: 13, 100; Cree: 13, 79, 80; Algonquin: 69]. »

**Larix laricina — mélèze laricin**
> « Inner bark: Used as a poultice or boiled to make a wash for burns, boils [Dene: 13; Chipewyan:
> 92], frostbite [Cree: 13], hemorrhoids, infected wounds [Cree, Metis: 13], or cuts [Cree: 13]. […]
> Boiled and tea used to wash a wound. Tea drunk for pain relief, stomach problems, mouth infections,
> sore throat, fever, cold, flu and bleeding. »

**Betula papyrifera — bouleau à papier**
> « Bark: […] Used as a cast for a broken arm or leg, a sprained ankle, or swollen limbs [Cree: 13;
> Dene: 101]. Steamed and peeled to produce thin sheets suitable for bandages [Metis: 13; Cree: 95]. »
> « Sap: Used as a cough medicine, warmed and taken as a tea for general health [Dene: 17]. Tea drunk
> for cough and breathing problems [Dene: 101]. »

**Alnus incana ssp. rugosa — aulne rugueux**
> « Bark: […] Shavings used as a poultice for sores or applied warm to swollen areas [Ojibwa: 85].
> Decoction of shavings used as wash for skin cancer […] Inner bark: […] Decoction to wash sore eyes
> [Cree: 95]. Boiled to yield a red liquid used in skin troubles [Abenaki: 67; Montagnais: 71]. »

**Salix sp. — saule**
> « Bark: […] Boiled until it turns to a thick paste, then placed in a towel and used locally as a
> compress for swells, back pain and other types of body pain (e.g. sprains). Tea used in cough
> [Montagnais: 71]. Bark from young shoots wrapped around a cut like a bandage [Dene: 99]. […] Inner
> bark: […] Inner bark from young shoots made into a poultice and used as a pain-killer on wounds
> [Dene: 99]. »

**Achillea millefolium — achillée millefeuille**
> « Whole plant: Used to stop bleeding [Dene: 13; Cree: 93]. […] Leaves: […] Chewed and applied to
> burns, cuts or bee stings [Algonquin: 68; Cree: 13]. Infusion used as a wash for pimples, mosquito
> bites or other skin eruptions [Chippewa: 47; Dene: 13; Chipewyan: 92]. Poultice used in skin
> disorders, cuts, burns and bee sti[ngs] »

**Plantago major — plantain majeur** (marqué \* : espèce introduite)
> « Leaves: Chewed into a paste applied to burns and scalds. […] Soaked in warm water and applied to
> bruises, sprains or sores as a poultice, also used to sooth burns, scalds, bee stings, and snake
> bites [Algonquin: 69; Ojibwa: 87]. Applied fresh on wounds, bruises, burns and haematoma
> [Atikamekw: 73]. »

**Rhododendron groenlandicum — thé du Labrador**
> « Medicinal tea [Cree: 94; Innu: 72]. […] Leaves: Decoction or infusion taken as a diuretic [Cree:
> 76; Algonquians: 63]. Steeped to make a tonic tea [Algonquians: 63]. »

**Aralia nudicaulis — aralie à tige nue**
> « Roots: […] Boiled in a box with hot stones, and decoction taken internally for stomachache, or
> merely as a beverage [Algonquians: 63]. […] Rhizomes: Chewed or made into a tea to treat heart
> pain, chronic chest pain, upset stomach, liver problems [Dene: 13; Chipewyan: 92] and sore throat
> [Cree: 13]. »

**Coptis trifolia — savoyane**
> « Steeped, used to saturate a rag to be placed in sore mouth [Mi'kmaq: 61; Malecite: 65]. […] Stem:
> Tea used as a wash for sore eyes, lips and interior of mouth [Montaganais: 70]. Chewed to allay
> canker or sores on the gums or in the mouth. […] Roots: […] Decoction used to soothe teething pain
> and heal gums, and as a mouth wash to treat sore mouth [Ojibwa: 87]. Decoction used for throat
> pain, cough and mouth infection [Montagnais: 71]. »

**Comptonia peregrina — comptonie voyageuse**
> « Leaves: Steeped and rubbed on the skin to cure poison ivy rash [Mi'kmaq: 60; Algonquians: 63].
> […] Steeped and tea taken as a general tonic. Poultice used for rheumatism and external sores
> [Mi'kmaq: 62]. »

**Myrica gale — myrique baumier**
> « Used for sore eyes. Boiled and tea applied to skin rash and sores, or drunk to cure mouth
> infections and sore throat [Dene: 101]. Stem: Boiled with leaves and fruits and taken to treat
> tuberculosis [Dene: 98]. »

**Cornus canadensis — quatre-temps**
> « Medicinal plant [Innu: 72]. Used to heal sores and cancers [Mi'kmaq: 61]. Steeped and used for

> leaves and fruits: Steeped and given against fits [Malecite: 65]. »

## Ce que la source ne donne pas

- Aucune dose, aucune durée, aucune concentration : ce sont des usages rapportés.
- Aucune notion de « contenant stérile » pour l'écorce de bouleau (elle donne l'attelle et la
  bande à pansement).
- Aucun usage répulsif de la myrique baumier.
- La vitamine C des aiguilles n'y est pas chiffrée (voir `25-durzan-annedda-coniferes.md`).
