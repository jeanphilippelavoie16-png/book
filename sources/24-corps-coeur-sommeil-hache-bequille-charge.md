## 1. Auger et coll. 2017, *CMAJ* 189(6):E235 — neige et infarctus au Québec

- **URL :** https://www.cmaj.ca/content/189/6/E235
- **Copie :** `sources/24-corps-cmaj-2017-auger-neige-infarctus.html` (texte intégral)

> « We used a case-crossover study design to investigate the association between snowfall and
> hospital admission or death due to MI in the province of Quebec, Canada, between November and
> April during 1981-2014. »
> « Compared with 0 cm, 20 cm of snowfall was associated with an OR of 1.16 for hospital admission
> (95% CI 1.11-1.21) and 1.34 for death (95% CI 1.26-1.42) due to MI the following day among men.
> Corresponding ORs among women were 1.01 (95% CI 0.95-1.07) and 1.04 (95% CI 0.96-1.13). »
> « We suspect that shovelling was the main mechanism linking snowfall with MI. Men are
> potentially more likely than women to shovel, particularly after heavy snowfalls. Snow
> shovelling is a demanding cardiovascular exercise requiring more than 75% of the maximum heart
> rate, particularly with heavy loads. It is a unique activity with both static and dynamic
> components, involving the Valsalva manoeuvre and increased systolic blood pressure. Intense arm
> effort, repetitive motion, static upright posture, peripheral vasoconstriction and greater
> myocardial afterload all increase the risk of coronary plaque rupture and thrombus formation.
> Risks may be exacerbated by exposure to cold temperatures, which increases vasoconstriction and
> blood pressure [...] breathing cold air has been shown to reduce coronary blood flow. »
> « Some researchers have even reported that cardiac demands are elevated with automated snow
> removal. »

*Lecture :* un rapport de cotes de 1,34 = environ un tiers de risque de mort par infarctus en
plus, le lendemain d'une bordée de 20 cm, chez les hommes.

## 3. Weisgerber 1999, *An Ax to Grind: A Practical Ax Manual* — USDA Forest Service (domaine public)

- **Copie :** `sources/24-corps-usfs-1999-an-ax-to-grind.pdf` (68 p.) ; texte extrait :
  `sources/24-corps-usfs-1999-an-ax-to-grind.txt` (numéros de ligne ci-dessous)
- **Captée depuis :** https://www.pcta.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/an_ax_to_grind.pdf (copie
  de la publication 9923-2823-MTDC)

> (l. 1780) « Placement, control, proper stance, and technique are far more important than power. »
> (l. 1900-1912) « Chopping is an art. Start out loose and relaxed. [...] Each blow lands exactly
> where it is intended, with the proper force, and at the proper angle. There is no shock to the
> hands or shoulders. »
> (l. 1977) « Swing with a natural rhythmic and unforced motion. »
> (l. 2370-2378) « Take advantage of existing cracks or checks in the wood to help direct your
> first blow, because the first split is generally the most difficult. Swing straight down toward
> the top of the block. Use your body weight, with your knees snapping into position just as you
> hit the block of wood. Give the ax handle a slight twist just as the bit hits the block. This
> throws the block of wood apart and prevents the ax from sticking. »
> (l. 2390-2394) « Inevitably, your ax will become stuck in the block you are trying to split. The
> best way to remove it without damaging the ax is to rap the end of the handle sharply downward
> with the palm of your hand without holding the handle. »
> (l. 1805-1811) « Always wear good, stout leather boots [...] Gloves are a good idea for splitting
> firewood, where you are handling many pieces of splintered wood. »
> (l. 1833-1835, légende de la figure 74) « A dull ax or one that is too blunt is more likely to
> glance off dangerously than it is to cut »
> (l. 2294-2299, tronçonnage debout à côté de la bille) « Take a wide stance and chop between your
> feet [...] Again, keeping the handle parallel with the ground will prevent foot injuries. »
> (l. 2556-2558, équarrissage) « Standing on the ground, spread your legs apart so they are out of
> harm's way, and swing straight down »

*Lecture (ajout du 27 septembre 2026) :* « knees snapping into position » ne dit pas de **plier**
les genoux : les genoux « se placent d'un coup ». Le ch. 24 (étape 11) le traduit ainsi. Le
manche « parallèle au sol » est dit pour le tronçonnage ; le ch. 24 le transpose au fendage sur
billot (la bûche plus haute met le manche à l'horizontale à l'impact). Transposition notée au
suivi, section 3.

## 4. Béquille : la hauteur, et ce que l'aisselle ne doit pas porter (PMC8724683)

- ***Vasc Specialist Int* 2021**, « Bilateral Brachial Artery Aneurysms with Distal Embolisms in a
  Patient with Prolonged Crutch Compression » (CC BY-NC)
- **Copie :** `sources/24-corps-pmc8724683-bequilles-ajustement.xml`

> « Axillobrachial arterial complications are commonly associated with pressure from the axillary
> bar. Improper crutch fitting is also a contributing factor [...] Axillary crutches that are too
> long will likely accentuate pressure on the axillary structures. The axillary bar should be set
> at 77% of the patient's height, or at the level of the patient's height minus 40 cm. Joyce and
> Kirby recommended that there be 5 cm between the anterior axillary fold and the axillary bar.
> Potter and Wallace suggested that when standing with the feet together and crutch tips 8 to 10
> cm lateral to the feet, there should be 2 to 3 finger breadths of space between the patient's
> axilla and the axillary bar. To reduce the risk of injury, patients should be instructed to bear
> weight through the hands with their elbows extended. »

### De quel côté se tient l'appui seul — MedlinePlus, « Using a cane » (NLM, domaine public)

- **URL :** https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000343.htm — « Review Date 4/1/2025 »
- **Copie :** `sources/24-corps-medlineplus-canne.html` (captée le 27 septembre 2026)

> « The most common question about using a cane is, "Which hand should I hold it in?" The answer
> is the hand opposite the leg that you had surgery on, or that is the weakest. »
> « At the same time that you step forward with your weaker leg, swing the cane the same distance
> in front of you. [...] Take some of the pressure off your weaker leg by placing pressure on the
> cane. Step past the cane with your strong leg. [...] Turn by pivoting on your strong leg, not
> the weaker leg. »

*Lecture :* la page parle de la canne. Le ch. 24 l'applique à la béquille **seule** (section 9) et
au bâton (étape 47) : un seul appui, tenu du côté sain. Transposition notée au suivi.

## 5. La douleur comme jauge de la charge (modèle de surveillance de la douleur)

### *Pilot Feasibility Stud* 2026 (PMC13520403)

- **Copie :** `sources/24-corps-pmc13520403-douleur-guide-charge.xml`

> « As tolerance to loading increased, participants progressively increased their Achilles-tendon
> loading activities without exceeding pain ≥ 5/10 and pain the following morning not exceeding
> the previous day »
> « participants were instructed to incorporate up to 3 days of reduced activity intensity and
> Achilles-tendon loading following a day of heavy loading to promote adequate time for tissue
> remodeling »

### *PLoS One* 2026, protocole (PMC12952620)

- **Copie :** `sources/24-corps-pmc12952620-douleur-guide-charge-course.xml`

> « using the pain monitoring model as a guide, i.e., avoid running distances/intensities that
> cause their pain to increase to more than 4/10 during or in the day after the run »

*Lecture :* selon les équipes, le seuil est 4 ou 5 sur 10. Le livre garde **5 sur 10** pendant
l'effort et **« pas pire le lendemain matin que la veille »**, et le dit comme une jauge, pas comme
une mesure.
