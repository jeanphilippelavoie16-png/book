## 1. « An Ax to Grind: A Practical Ax Manual » — Bernie Weisgerber, juillet 1999, 9923-2823P-MTDC

- **URL :** https://www.fs.usda.gov/t-d/pubs/htmlpubs/htm99232823/page03.htm (section
  « Sharpening » du chapitre « Getting the Hang of It »)
- **Copies enregistrées :** `sources/82-usda-fs-an-ax-to-grind-affutage.html` (page entière) ;
  `sources/82-usda-fs-an-ax-to-grind-gabarit-fig68.jpg` (figure 68, gabarit d'affûtage « reproduced
  to exact size »)

### Extraits intégraux (anglais)

> « Never use an electric high–speed dry bench grinder to sharpen your ax. That type of a grinder
> will almost certainly draw the temper from the ax and ruin it. »

> « At the minimum, you've drawn the temper for at least 3/16 of an inch back from the edge. You
> can always tell when the temper is drawn because the color of the steel at the edge turns
> blue. »

> « Figure 63 –Tools for sharpening (clockwise from top left) include leather gloves; ax–bit width
> gauges; 8–inch, 10–inch, and 12–inch mill bastard files; Carborundum scythe stone; Carbor–undum
> ax stone; file card; and grooved Carborundum sharpening stone. Natural Arkansas sharpening
> stones also work. »

> « The single–cut file sharpens only on the push stroke. Lift it away from the ax head on the
> return stroke. »

> « When sharpening, always try for a fan–shaped effect on the cheek of the ax (Figure 66). File
> back for a distance of approximately 2 to 3 inches from the cutting edge right at the middle
> point of the ax. Work your way from the cheek down to the actual edge, keeping a rounded
> profile. »

> « It is time to check the shape of the edge with a sharpening gauge (Figure 67). This gauge can
> be handmade from a piece of cardboard, a small piece of brass, or even a piece of wood. See the
> illustration for the proper shape of your edge. The angle is about 25 degrees, but is slightly
> convex. The gauge is exactly to scale and can serve as a template (Figure 68). »

> « Continue to file equally on both sides of the ax until the sharpening gauge pattern fits
> exactly over the edge. »

> « Manmade stones are usually oil stones of Carborundum and come with a coarse and a fine side. »

> « The Washita is my favorite for fast cutting and the Hard Arkansas is my favorite for
> finishing. »

### Lecture de l'angle

Le gabarit de la figure 68 est une encoche en V dans laquelle **tout le tranchant** s'engage, les
deux joues ensemble ; il « fits exactly over the edge ». Les 25° sont donc l'**angle total** du
tranchant (les deux faces), pas l'angle d'une face : environ 12 à 13° par face. La figure montre
trois profils, « too thin », « correct », « too thick », et le profil correct est bombé.

## 2. « Crosscut Saw Manual » — Warren Miller, juin 1977, révisé décembre 2003, 7771-2508-MTDC

- **URL :** https://www.fs.usda.gov/t-d/pubs/pdfpubs/pdf77712508/pdf77712508dpi72.pdf
- **Copie enregistrée :** `sources/82-usda-fs-crosscut-saw-manual.pdf` (PDF entier)

### Extraits intégraux (anglais)

Outils (section « Filing the Saw ») :

> « Jointing --Jointer (short or long). --7- or 8-inch special crosscut file (mill bastard blunt
> file). --Saw vise. »

> « Raker fitting --7- or 8-inch slim-taper (triangular) file. --Pin gauge, raker gauge, or 8- to
> 16-ounce tinner's riveting hammer for swaging. --6-inch, slim-taper file with "safe" corners
> (corners ground smooth). --6-inch mill-bastard file. --Saw vise. »

> « Pointing up cutter teeth --7- or 8-inch special crosscut file (mill bastard blunt file) for
> lance-tooth saws. --6- or 8-inch Great American crosscut file for championtooth saws. »

Profondeur des dents de nettoyage (« rakers ») :

> « The raker teeth remove shavings that the cutter teeth have severed from the wood. For a saw to
> operate efficiently, the raker must remove all the wood severed by the cutter teeth, but no
> more. »

> « Optimum depth is obtained by experiment, but figures vary from 0.008 inch for hard or dry wood
> to 0.030 inch for soft, springy wood; 0.012 inch is a good average figure to begin with. »

> « Because the cutting teeth exert pressure on the wood as they cut, a certain thickness of wood
> is compressed and springs back after the teeth pass over. [...] The amount of springback varies
> with wood type, moisture content, saw weight, and cutter tooth shape. Optimum raker depth
> depends on all these factors. »

> « One way of checking the setting of a raker gauge is to file a raker using the gauge. Place a
> straightedge between the two cutter teeth on each side of the filed raker and measure the
> relative height with a feeler gauge placed between the raker and straightedge. »

> « A broken raker tip allows the other tip on the raker to bite too deeply on the cutting stroke,
> causing the saw to catch just as it does with a long raker. File the unbroken tip shorter, about
> 0.005 inch initially. If it still catches, continue filing. »

Voie :

> « A set of 0.010 inch is a good preliminary figure. » — « Felling saw, 0.010 inch Bucking saw,
> 0.015 inch »

Dents de coupe :

> « The consensus is that there should be less bevel on a cutter tooth for hardwood than for
> softwood. »

## Ce que le livre en tire (conversions du rédacteur : 1 po = 25,4 mm)

| Donnée | Valeur | Métrique |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Angle du tranchant de hache | environ 25° au total, légèrement bombé | ≈ 12 à 13° par face |
| Limes pour la hache | plates bâtardes de 8, 10 et 12 po | 20, 25 et 30 cm |
| Limes pour le godendard | tiers-point effilé de 7 ou 8 po ; plate bâtarde de 6 po ; lime spéciale « crosscut » de 7 ou 8 po | 18-20 cm ; 15 cm ; 18-20 cm |
| Pierres | combinée grossière et fine ; Washita pour dégrossir, Hard Arkansas pour finir | aucun numéro de grain |
| Profondeur des dents de nettoyage sous les dents de coupe | 0,008 po (bois dur ou sec) à 0,030 po (bois tendre et élastique) ; départ à 0,012 po | 0,2 à 0,76 mm ; départ à 0,3 mm |
| Voie | 0,010 po (abattage), 0,015 po (tronçonnage) | 0,25 mm ; 0,38 mm |

**Ce que ces manuels ne donnent pas :** aucun angle pour les couteaux, les ciseaux à bois, les
fers de rabot ou les burins ; aucun numéro de grain.
