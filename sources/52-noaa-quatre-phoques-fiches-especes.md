# NOAA Fisheries — Fiches d'espèce des quatre phoques du Saint-Laurent

- **Organisme :** National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, NOAA Fisheries (organisme
  fédéral américain) — fiches « Species Directory »
- **Consultées le :** 24 septembre 2026
- **Pages enregistrées :** `52-noaa-harbor-seal.html`, `52-noaa-gray-seal.html`,
  `52-noaa-harp-seal.html`, `52-noaa-hooded-seal.html`
- **Sert au chapitre :** 52, §5-A (noms latins, poids, risques sanitaires) et §5-B (reconnaître
  la bête morte)
- **Pourquoi une source américaine :** la page « Identifier les espèces » du MPO charge son
  tableau par script et ne livre que les noms communs (constaté de nouveau le 24 septembre 2026,
  page modifiée le 2018-03-23). Les quatre espèces vivent des deux côtés de la frontière
  (Atlantique Nord-Ouest).

## 1. Phoque commun — https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/harbor-seal

> « Harbor Seal | Phoca vitulina »
> « Weight 24 pounds (birth) to 180 to 285 pounds (adult) Length About 5 to 6 feet »
> « Harbor seals weigh up to 285 pounds and measure up to 6 feet in length. Males are slightly
> larger than females, and seals in Alaska and the Pacific Ocean are generally larger than those
> found in the Atlantic Ocean. »
> « Harbor seals have short, dog-like snouts. The color of each seal's fur varies but there are
> two basic patterns: light tan, silver, or blue-gray with dark speckling or spots, and a dark
> background with light rings. »
> « Harbor seals accumulate contaminants, which threaten their immune and reproductive systems,
> in their blubber, blood, and organs (for example, liver or brain). »

## 2. Phoque gris — https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/gray-seal

> « Gray Seal | Halichoerus grypus »
> « Weight 550 to 880 pounds Length 7.5 to 10 feet »
> « They are sometimes called "horseheads" (adult males in particular have large, horse-like
> heads) because of their large, curved noses. »
> « Adult female gray seals are about 7.5 feet long and weigh about 550 pounds, while adult males
> can reach 10 feet long and weigh about 880 pounds. Females have silver-gray or brown fur which
> may or may not have scattered dark spots, while males have dark gray or brown fur which may or
> may not have silver-gray spots. Males also have longer noses than females. The male nose is so
> distinctive that the gray seal's scientific name, Halichoerus grypus, means "hooked-nosed pig
> of the sea." »
> « These contaminants accumulate in gray seals' blubber stores, threatening their immune and
> reproductive systems. »

## 3. Phoque du Groenland — https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/harp-seal

> « Harp Seal | Pagophilus groenlandicus »
> « Harp seals are about 5 to 6 feet long, weigh about 260 to 300 pounds, and have a robust body
> with a small, flat head. »
> « Adult harp seals have light gray fur with a black mask on their face and a curved black patch
> on their back. »

## 4. Phoque à capuchon — https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/hooded-seal

> « Hooded Seal | Cystophora cristata »
> « Male hooded seals are about 8.5 feet long and weigh about 423 to 776 pounds, while females
> are about 6.5 feet long and weigh about 320 to 660 pounds. Both male and female adults have
> silver-gray fur with darker patches of different sizes and shapes across their bodies. »

## Ce que ces pages NE disent PAS

- **Aucun rendement** : ni poids de carcasse, ni viande désossée, ni lard, ni huile.
- **Rien sur les narines** (en V chez le phoque commun, parallèles chez le phoque gris) : ce
  caractère du tableau 5-B reste sans source.
- Aucune limite de consommation liée aux contaminants : seulement le fait qu'ils s'accumulent
  dans le lard, le sang et les organes (foie, cerveau).
- Rien sur la flottabilité d'une bête tuée à l'eau.
