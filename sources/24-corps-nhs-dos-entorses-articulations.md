## 1. Back pain — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/

- **Copie :** `sources/24-corps-nhs-back-pain.html`
- **Revue affichée :** 05 March 2026

> « Back pain, particularly lower back pain, is very common. It usually improves within a few
> weeks but can sometimes last longer or keep coming back. »

> « it does not improve after resting or is worse at night »
> « it's worse when sneezing, coughing or pooing »
> « it's coming from the top of your back (between your shoulders), rather than your lower back »
> « you feel hot, cold, shivery or generally unwell »

> « pain, tingling, weakness or numbness in both legs
> a loss of feeling around your genitals or anus
> changes in your bladder or bowels, such as difficulty peeing, or peeing or pooing yourself
> [...] chest pain
> it started after a serious accident, such as a car accident »

> « stay active and try to continue with your daily activities
> take anti-inflammatory medicine like ibuprofen (these may not be suitable for everyone) –
> paracetamol on its own is not recommended for back pain but it may be used with another
> painkiller [...]
> use an ice pack (or bag of frozen peas) wrapped in a tea towel to reduce pain and swelling
> use a heat pack (or hot water bottle) wrapped in a tea towel to relieve joint stiffness or
> muscle spasms »

> « do not stay in bed for long periods of time »

## 2. Sciatica — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sciatica/

- **Copie :** `sources/24-corps-nhs-sciatica.html`

> « It usually gets better in a few weeks to a few months but can last longer. »
> « The symptoms of sciatica usually affect your bottom and the back of one leg, often including
> your foot and toes. »
> « You probably do not have sciatica if you only have back pain. »
> « do not sit or lie down for long periods – even if moving hurts, it's not harmful and can help
> you get better faster »
> « hold heat packs to the painful areas »
> « have sciatica on both sides
> have weakness or numbness in both legs that's severe or getting worse
> have numbness around or under your genitals, or around your bottom (anus)
> find it hard to start peeing, cannot pee or cannot control when you pee »

## 3. Sprains and strains — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sprains-and-strains/

- **Copie :** `sources/24-corps-nhs-sprains-and-strains.html`
- **Revue affichée :** 23 April 2024

> « Torn or twisted ligament (tissue that connects the joints) » (sprain) /
> « Overstretched or torn muscle (also known as a pulled muscle) » (strain)
> « Most common in wrists, ankles, thumbs, knees » / « Most common in feet, legs, back »

> « For the first 2 to 3 days after a sprain or strain, follow the 5 steps known as PRICE [...]
> Ice – apply an ice pack (or a bag of frozen vegetables wrapped in a tea towel) to the injury
> for up to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours.
> Compression – wrap a bandage around the injury to support it during the day.
> Elevate – keep it raised on a pillow as much as possible. »

> « To help prevent swelling, try to avoid heat (such as hot baths and heat packs), alcohol and
> massages for the first couple of days.
> When you can move the injured area without pain stopping you, try to keep moving it so the
> joint or muscle does not become stiff. »

> « After 2 weeks, most sprains and strains will feel better.
> Avoid strenuous exercise such as running for up to 8 weeks, as there's a risk of further damage.
> Severe sprains and strains can take months to get back to normal. »

> « Not warming up before exercising, tired muscles and playing sport are common causes. »

> « you heard a crack when you had your injury
> the injured body part has changed shape or is pointing at an odd angle
> the injured body part is numb, tingling or has pins and needles
> the skin around the injury has changed colour, such as looking blue or grey, or is cold to touch
> You may have broken a bone »

## 4. Knee pain — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/knee-pain/

- **Copie :** `sources/24-corps-nhs-knee-pain.html`

> « your knee locks, gives way or painfully clicks – painless clicking is normal
> you have a very high temperature (or feel hot, cold or shivery) and have redness or heat around
> your knee – this can be a sign of infection »
> « Unstable, gives way when you try to stand, unable to straighten, may hear a popping sound
> during injury » → « Torn ligament, tendon or meniscus, cartilage damage »
> « Warm and red, kneeling or bending makes pain and swelling worse » → « Bursitis »

## 5. Bursitis — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/bursitis/

- **Copie :** `sources/24-corps-nhs-bursitis.html`

> « use ice – gently hold an ice pack [...] on the area for around 10 minutes at a time and repeat
> every few hours during the day »
> « use padding when putting a lot of pressure on joints (for example, when kneeling)
> take regular breaks if you do things that put pressure on a joint, like kneeling »
> « clean any cuts on the joints to prevent infections »

## 6. Shoulder pain — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/shoulder-pain/

- **Copie :** `sources/24-corps-nhs-shoulder-pain.html`

> « You usually need to do these things for 2 weeks before shoulder pain starts to ease.
> It can take 6 months or longer to recover from shoulder pain. »
> « stay active and gently move your shoulder
> try shoulder exercises for 6 to 8 weeks to stop pain returning »
> « hot water bottle wrapped in a tea towel for up to 20 minutes, 2 to 3 times a day »
> « do not completely stop using your shoulder – this can stop it getting better »

## 7. Tennis elbow — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/tennis-elbow/

- **Copie :** `sources/24-corps-nhs-tennis-elbow.html`
- **Revue affichée :** 31 May 2024

> « It usually goes away with rest but can sometimes last over a year. »
> « lifting or bending your arm / gripping objects / moving your wrist »
> « try wearing a forearm strap or a wrist or elbow brace »
> « You can get tennis elbow if you do activities that involve gripping something and repeatedly

## 8. Carpal tunnel syndrome — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/carpal-tunnel-syndrome/

- **Copie :** `sources/24-corps-nhs-carpal-tunnel-syndrome.html`

> « tingling, numbness and pain in your hand and fingers »
> « a weak thumb or difficulty gripping
> These symptoms often start slowly and come and go. They're usually worse at night. »
> « A wrist splint is something you wear on your hand to keep your wrist straight. [...] You wear it
> at night while you sleep. You may need to wear a splint for up to 6 weeks before it starts to
> feel better. »
> « Stop or cut down on anything that causes you to frequently bend your wrist or grip hard, such
> as using vibrating tools »

## Ce qui n'est pas repris

Les délais « voir un médecin après 2 semaines », les numéros 111 et 999, « ne pas conduire
soi-même aux urgences », la physiothérapie : tout suppose quelqu'un qui répond.
