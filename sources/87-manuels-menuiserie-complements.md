### Profondeur de la mortaise

> « The tenon in its simplest form is made by dividing the end of a piece of wood into three
> parts and cutting out rectangular pieces on both sides of the part left in the middle. The
> mortise is the rectangular hole cut to receive the tenon and is made slightly deeper than the
> tenon is long. »

### Jeu dans la largeur des tenons (tablette large à tenons clavetés)

> « Care must be taken in cutting the mortises to keep their sides clean and sharp and to size.
> In making the mortises for the keyed tenons, the length of mortise must be slightly in excess
> of the width of the tenon--about 1/8 in. of play to each side of each tenon. With a shelf of
> the width specified for this table, if such allowance is not made so that the tenons may move
> sideways, the shrinkage would split the shelf. »

### Équerrage aux diagonales

> « When clamping up the second set of rails make sure the frame of the chair is square. The best
> way to test for squareness is to measure the diagonals with a stick. Spring the frame until
> they measure alike, using a brace to hold the frame in position until the glue can harden. »

## C. Charles G. Wheeler, *Wood-working for Beginners* (1899) — passage complet sur la cheville

- **URL :** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/43604/pg43604.txt (déjà cité à la fiche principale,
  section D ; la phrase du milieu, qui n'y figurait pas, est ajoutée ici)

> « The pin should be slightly pointed before driving, on the same principle that the end of the
> tenon is bevelled. It is not necessary to round the pin. An eight-sided one is just as good.
> Do not use too large pins. In ship-building, bridge-building, and old-fashioned house-framing
> pins and treenails from 1" to 1-3/4" or more in diameter, are used. Dowels of various sizes will
> usually answer for such framing as you may have to do (though a rift-pin is stronger). For such
> work as pinning a joint in a chair, you will not need anything larger than a 1/4" hardwood
> pin. »

## Ce que ces passages établissent pour le chapitre 87

| Point du chapitre | Ce que disent les manuels |
| --- | --- |
| Mortaise borgne plus profonde que le tenon (§5.5 étape 18 ; §6) | **Confirmé** : « slightly deeper than the tenon is long ». **Aucun chiffre** |
| Jeu dans la largeur du tenon (§5.5 étape 22 ; §6) | **Environ 1/8 po (3 mm) de chaque côté de chaque tenon**, pour une **tablette large** à tenons clavetés ; sans ce jeu, « the shrinkage would split the shelf ». Aucune valeur pour un tenon de traverse étroite |
| Diagonales (§5.5 étape 24 ; §6) | Mesurées **au bâton**, « until they measure alike ». **Aucune tolérance chiffrée** |
| Diamètre de cheville (§5.6 étape 28) | Valeurs absolues : **1/4 po (6 mm)** de bois dur pour une chaise ; **1 à 1 3/4 po (25 à 45 mm) ou plus** en charpente ancienne, en construction navale et de ponts. **Aucune proportion** à l'épaisseur du tenon |
| Pointe de la cheville (§5.6 étape 27) | « Slightly pointed », par le même principe que le chanfrein du bout du tenon ; octogonale suffit. **Aucune longueur** |
