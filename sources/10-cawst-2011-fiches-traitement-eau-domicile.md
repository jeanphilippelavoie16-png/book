## Extraits intégraux

### Décanter (fiche « Settling »)

> « Water quality can sometimes be improved by allowing it to stand undisturbed long enough for larger suspended
> particles to settle out by gravity, including those that cause turbidity (e.g. sand and silt) and certain pathogens
> (e.g. protozoa and helminths). Fine clay particles and other pathogens like bacteria and viruses are generally too
> small to settle by gravity. »

> « Storing water for at least one day will also promote the natural die-off of some bacteria. »

> « Water can be settled for a few hours and up to days depending on its quality. The settled water is then carefully
> removed by decanting, ladling or other gentle methods that do not disturb the sedimented particles. »

### Javel : temps de contact selon la température (fiche « Chlorination »)

> « Very turbid water should be sedimented or filtered prior to chlorination »

> « Use a 30-minute minimum contact time »

> « The contact time should be increased to 1 hour when the temperature is between 10˚ and 18˚C. It should be
> increased to two or more hours when the temperature falls below 10˚C. »

> « Toxoplasma oocysts and Cryptosporidium parvum oocysts are highly resistant to chlorine disinfection (CDC, 2007).
> Chlorine alone should not be expected to inactivate these pathogens. »

### Filtres à membrane (fiche « Membrane Filters »)

Tableau : très efficace contre les virus : ultrafiltration (UF), nanofiltration, osmose inverse ; la microfiltration
(MF, 0,1 à 10 µm) ne figure que pour les protozoaires. Pores : MF 0,1 à 10 µm ; UF 0,01 à 0,1 µm.

> « Ultrafiltration is the most common membrane filtration in household drinking water treatment. »

### Ultraviolet (fiche « Ultraviolet (UV) Disinfection »)

> « It is common for a UV treatment system to incorporate a pre-filter to remove turbidity since it can interfere with
> UV light penetration through the water. »

> « Inlet Water Criteria : Turbidity < 5 NTU » ; « Very turbid water should be sedimented or filtered prior to UV
> treatment » ; « The UV bulbs should be replaced every 12 months. »

### Désinfection solaire (fiche « Solar Disinfection (SODIS) »)

> « The source water should first be sedimented and/or filtered if turbidity levels are greater than 30 NTU »

> « Fill the plastic bottle ¾ full of low turbidity water. Shake the bottle for about 20 seconds and then fill the
> bottle completely. Place the bottles horizontally on a roof or rack in the sun for the following times: 6 hours if
> the sky is cloudless or up to 50% cloudy ; 2 consecutive days if the sky is more than 50% cloudy ; Do not use SODIS
> during days of continuous rainfall. »

> « The bottles must NOT be placed so that they are in shade for part of the day. The most favourable geographical
> regions for SODIS are located between latitudes 15 N and 35 N (as well as 15 S and 35 S). »

> « The treatment efficiency can be improved if the plastic bottles are placed on sunlight reflecting surfaces, such
> as corrugated aluminum or zinc roofs. This can increase the water temperature by about 5°C. »

> « SODIS alone should not be expected to inactivate all Cryptosporidum parvum oocysts. »

## Ce que le livre en tire

- Décanter : de quelques heures à quelques jours selon l'eau ; un jour de repos fait aussi mourir une partie des
  bactéries ; l'argile la plus fine ne tombe pas (remplace « 12 à 24 h », non retrouvé).
- Javel en eau froide : 1 h entre 10 et 18 °C, 2 h ou plus sous 10 °C (donne la durée qui manquait).
- Virus : seuls les filtres à membrane les plus fins (ultrafiltration et plus fin) les retiennent.
- UV : eau à moins de 5 UNT, préfiltre, lampe changée chaque année.
- Soleil : bouteille de plastique, secouée, couchée sur une surface qui réfléchit (tôle), jamais à l'ombre ; la
  méthode est faite pour les latitudes de 15 à 35 degrés : beaucoup plus au sud que la Côte-Nord.
