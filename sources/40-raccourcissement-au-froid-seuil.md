## 1. Honikel KO, Roncalés P, Hamm R. « The influence of temperature on shortening and rigor onset in beef muscle ». *Meat Science* 1983 ; 8(3) : 221-241. doi:10.1016/0309-1740(83)90046-3 ; PMID 22055561

Extraits intégraux du résumé :

> « At sufficient ATP concentration and temperatures below about 15°C, pre-rigor beef muscles
> (neck muscles) contract; this phenomenon is known as cold shortening. There is also a
> contracture at higher temperatures occurring just before rigor onset which is called rigor
> shortening. While rigor shortening starts in neck muscles at pH around 6·3-6·0 and at about
> 2 μMol ATP/g muscle, cold shortening can begin at pH around 7·0 and the full ATP concentration
> (4 μMol ATP/g) in the muscle. Shortening can take place as long as there is no irreversible
> formation of the actomyosin complex in the muscle, i.e. before rigor onset occurs […] »

> « At 38°C the rigor onset occurs at pH 6·25 and about 2 μMol ATP/g muscle, dropping at 15°C to
> pH 5·75 and 1 μMol ATP/g muscle. […] Thus, the onset of rigor is influenced by more than one
> factor. Temperature, pH and ATP concentration each play a rôle. »

> « The results are of particular interest for the handling of hot-boned meat; that is, for both
> the cooling of pre-rigor muscle and the use of hot-boned meat for processing. »

## Ce que le chapitre en tire

- Seuil : un muscle **encore avant la rigidité** se contracte quand il passe **sous 15 °C
  environ** (bœuf). Le froid d'une nuit de gel est très loin dessous.
- Un morceau détaché refroidit bien plus vite qu'une carcasse entière : c'est exactement le cas du
  dos désossé chaud.
- Le moment où la rigidité s'installe dépend de la **température**, du **pH** et de la réserve
  d'**ATP** du muscle — pas d'une durée fixe.
