## Extraits intégraux

> « Giant liver flukes infect a large spectrum free-living animals (deer, moose, reindeer) and
> domestic ruminants (cattle, sheep, goat) »

> « Unlike other liver flukes, Fascioloides magna is found directly in pseudofibrosis cysts in the
> liver. »

> « The definitive hosts, ungulates, are infected when they ingest grass on which the cercariae
> have encysted. »

> « Large ungulates such as moose (Alces alces), bison (Bison bison), sika deer (Cervus nippon),
> llama (Lama glama), horse, pig, and cattle are classified as dead-end hosts »

> « Unlike aberrant hosts, the immune system of dead-end hosts also creates a pseudocyst in the
> hepatic parenchyma, but these accumulate immature eggs because they do not have an outlet into
> the environment »

> « Infections in domestic dead-end hosts such as bovids, suids, llamas, and horses are
> characterized by excessive fibrosis, thick-walled encapsulation of the parasite within the
> hepatic parenchyma, and black pigmentation in the abdominal cavity »

> « Foreyt and Parish (1990) confirmed this in an experimentally infected llama, where the
> pathological effects of infection were similar to that seen in cattle and moose »

## Ce que la page ne dit pas

Aucune fréquence chez l'orignal du Québec ou de la Côte-Nord : le « fréquemment » du chapitre 41
n'est pas appuyé pour la douve. La source la plus directe sur ce qu'on voit dans un foie
d'orignal est la fiche des kystes de ténia (`41-adfg-kystes-tenia-foie.md`), qui dit
« commonly ».
