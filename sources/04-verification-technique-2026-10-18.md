## 1. AIEA, TRS-472 (2010) : le césium dans le poisson d'eau douce et dans le sol

- **Document :** IAEA, *Handbook of Parameter Values for the Prediction of Radionuclide Transfer in Terrestrial and
  Freshwater Environments*, Technical Reports Series No. 472, Vienne, 2010, ISBN 978-92-0-113009-9.
- **URL :** https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/trs472_web.pdf (HTTP 200)
- **Capture :** `04-aiea-trs472-2010-transfert-terre-eau-douce.pdf` (document entier, 1,6 Mo)
- **Consulté le :** 2026-10-18

> Tableau 57, « Summary of concentration ratios (CR) for freshwater fish tissues (L kg–1, fresh weight) », ligne Cs :
> corps entier, N = 145, moyenne 3.0×10³, minimum 7.5×10¹, maximum 2.4×10⁴ ; **muscle, N = 106, moyenne 2.5×10³**,
> minimum 1.4×10², maximum 1.5×10⁴.

> « The fish to water CR for radiocaesium and radiostrontium can be tabulated accounting for the inverse relationship
> between the CR and the analogous potassium and calcium concentration, respectively, in the surrounding water »
> ; « For radiocaesium in predatory/omnivorous fishes, the CR can be estimated using the following equation : CR =
> 4880 / [K+] where [K+] is the concentration of potassium (K+) in lake water (in mg L–1). »

> Tableau 12, « Kd values for selected radionuclides in soils » (L kg–1) : **Cs, tous sols, N = 469, moyenne
> 1.2×10³** (sable 5.3×10², loam et argile 3.7×10², organique 2.7×10²) ; **I, tous sols, N = 250, moyenne 6.9**.

> Tableaux 26 et 27, transfert au lait (Fm, d L–1), ligne I : vache 5.4×10⁻³ ; chèvre 2.2×10⁻¹.

> Tableau 76, « Food processing retention factor (Fr) and the processing efficiency (Pe) for meat », mammifères
> (vache, porc, mouton, cerf, lapin) : « Boiling meat » : Cs, Fr de référence 0.4 (0.2 à 0.7) ; Sr 0.5. « Boiling
> bone » : **Cs, Fr de référence 0.3 (0.2 à 0.3) ; I 0.98 ; Sr 0.99**. « Pickling wet (salting), marinating » : Cs
> 0.5 (0.1 à 0.7).

**Lecture :** le poisson d'eau douce concentre en moyenne 2 500 fois dans son muscle le césium de l'eau, et
d'autant plus que l'eau est pauvre en potassium (lac pauvre, lac de tête). La terre retient le césium dissous
environ 170 fois plus fort que l'iode (1 200 contre 6,9 L/kg). Le lait de chèvre prend beaucoup plus d'iode, par
unité d'iode mangée, que le lait de vache. Bouillir un os y laisse 99 % de son strontium et en sort 70 % du
césium : le bouillon d'os porte du césium, presque pas de strontium.

## 2. AIEA, TRS-422 (2004) : le césium dans le poisson de mer

- **Document :** IAEA, *Sediment Distribution Coefficients and Concentration Factors for Biota in the Marine
  Environment*, Technical Reports Series No. 422, Vienne, 2004, ISBN 92-0-114403-2.
- **URL :** https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/TRS422_web.pdf (HTTP 200)
- **Capture :** `04-aiea-trs422-2004-facteurs-concentration-mer.pdf` (document entier, 0,5 Mo)

> Tableau III, « Concentration factors for fish », ligne Cs : IAEA-TECDOC-211, 5 × 10¹ ; **valeur recommandée,
> 1 × 10²** (note F20).

> F20 : « The recommended CF is based primarily on measurements of 137Cs in fish and samples of sea water. »

**Lecture :** 100 L/kg pour le poisson de mer, contre 2 500 L/kg en moyenne pour le muscle du poisson d'eau douce
(TRS-472) : à même concentration de césium dans l'eau, le poisson de mer en prend environ 25 fois moins.

## 3. Ministère de l'Environnement du Japon (2018) : poisson de mer et poisson d'eau douce après Fukushima

- **Document :** MOE, *BOOKLET to Provide Basic Information Regarding Health Effects of Radiation*, chap. 8.6,
  « Inspection Results for Fishery Products (Marine Fish Species Caught off the Coast of Fukushima Prefecture and
  Freshwater Fish Species Caught in Fukushima Prefecture) », mis à jour le 28 février 2018.
- **URL :** https://www.env.go.jp/en/chemi/rhm/basic-info/2018/pdf/basic-1st-08-06-02.pdf (HTTP 200)
- **Capture :** `04-japon-moe-2018-poisson-mer-et-eau-douce.pdf`

> « The percentage of samples exceeding the standard limit (100 Bq/kg) was 57% for marine fish and 45% for freshwater
> fish during the period from April to June 2011, but the percentage decreased by half in one year after the accident.
> [...] There have been no such marine fish samples since FY2015, but some freshwater fish samples still show
> radioactive cesium concentrations exceeding the standard limit. »

**Lecture :** au début, le poisson de mer près de la centrale était plus chargé, parce que la centrale a rejeté de
l'eau contaminée directement dans la mer ; il s'est ensuite déchargé plus vite que le poisson d'eau douce. Sans rejet
direct dans le fleuve, le cas de la Côte-Nord est celui des tableaux de l'AIEA.

## 5. ORNL, Kearny, *Nuclear War Survival Skills* (1987) : passages nouveaux pour le ch. 4

**Capture déjà au dossier :** `06-ornl-kearny-nwss-1987.pdf` (texte extrait par `pdftotext` le 18 octobre 2026).
Numéros de page du livre.

### La pluie et l'eau (chap. 8, p. 71 et 72)

> « Survivors of a nuclear attack should realize that neither fallout particles nor dissolved radioactive elements or
> compounds can be removed from water by chemical disinfection or boiling. »

> « 5. Contaminated water from streams, which would be especially dangerous if the stream is muddy from the first
> heavy rains after fallout is deposited. The first runoff will contain most of the radioactive material that can be
> dissolved from fallout particles deposited on the drainage area. »

> « 6. Water collected from fallout-contaminated roofs. This would contain more fallout particles than would the runoff
> from the ground. 7. Water obtained by melting snow that has fallen through air containing fallout particles, or from
> snow lying on the ground onto which fallout has fallen. Avoid using such water for drinking or cooking, if
> possible. »

> (p. 73) « 4. Take soil containing some clay almost any soil will do from at least 4 inches below the surface of the
> ground. (Nearly all fallout particles remain near the surface except after deposition on sand or gravel.) »

> (p. 72 et 73) « Filtering through earth removes essentially all of the fallout particles and more of the dissolved
> radioactive material than does boiling-water distillation, a generally impractical purification method that does
> not eliminate dangerous radio-active iodines. Earth filters are also more effective in removing radioactive iodines
> than are ordinary ion-exchange water softeners or charcoal filters. »

### Les mythes (chap. 2)

> « Food and water in dust-tight containers are not contaminated by fallout radiation. Peeling fruits and vegetables
> removes essentially all fallout [...]. Water from many sources -- such as deep wells and covered reservoirs, tanks,
> and containers -- would not be contaminated. Even water containing dissolved radioactive elements and compounds can
> be made safe for drinking by simply filtering it through earth »

### Les légumes (p. 116)

> « As a general rule, do not eat fresh vegetables until advised it is safe to do so. If under wartime conditions no
> official advice is obtainable, avoid eating fresh leafy vegetables that were growing or exposed at the time of
> fallout deposition; thoroughly wash all vegetables and fruits. »

### Les retombées d'une explosion en altitude (chap. 5, p. 49)

> « Fortunately, air bursts produce only tiny particles, and only a small fraction of these, while they still are very
> radioactive, are likely to be promptly brought to earth in scattered "hot spots" by rain-outs and snow-outs. »

### La neige comme écran (p. 158)

> « Use snow for shielding material for aboveground shelters if the earth is so deep-frozen that digging is
> impractical. [...] With wetted or well-packed snow 5 ft thick, the protection factor is about 50. »

### La pompe : un panneau sans volets ne pompe pas (chap. 6, p. 50 ; annexe B, p. 194 à 207)

> (p. 50) « I called it a Punkah-Pump, because its hand-pulled operation is somewhat like that of an ancient fan
> called a "punkah" [...]. (Unlike the punkah, however, this air pump can force air to move in a desired direction
> and is a true pump.) »

> (p. 194) « As can be seen in Figs. 1 and 2, a KAP operates by being swung like a pendulum. It is hinged at the top
> of its swinging frame. When this air pump is pulled by a cord as illustrated, its flaps are closed by air pressure
> and it pushes air in front of it and "sucks" air in back of it. [...] During its power stroke, the pump's flaps are
> closed against its flap-stop wires or strings, which are fastened across the face of the frame. »

> (p. 195) « When a KAP swings freely back as a pendulum on its return stroke, all its flaps are opened by air
> pressure. The pumped air stream continues to flow in the direction in which it has been accelerated by the power
> stroke, while the pump itself swings in the opposite direction (see Fig. 2). Thus the flaps are one-way valves that
> operate to force air to flow in one direction, where desired. »

> (p. 195) « If your doorway or other ventilation opening is narrower or wider than 30 inches, you should make your
> KAP 1 inch narrower than the narrowest opening in which you plan to install it. »

> (p. 197) « If the frame is made of only one thickness of board 3/4 in. to 1 in. thick, it will not be sufficiently
> heavy to swing back far enough on its free-swinging return stroke. »

> (p. 199) « So that the flaps may swing open on only one side of the frame (on its front, or face), you must attach
> horizontal flap-stops made of strings or wires across the face of the frame. »

> (p. 201) « To pump its maximum volume, an air-supply KAP with good metal hinges should be installed in its opening so
> that it swings only about 1/2 in. above the bottom of the opening and only 1/2 in. to 1 in. from the sides of the
> opening. »

> (p. 205) « Operate your 3-foot KAP by pulling it with an easy, swinging motion of your arm. To pump the maximum volume
> of air, you should pull the KAP toward you until its frame swings out to an almost-horizontal position. Then quickly
> move your hand so that the pull-cord is kept slack during the entire, free-swinging return stroke. »

> (p. 206) « For pumps 24 inches or less in height, make the finished flaps only 3-1/2 inches wide and space their
> pivot-wires 3 inches apart. [...] A KAP 24 inches high will pump enough outdoor air for only a few people, except
> in cold weather. Small, yet efficient KAPs can be made even if the only materials available are straight sticks
> about 1-1/4 inches in diameter, strips of cloth to tie the frame together and to make the hinges and the pull cord,
> polyethylene film from large trash bags for the flaps, freezer or duct tape (or needle and thread) to make the flap
> hems, coat hanger wire or string for the pivot-wire, and string or ordinary thread for the flap-stops. A sharp knife
> is the only essential tool. »

> (p. 194) « The visible fallout particles rapidly "fall out" of slow moving air. The air that a KAP pumps through a
> shelter moves at a low speed and could carry into the shelter only a very small fraction of the fallout particles
> that cause the radiation hazard outside. This fraction, usually not dangerous, can be further reduced if occupants
> take the simple precautions described in these instructions. »

> (annexe E, p. 271) « Furnace or air-conditioner dust filters, those made of oiled fiberglass fibers, will remove
> practically all but the very smallest fallout particles. [...] (If commercial dust filters are not available, bath
> towel cloth will serve. However, in very dusty areas a cloth filter may become overloaded, thus seriously reducing
> the rate of airflow much sooner than if an oiled fiber filter is used as a prefilter.) »

**Conversions :** 1 po = 2,5 cm (cadre plus étroit que l'ouverture) ; ½ po = 1,3 cm, 1 po = 2,5 cm (jeu) ; 3½ po =
8,9 cm (largeur des volets) ; 3 po = 7,6 cm (écart des fils) ; 24 po = 61 cm ; 1¼ po = 3,2 cm (bâtons) ; 5 pi =
1,5 m (neige).

## 6. AIEA (2006) : le césium d'une centrale lointaine se dissout presque tout

- **Document :** IAEA, *Environmental Consequences of the Chernobyl Accident and their Remediation: Twenty Years of
  Experience*, Report of the Chernobyl Forum Expert Group « Environment », Radiological Assessment Reports Series,
  Vienne, 2006.
- **URL :** https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/Pub1239_web.pdf (HTTP 200, 9,1 Mo)
- **Captures :** `04-aiea-2006-tchornobyl-environnement-titre.pdf` (page de titre) et
  `04-aiea-2006-tchornobyl-environnement-p33-34-texte.txt` (texte des pages 33 et 34 du PDF ; la page 34 porte une
  carte de 8,8 Mo, elle n'est pas recopiée en PDF).

> « In fallout sampled at the Chernobyl meteorological station from 26 April to 5 May 1986 [...], the water soluble and
> exchangeable [...] forms of 137Cs varied from 5% to more than 30% [...]. At shorter distances the portion of water
> soluble and exchangeable forms of 137Cs and 90Sr was, obviously, lower, due to the presence of larger particles; at
> longer distances the fraction of soluble condensed particles increased. As one example, almost all the 137Cs
> deposited in 1986 in the United Kingdom was water soluble and exchangeable. »

**Lecture :** les « moins de 2 % dissous » de l'ORNL valent pour les retombées d'une explosion (particules fondues) ;
loin d'une centrale accidentée, le césium tombe sous une forme qui se dissout presque toute.

## 8. FDA (2004) : l'iodure de potassium ne se dégrade pas

- **Document :** FDA, CDER, *Guidance for Federal Agencies and State and Local Governments. Potassium Iodide Tablets
  Shelf Life Extension*, mars 2004.
- **URL :** https://www.fda.gov/media/72521/download (HTTP 200)
- **Capture :** `04-fda-2004-ki-duree-de-conservation.pdf`

> « Stability studies over many years have confirmed that none of the components of KI tablets, including the active
> ingredient, has any significant potential for chemical degradation or interaction with other components or with
> components of the container closure system when stored according to labeled directions. »

> « To date, the only observed changes during stability testing have been the failure of some batches of KI tablets to
> meet the USP S1 dissolution specification [...]. Some tablets tested required slightly longer than the specified
> time to achieve dissolution, but even in the case of a failure of this sort, the product would remain usable. In such
> cases, instructions can be provided to crush the tablets and mix them with a juice or other liquid prior to
> administration [...]. In the specific case of KI tablets, a yellowish discoloration would be indicative of stability
> problems. Since pure KI is known to be very stable (as long as it is protected from moist air), ongoing evaluation and
> testing of each batch is probably unnecessary as long as the market package remains intact »

Et, dans le document de 2002 déjà au dossier (`04-fda-2002-ki-questions-reponses.pdf`, Q2 et Q4) :

> « Higher doses (e.g., up to 130 mg) would be equally effective and, particularly among school-age children,
> extremely safe » ; « absolute precision in dosing is generally not critical to safety or efficacy » ; « the overall
> benefits of taking up to 130 mg of KI instead of the lower doses recommended for certain age groups far exceed the
> small risks of overdosing. However, where feasible, adherence to FDA guidance should be attempted when dosing
> infants. »

## 9. REMM (HHS) : se décontaminer la peau sans eau

- **Document :** U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, *Radiation Emergency Medical Management (REMM)*,
  « Management of Patients with External Contamination ».
- **URL :** https://remm.hhs.gov/ext_contamination.htm (HTTP 200)
- **Capture :** `04-remm-hhs-decontamination-externe.html`

> « In a water deficient environment, gently brush skin surface to remove a portion of the stratum corneum layer and
> dislodge contamination held by skin proteins. »

> « Wash hair [...] with tepid water and mild soap or shampoo without conditioner. [...] Conditioners should not be
> used because they can bind radioactive material to hair protein » ; « Contaminated hair can be clipped if shampooing
> is ineffective. Do not shave hair »

**Ce que la page ne dit pas :** rien sur le ruban adhésif. Aucune page officielle trouvée (CDC, Ready.gov, REMM, ORNL,
gouvernement du Royaume-Uni) ne fait lever la poussière de la peau ou des cheveux au ruban ; une procédure
universitaire trouvée par le moteur de recherche le fait sur la peau et prévient que l'arrachement irrite.

## 10. Le poste d'auto : les deux fils d'alimentation

- **Document :** Retro Manufacturing (fabricant de postes de remplacement), « Wiring Your Radio ». Page de fabricant,
  pas une source officielle.
- **URL :** https://help.retromanufacturing.com/knowledge-base/wiring-your-radio/ (HTTP 200)
- **Capture :** `04-retromanufacturing-fils-poste-auto.html`

> « Basic Wiring: Yellow wire 12 volts constant · Red wire 12 volts ignition/accessory · Black wire ground. If you do
> not connect ALL of these wires the radio WILL NOT work. »

**Lecture :** sur un poste de remplacement, le jaune alimente et le rouge allume ; il faut les deux au positif. Sur un
poste d'origine, les couleurs ne suivent pas ce code (même page : chercher les fils au multimètre).
