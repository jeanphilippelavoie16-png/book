## Extraits intégraux (anglais)

### 9.2.3 — Surveillance

> « 11. Reassure and monitor: reassure and constantly re-check the person's condition for any
> change. »

> « 5. If unconscious and breathing normally: place the person on their side (Recovery Position)
> and ensure the airway is clear and frequently check breathing »

### 9.2.3 — Signes de gravité chez l'adulte

> « rapid breathing (breathing rate ≥ 22 breaths per minute) is the most reliable indicator of
> serious illness in adults. »

> « rapid heart rate (>100/min for adult not exercising), but may become weak or slow or new
> irregular heart rate »

> « cool, sweaty, pale, blotchy, or discoloured (often described as mottled) skin »

### 9.2.3 — Signes de gravité chez l'enfant

> « Serious illness in infants and children can deteriorate quickly. »

> « Altered responsiveness: hard to wake, unusually sleepy or floppy, decreased responsiveness. »

> « Circulation/urine output: not passing urine (or no wet nappy) for several hours. »

> « In children, a parent's worry that their child is more unwell than their usual childhood
> illness, or a first aider's sense that "something is not right," should always be treated as a
> red flag. These concerns can signal serious illness, including sepsis, even before changes in
> vital signs are obvious. »

### 9.1.4 — Blessure à la tête

> « 5. Ongoing observation: If concerned about a possible significant head injury, maintain close
> monitoring until the person is handed over to a health care professional. »

## Ce que ces extraits permettent d'écrire

Aucun intervalle en minutes. La règle publiée est **qualitative** : surveiller **constamment** tant
que l'état est grave, **vérifier souvent** la respiration d'une personne inconsciente, et traiter
tout changement comme un signal. Hors drapeau, deux repères chiffrés utiles à la feuille de suivi
du chapitre : chez l'adulte, **22 respirations par minute ou plus** est le meilleur indicateur de
maladie grave, et un pouls **au-dessus de 100 au repos** en est un autre.
