## Extraits intégraux

### Le thuya contient de la thuyone, et c'est son constituant principal

> « Thuja occidentalis leaves and twigs are rich in essential oils, mainly terpenes with thujone
> being the predominant constituent. The leaves of Thuja occidentalis contain 0.4% to 1% essential
> oils. The total content of essential oils in dried young twigs was reported as 1.4 to 4%. The
> content of thujone in dried twigs was determined as 7.6 mg/g (0.76%), consisting of 85%
> α-thujone and 15% of the more toxic β-thujone. »

> « The pharmacodynamic properties of Thuja occidentalis are mainly attributed to the essential
> oils, especially thujone. Thujone is a strong irritant and has cytotoxic properties. »

### Elle passe par les muqueuses et même par la peau

> « The lipophilic character of the constituents of concern like thujone is indicative of a
> relatively good dermal resorption from mucous membranes and even from intact skin. Thujone is
> predominantly eliminated via kidneys and lungs. »

### Les intoxications humaines

> « In humans, Thuja extracts were used in folk medicine as a contraceptive and an abortificiant.
> In cases of overdosing and abuse, the oral intake of Thuja extracts induced severe metabolic
> disturbances especially degenerative disorders of the liver. The intoxication was accompanied by
> irritant effects on the mucous membranes of the gastro-intestinal tract, severe and long lasting
> contractions also of the uterus and severe metabolic disorders due to liver and kidney lesions
> with fatal outcome. However, the symptoms of poisoning after oral intake of plant material are
> normally mild. Infants who ingested leaves or small twigs of Thuja occidentalis showed mild
> gastrointestinal disorders and vomiting. »

> « Repeated intake of thujone-containing beverages (e.g. absinth) was reported to cause permanent
> disorders of the central nervous system. »

### Doses

> « As regard the constituent thujone LD50 values were given as 87.5 mg/kg bw after subcutaneous
> administration in mice and 240 mg/kg bw after intraperitoneal administration to rats. For
> humans, an oral intake of 1.25 mg/kg bw (about 75 mg/person) was reported to be without adverse
> effects. »

> « In traditional phytotherapy, recommended oral doses of the drug consisting of dried young twigs
> are for instance 1 to 2 g, corresponding to up to 40 to 80 mg thujone, 3 times a day. »

## Ce que le livre en tire

- **Le thuya occidental contient de la thuyone, et c'est le constituant principal de son huile
  essentielle** : 7,6 mg par gramme de rameaux séchés. La règle « thuyone du cèdre toxique » a
  maintenant sa source directe.
- **Ce qui tue, c'est l'extrait concentré, pas la feuille mâchouillée** : l'extrait pris en
  surdose ou en usage abusif a donné des lésions du foie et des reins mortelles ; la plante
  avalée telle quelle donne d'habitude des troubles légers (vomissements chez le jeune enfant).
  C'est exactement le mécanisme du chapitre 31 : **l'infusion concentrée, préparée pour bien
  faire**, est la forme dangereuse.
- Recoupement de dose, calcul sur les valeurs des deux sources, pas une mesure : à 7,6 mg/g,
  **moins d'un gramme de rameaux séchés** dépasse la limite canadienne de 6 mg de thuyone par
  jour chez l'adulte (`25-sante-canada-nnhpd-thuyone-limite.md`).
