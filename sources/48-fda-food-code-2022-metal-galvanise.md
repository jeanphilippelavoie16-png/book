# FDA — Food Code 2022 : métal galvanisé, cuivre et laiton au contact des aliments

- **Organisme :** U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), U.S. Public Health Service
- **Titre :** *Food Code 2022 — Recommendations of the United States Public Health Service*
  (version du 18 janvier 2023)
- **URL :** https://www.fda.gov/media/164194/download
- **Consultée le :** 24 septembre 2026
- **Page enregistrée :** `sources/48-fda-food-code-2022.pdf` (le document complet, 4,99 Mo)
- **Sert à :** ch. 48, §9 — contenant galvanisé et gras chaud. Peut servir aux ch. 60, 84 et 88
  (cuivre, laiton, galvanisé).

## Extraits intégraux — la règle (section 4-101.15)

> « 4-101.15 Galvanized Metal, Use Limitation. Galvanized metal may not be used for UTENSILS or
> FOOD-CONTACT SURFACES of EQUIPMENT that are used in contact with acidic FOOD. »

## Extraits intégraux — le mécanisme (Annexe 3, p. 156, « Public Health Reasons »)

> « Galvanized means iron or steel coated with zinc. Metals such as iron and steel are coated
> with zinc to prevent rusting. Under certain conditions, zinc may leach from galvanized
> food-contact surfaces into foods that are high in water content. The risk of leaching increases
> with increased acidity of foods contacting the galvanized food-contact surface. On contact with
> acidic foods and beverages, the zinc may be converted to zinc salts which are readily absorbed
> by the body »

> « Zinc is generally considered to be non-toxic, and in fact is a required mineral for many
> processes that occur in the human body. However, zinc is known to be toxic when ingested in
> large quantities. Symptoms of zinc poisoning include vomiting, nausea, lethargy, fatigue, and
> epigastric pain. Most reports of zinc poisoning implicate contaminated food that resulted from
> storage in a galvanized metal container. »

## Extraits intégraux — cuivre et laiton (même annexe, section 4-101.14)

> « High concentrations of copper are poisonous and have caused foodborne illness. When copper
> and copper alloy surfaces contact acidic foods, copper may be leached into the food. »

> « Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc and contains lead which is used to combine the two
> elements. […] All 3 constituents are subject to leaching when they contact acidic foods, and
> food poisoning has resulted from such contact. »

## Ce que la page NE dit PAS

- Rien sur la **chaleur** comme facteur : ce qui fait passer le zinc, selon la FDA, c'est **l'eau
  dans l'aliment** et **l'acidité**.
- Rien sur le gras, cru ou fondu.
