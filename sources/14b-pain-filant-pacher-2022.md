# Pacher et coll. 2022 — Le pain filant (« ropiness »), et la température au cœur de la miche

- **Type de source :** ⚠ article de synthèse évalué par les pairs, pas une source officielle.
- **Référence :** Pacher N, Burtscher J, Johler S, Etter D, Bender D, et coll. « Ropiness in
  Bread — A Re-Emerging Spoilage Phenomenon ». *Foods* 2022;11(19):3021.
  doi:10.3390/foods11193021
- **URL :** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9564316/
- **Date de consultation :** 2026-09-23
- **Sert au chapitre :** 14b — Le pain (§3, §6, §7)

## Extraits intégraux

> « Ropy bread spoilage manifests in sticky and stringy degradation of the crumb, slime
> formation, discoloration, and an odor reminiscent of rotting fruit. »

> « Bacillus endospores are not only heat stable and able to survive baking in the center of the
> bread crumb, but they are also highly resistant to desiccation »

> « Soil is considered as the primary endospore reservoir [...] representing a major route of
> entry for Bacillus spp. into the food chain »

> « Temperature kinetics revealed that the bread core temperature increases to 100 °C in the
> first 15 min of baking. Due to the moisture evaporation–condensation mechanism, the bread crumb
> temperature becomes stable slightly below 100 °C, regardless of the oven temperature »

> « Pepe et al. [25] noticed that high-type breads (height ≥ 10 cm) were more frequently affected
> by ropiness, suggesting that endospores survive the baking procedure in the middle of the bread
> crumb. Underbaking further promotes rope development »

> « Since rope formers are very sensitive to low pH values, ropiness can be prevented by the
> addition of preservatives [...] such as propionic acid, calcium propionate, acetic acid »

> « A frequently used natural preservative of bread is the addition of sourdough (Figure 2B),
> whose microbiota is dominated by lactic acid bacteria (LAB) [84]. Due to the production of
> lactic and acetic acids by LAB, the bread pH drops drastically, thus mostly inhibiting the
> growth of the Bacillus genera. »

> « B. cereus is responsible for two foodborne gastrointestinal illnesses. [...] Consequently,
> rope-forming bacteria may be hazardous, since bread is consumed by people of all ages »

## Chiffres retenus

| Valeur | Objet |
| --- | --- |
| Le cœur de la miche plafonne **juste sous 100 °C**, quelle que soit la chaleur du four | Pourquoi la sonde lit le cœur et non le four |
| Miches de **10 cm et plus** de hauteur plus souvent atteintes | Les spores survivent au centre des grosses miches |

## Note du rédacteur

Le pain filant n'a pas de page officielle canadienne. Le chapitre en retient trois choses :
l'aspect et l'odeur (fruit pourri, mie collante qui file), le fait que les spores viennent du
sol par le grain et survivent au four au centre de la miche, et le fait que l'acidité du levain
l'empêche. La consigne (jeter la miche) ne demande aucun chiffre.
