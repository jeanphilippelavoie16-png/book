## Extraits intégraux

### Quand la raquette devient nécessaire

> « In deep snow (61 cm (2') or greater in depth) the individual has almost no mobility without the
> aid of skis or snowshoes. »

> « By using snowshoes, individual mobility will be restored to a point approximately equal to that
> of foot movement on hard ground. »

> « Skis or snowshoes are usually employed in military operations when the depth of snow is 30 cm
> (1') or more. »

### Ce que la raquette fait et ne fait pas (§ 4-36)

> « Flotation is provided by the webbing, which is closely laced and prevents the snowshoe from
> sinking too deeply into the snow when weight is placed upon it. »

> « The use of snowshoes when pulling and carrying heavy loads is particularly practical, as the hands
> and arms remain free. On steep slopes, however, the use of snowshoes is considerably limited because
> traction becomes negligible and the snowshoe will slide, causing loss of footing. »

> « In deep snow the trailbreaker must be changed frequently. Especially when wet, snow tends to stick
> to the webbing, thereby adding weight to the snowshoe. »

> « Trail. [...] The excellent flotation provided by its large surfaces makes the trail snowshoe best
> for cross-country marches, deep snow conditions, and trailbreaking. »

> « Bearpaw. This type of snowshoe is short, wide, and oval in shape, with no frame extension [...].
> The bearpaw snowshoe is preferable to the trail type for close work with weapons and vehicles, in
> heavy brush, and in other confined areas. »

### Entretien (§ 4-37)

> « Repairs must be made immediately, otherwise the webbing will loosen and start to unravel. If
> unvarnished, the rawhide webbing on wooden snowshoes will absorb moisture, stretch and turn white,
> particularly in wet snow. It should be dried out slowly, avoiding direct flames »

> « Stepping into water is to be avoided; the water will freeze and snow will stick to it. »

> « They should be kept away from open fires and out of reach of rodents. »

> « In off-seasons, wooden snowshoes are stored in a dry, well-ventilated place so that the rawhide will
> not mildew or rot and the frames warp. »

### Technique (§ 4-38)

> « In taking a stride, the toe of the snowshoe is lifted upward, to clear the snow, and thrusted
> forward. Energy is conserved by lifting it no higher than is necessary to clear the snow and slide
> the tail over it. »

> « Traction is generally very poor on hard-packed or crusty snow. Steeper terrain is ascended by
> traversing and packing a trail similar to a shelf across it. »

> « In the event the snow is sufficiently hard-frozen to support the weight of a person, it is
> generally better to remove the snowshoes and proceed temporarily on foot. »

> « Care must be taken not to place too much strain on the snowshoe ends by bridging a gap, since the
> frame may break. »

> « Wet snow will frequently ball up under the feet, interfering with comfortable walking. This snow
> should be knocked off with a stick or pole as soon as possible. »

> « The bindings must not be fastened too tightly or circulation will be cut off, and frostbite may
> occur. During halts, bindings should be checked for fit and possible readjustment. »

### Entraînement (§ 4-39)

> « Stiffness and soreness of muscles are to be expected at first. The initial training should be
> gradual with regard to loads carried and distances covered. »

### Tirer un traîneau (§ 4-43)

> « The sled harnesses are adjusted to fit loosely on the individuals. »

> « When crossing small ditches, the sled is stopped in the ditch while the pullers go as far as the
> two ropes allow. Then, by a simultaneous pull, the sled is brought up out of the ditch. To change
> direction in woods, the pullers continue to move straight forward until the sled comes to the
> desired turning point. »

> « In difficult terrain a relaying technique may be used [...]. When using this technique care must
> be taken to insure that the sled is well anchored each time the pullers move up since a runaway sled
> may not only damage itself but is a serious hazard to anyone below. Where steep slopes must be
> ascended for considerable distances, less energy will be expended if the sleds are left behind and
> the sled load backpacked to the objective. »

> « If the team is on snowshoes, the pullers can descend normally while the man in the rear insures
> that the sled does not overrun those in front. »

> « For short descents in wooded areas, the braker should position himself behind a tree for added
> stability in lowering the sled. »

> « Traversing by both skiers and snowshoes may be used on long, steep downhill slopes. In this case the
> puller nearest the sled and the rear man should remain above the sled and as far from it as the ropes
> will allow. »

> « In very steep terrain a long rope, when available, may be used to lower the sled straight down the
> slope. »

## Ce qu'on en tire pour le chapitre 20b

- Seuils de neige : **30 cm** (la raquette devient l'outil normal), **61 cm** (sans elle, on ne
  bouge presque plus).
- Patte d'ours pour le bois dense et les endroits serrés ; raquette longue pour ouvrir la trace en
  neige profonde et à découvert.
- La babiche non vernie boit l'eau, s'étire et blanchit ; elle sèche lentement, loin de la flamme.
- Attache trop serrée : la circulation coupe et la gelure arrive. Vérifier aux haltes.
- Croûte qui porte : on enlève les raquettes. Ne jamais faire porter le cadre en pont.
- Traîneau : harnais lâche ; fossé franchi en arrêtant la charge au fond puis en tirant ensemble ;
  virage au bout de la corde ; en descente, un homme derrière retient ; charge qui s'échappe =
  danger grave pour qui est en bas.
