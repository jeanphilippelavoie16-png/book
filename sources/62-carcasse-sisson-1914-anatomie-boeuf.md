## Squelette du bœuf

> « The usual vertebral formula is C7 T13 L6 S5 Cy18-20. » (p. 125)

(7 cervicales, 13 thoraciques, 6 lombaires, 5 sacrées.)

Fémur (p. 152-153) : « The head is smaller than in the horse, and the articular surface extends
considerably on the upper surface of the neck. The fovea capitis is a small depression on the
middle of the head for the attachment of the round ligament. » — et, sur l'os long en général
(p. 19) : « The cylindrical part, termed the shaft or body (Corpus), is tubular, and incloses the
medullary cavity, which contains the medulla or marrow. »

Articulation de la hanche du bœuf (p. 250) : « Ox. — The shallowness of the acetabulum is
compensated by the greater size of the marginal cartilage, which is specially large laterally.
The head of the femur has a smaller radius of curvature than that of the horse, and the articular
surface extends a considerable distance outward on the upper surface of the neck. The round
ligament is entirely intra-articular; it is small, and sometimes absent. The accessory ligament is
absent. » — Chez le cheval (p. 231) : « The round ligament (Lig. teres) is a short, strong band
which is attached in the subpubic groove close to the acetabular notch, passes outward, and ends
in the notch on the head of the femur ».

Ligament nuchal du bœuf (p. 244, fig. 256, enregistrée : `sisson-1914-fig256-ligament-nuchal-boeuf.jpg`) :
« Ox. — The ligamentum nuchae is better developed than in the horse. The funicular part is clearly
divided into two lateral halves, which are round at their occipital attachment, but from the axis
backward become rapidly wider and flat. This wide portion is almost sagittal, lies on either side
of the vertebral spines, and is covered by the trapezius and rhomboideus muscles. From the highest
part of the withers (third thoracic spine) it gradually diminishes in size and fades out in the
lumbar region. »

## Muscles du bœuf (chapitre « The Muscles of the Ox », p. 344-358)

**Cou.** « The splenius is thin. It arises directly from the first three or four thoracic spines,
and ends by a thin tendon on the occipital bone, the wing of the atlas, and the transverse process
of the axis, blending with the brachiocephalicus, longissimus capitis, and omo-transversarius. »

**Ceinture de l'épaule.** « The trapezius is much thicker and broader than in the horse, and is
undivided. It arises on the ligamentum nuchae and supraspinous ligament, from the atlas to the
twelfth thoracic vertebra. » — « The rhomboideus is clearly divided into cervical and thoracic
parts ». — « The superficial pectoral muscle is thinner than in the horse and its two parts are not
so clearly separable. » — « The deep pectoral muscle arises as far forward as the second rib and
is undivided. » — « The serratus ventralis is clearly divided into cervical and thoracic parts.
The former is large and extends from the third (or second) cervical vertebra to the fifth rib […]
It is inserted on a large triangular area on the antero-superior part of the costal surface of the
scapula. The thoracic part is relatively thin and is covered by a very strong aponeurosis; it is
attached to the fourth to the ninth ribs by six digitations ».

**Épaule et bras.** « The supraspinatus, infraspinatus, and teres minor do not differ materially
from those of the horse. » — « The medial head of the triceps is more developed than in the horse,
and extends up to the neck of the humerus. »

**Thorax et diaphragme.** « The diaphragm presents several important differential features. Its
slope is much steeper and its width is greater than in the horse. The upper limit of the costal
attachment extends almost in a straight line from the last rib about a hand-breadth above its
middle to the junction of the eighth rib with its cartilage, and along the latter to the sternum.
[…] The right crus divides into two branches, which circumscribe the hiatus oesophageus […] The
left crus is small. »

**Dos.** « The longissimus dorsi resembles that of the horse, but it is more fleshy anteriorly, and
the spinalis dorsi is clearly distinguishable from the common mass. »

**Paroi abdominale.** « The obliquus abdominis externus […] The direction of the fibers in the
flank is horizontal » ; « The rectus abdominis arises on the lateral border of the sternum as far
forward as the third costal cartilage. […] There are five tendinous inscriptions ».

**Muscles sous-lombaires.** « The psoas minor begins at the disc between the twelfth and
thirteenth thoracic vertebrae. The psoas major has a fleshy origin on the posterior border of the
last rib, and a thin tendon attached to the twelfth rib […] The iliacus begins under the body of
the sixth lumbar vertebra, and is more closely united with the psoas major than in the horse. »

**Hanche et cuisse, face externe.** « The tensor fasciae latae is large, and the fleshy part extends
further down than in the horse. » — « The gluteus medius is small, the lumbar part being
insignificant and extending forward only to the fourth lumbar vertebra. Its deep portion (gluteus
accessorius) is easily separable ». — « The biceps femoris is very wide at its upper part […] It
arises from the sacral spines, sacro-sciatic ligament, and tuber ischii. It is divided by a
fibro-elastic septum in the thigh into two portions, which end in front and below on a wide
aponeurosis; the latter is attached to the patella and its lateral ligament and blends with the
fascia cruris and fascia lata. There is no femoral attachment. » — « The semitendinosus and
semimembranosus arise on the ischium only. The latter is very large and has a branch attached to
the medial condyle of the tibia. »

**Face antérieure.** « The quadriceps femoris resembles that of the horse; but the vasti (and
especially the medial one) are not so thick, and the vastus intermedius is more clearly separable,
and consists of two parts. »

**Face interne.** « The sartorius arises by two heads […] The gracilis is more extensively united
with its fellow at its origin than in the horse. The pectineus is large […] The adductor resembles
that of the horse, but does not reach to the medial condyle of the femur. »

## Muscles du cheval auxquels renvoie le texte du bœuf

**Complexus (*semispinalis capitis*)** (p. 273) : « This is a large triangular muscle which lies
chiefly on the ligamentum nuchae, under cover of the splenius. […] Structure. — The origin of the
muscle at the withers is aponeurotic. In the neck the bundles arising from the articular processes
run obliquely upward and forward, giving the lower part of the muscle a distinct pennate character.
The dorsal part of the belly is crossed obliquely by four or five tendinous intersections. »

**Supraspinatus** (p. 298-299) : « This muscle occupies the supraspinous fossa, which it fills,
and beyond which it extends […] Origin. — The supraspinous fossa, the spine, and the lower part of
the cartilage of the scapula. Insertion. — The anterior parts of the proximal tuberosities of the
humerus. […] The surface of the muscle is covered by a strong aponeurosis ».

**Infraspinatus** (p. 299) : « This muscle occupies the greater part of the infraspinous fossa and
extends beyond it posteriorly. […] This muscle is also covered by a strong aponeurosis […] A thick
tendinous layer partially divides the muscle into two strata, and, coming to the surface at the
shoulder joint, constitutes the chief means of insertion. »

**Triceps brachii** (p. 303) : « This, together with the preceding muscle, constitutes the large
muscular mass which fills the angle between the posterior border of the scapula and the humerus.
It is clearly divisible into three heads. (a) Long head […] the largest and longest of the three
heads, is a powerful, thick, triangular muscle, which extends from the posterior border of the
scapula to the olecranon. […] the muscle is penetrated by a tendinous intersection from which many
fibers take origin obliquely. »

**Longissimus dorsi** (p. 278) : « This is the largest and longest muscle in the body. It extends
from the sacrum and ilium to the neck, filling up the space between the spinous processes medially
and the lumbar transverse processes and the upper ends of the ribs ventrally; consequently it has
the form of a three-sided prism. »

**Psoas major** (p. 319) : « It is triangular, with the base anterior. Origin. — The ventral
surfaces of the transverse processes of the lumbar vertebrae and the last two ribs. Insertion. —
The trochanter minor of the femur, by a common tendon with the iliacus. »

**Tensor fasciae latae** (p. 320) : « It is triangular in form, with its apex at the tuber coxae. »

**Gluteus medius** (p. 321) : « Insertion. — (1) The summit of the trochanter major of the femur ».

**Semitendinosus** (p. 323) : « It lies at first behind the biceps, then passes downward on the
back of the thigh, between that muscle and the semimembranosus ».

**Semimembranosus** (p. 324) : « This very large, three-sided muscle lies on the medial surface of
the preceding muscle and the gastrocnemius ».

**Gracilis** (p. 325) : « This is a wide, quadrilateral muscle, which is situated behind the
sartorius, and covers the greater part of the medial surface of the thigh. »

**Pectineus** (p. 326) : « This muscle is fusiform and extends from the anterior border of the
pubis to the middle of the medial border of the femur. »

**Quadriceps femoris** (p. 329) : « This constitutes the large muscular mass which covers the front
and sides of the femur. It has four heads, one of which, the rectus, arises from the ilium; the
other three arise from the femur. All are inserted into the patella. (1) Rectus femoris. — This is
fusiform and rounded. »

**Rectus abdominis** (p. 290) : « This muscle is confined to the ventral part of the abdominal
wall; it extends from the sternal region to the pubis. […] Structure. — The fibers of the muscle
are directed longitudinally. Nine to eleven transverse bands of fibrous tissue extend in an
irregular manner across the » [muscle] — chez le bœuf, cinq inscriptions (voir plus haut).

**Diaphragme** (p. 285-287) : « The muscle consists of a fleshy rim which may be subdivided into
costal and sternal parts; a lumbar part, composed of two crura; and a tendinous center. » —
« (3) Lumbar part (Pars lumbalis): (a) The right crus (Crus dextrum) is attached to the ventral
longitudinal ligament, and by this means to the first four or five lumbar vertebrae. (b) The left
crus (Crus sinistrum) is attached in a similar fashion to the first and second lumbar vertebrae. »
— « Between the crura and the attachment to the last rib the edge of the muscle crosses the
ventral surface of the psoas muscles without attachment, forming the so-called lumbo-costal arch ».
(Cheval ; pour le bœuf, voir plus haut : pente plus raide, pilier droit fendu en deux autour de
l'œsophage, pilier gauche petit.)

**Rectus femoris, structure** (p. 330) : « The lower portion of the muscle is pennate, the fibers
on either side converging on the tendon at an acute angle. »

**Vastus lateralis** (p. 330-331) : « This lies on the lateral surface of the femur, extending from
the great trochanter to the patella. It is wide in its middle, smaller at each end. […] Structure.
— The fibers are directed downward and forward, many being inserted into the tendinous sheet which
covers the side of the rectus. »

Figure du diaphragme (p. 286, fig. 275, « Diaphragm of Horse; Abdominal Surface », d'après
Schmaltz ; enregistrée : `sisson-1914-fig275-diaphragme-psoas-cheval.jpg`) : vue de la cavité,
avec le *psoas minor* et le *psoas major* sous les vertèbres lombaires, les piliers (C.d., C.s.), la
partie costale (« Pars costalis »), le centre tendineux, l'arc costal.

**Gastrocnemius** (p. 338) : « Insertion. — The posterior part of the tuber calcis. Action. — To
extend the hock and to flex the stifle ».

**Lecture des lettres sur la planche du cerf (Tafel 3).** La légende du volume IV n'a pas été
trouvée en ligne (seul le volume de texte du cheval, 1900, est à l'Internet Archive). Les lettres
se lisent par la légende du bœuf donnée par Sisson (mêmes auteurs, même système), **en
vérifiant la position** : p (croupe) = *gluteus medius* ; q, q' (grand muscle de la face externe
de la cuisse) = *biceps femoris* **[lecture par position, lettre absente des légendes de
Sisson]** ; r (arrière de la cuisse) = *semitendinosus* ; o (devant de la cuisse, sous la pointe de
la hanche) = *tensor fasciae latae* **[par position]** ; f, f' = chefs long et latéral du
*triceps* ; g, g' = pectoral superficiel ; h = pectoral profond ; i = *serratus ventralis* ; k =
*latissimus dorsi* ; l, l' = oblique externe ; 16 = pointe de la hanche (*tuber coxae*) ; 17 =
pointe de la fesse (*tuber ischii*) ; 19 = grand trochanter ; 20 = rotule ; 8 = olécrane.
Squelette (Tafel 4) : H. = vertèbre cervicale (Halswirbel), R. = côte (Rippe), L. = vertèbre
lombaire (Lendenwirbel) — la planche marque la **13e côte** (13.R.) et la **6e lombaire** (6.L.).
