## Extraits intégraux

> « Jerky can be made from almost any lean meat, including beef, pork, venison or smoked turkey
> breast. »

> « If pork or wild game is used to make jerky, the meat should be treated to kill the trichinella
> parasite before it is sliced and marinated. »

> « When preparing jerky from wild game, it is important to remember that the wound location and
> skill of the hunter can affect the safety of the meat. »

> « The risk of foodborne illness from home-dried jerky can be decreased by allowing the internal
> temperature of the meat to reach 160ºF, but in such a way as to prevent case hardening. Two
> methods can be used: heating meat strips in marinade before drying or heating the dried jerky
> strips in an oven after the drying process is completed. When the strips are heated in a
> marinade before drying, drying times will be reduced. »

> « The thickness of the meat strips will make a difference in the safety of the methods
> recommended in this book. Slice meat no thicker than ¼ inch. »

> « To heat, bring strips and marinade to a boil and boil for 5 minutes before draining and
> drying. If strips are more than ¼ inch thick, the length of time may need to be increased. »

> « Place the racks in a dehydrator or oven preheated to 140ºF. »

> « Dry until a test piece cracks but does not break when it is bent (10 to 24 hours for samples
> not heated in marinade). Samples heated in marinade will dry faster. Begin checking samples
> after 3 hours. »

> « Again, an internal temperature of 160ºF is necessary to eliminate disease-causing bacteria such
> as E. coli O157:H7, if present. »

## Conversions (faites par le vérificateur)

¼ po = **6 mm** ; 140 °F = **60 °C** ; 160 °F = **71 °C**.

## Ce que la page ne dit pas

Aucune durée à l'air libre, au froid ou à la fumée froide : les 10 à 24 heures valent pour des
lanières de 6 mm au séchoir ou au four à 60 °C.
