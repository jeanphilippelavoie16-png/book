## Extraits intégraux

### Résumé

> « Background: Normal healthy skin has potential of hydrogen (pH) range of 5.4-5.9 and a normal
> bacterial flora. Use of soap with high pH causes an increase in skin pH, which in turn causes an
> increase in dehydrative effect, irritability and alteration in bacterial flora. »

> « Materials and Methods: The samples of soaps and shampoos were collected from shops in the
> locality. […] Solution of each sample was made and pH was measured using pH meter. Results:
> Majority of the soaps have a pH within the range of 9-10. »

### Discussion

> « In a study done by Tyebkhan G, in which pH paper was used for measuring the pH of
> soaps/cleansers, it was found that the soaps commonly used by the population at large have a pH
> ranging between 7 and 9. […] But in our study, we have found that majority of the samples had a
> pH ranging between 9.01 and 11. […] The difference may be due to greater accuracy of the pH
> meter used in our study when compared to the pH paper. »

> « Even those products, which were promoted as anti-acne soaps had a pH above nine. »

## Notes de captation

**Ce que la source donne :** un savon vrai, du commerce, est **alcalin** : la plupart entre **9 et
10** au pH-mètre (jusqu'à 11 pour certains), et le papier pH lit plus bas que le pH-mètre (7 à 9
dans l'étude citée). Un savon à pH 9-10 n'est donc pas un savon raté : c'est un savon.

**Recoupement avec la fiche déjà au dossier** `89-cnesst-hydroxyde-de-sodium.md` (20 sept.) :
« pH : 11,5 pour solution 0,013 % (p/p) ». Une trace de soude libre suffit à dépasser 11.

**Ce que la source ne donne pas :** un seuil officiel au-delà duquel un savon est « caustique ».
La conduite qui en découle sans seuil inventé : tester la barre maison **à côté d'un savon du
commerce, sur le même papier, de la même façon**, et comparer.
