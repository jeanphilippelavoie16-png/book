## A. University of Maine Cooperative Extension — Bulletin #2751, *Starting Seeds at Home*

- **URL :** https://extension.umaine.edu/publications/2751e/
- **Page enregistrée :** `97-umaine-2751-semis-maison.html`
- **Mise à jour :** « Updated January 2021 by Marjorie Peronto; Frank Wertheim […] and Emma
  Fournier » ; « © 2008, 2021, 2023, 2024 »
- **Pourquoi le Maine :** « Last frost dates for most of Maine are in late May or early June » —
  la même fenêtre que la Côte-Nord (fiche ECCC 97).

### Extraits intégraux

> « When you grow your own seedlings, you can get a jump on Maine's short gardening season, and
> start enjoying flowers and harvesting vegetables four to six weeks earlier than if you had
> waited for the ground to warm up enough to sow the seeds outside. »

> « Seedlings may take from 4 to 12 weeks; the amount of time will usually be indicated on the
> seed package. To determine when to start seeds indoors, count back from the average last spring
> frost date for your area. »

> « Inexpensive bottom heat mats warm the growing medium to about 70 degrees Fahrenheit. […] In
> the absence of a bottom heat mat, find a warm spot in the house, like on top of a refrigerator
> or near a wood stove. Avoid putting the trays into direct sunlight at this time. The soil could
> get too hot and kill the seeds. »

> « As soon as about 50% of the seeds you planted begin to sprout, remove the tray from the
> source of bottom heat, take off the plastic covering, and move the seedlings to your
> supplemental light stand. »

Tableau « Time and temperature needed to grow vegetable transplants » (mot à mot, colonnes :
jours du semis à la levée | température optimale du sol °F | semaines de la levée au repiquage) :

> « Broccoli | 7–10 | 50–85 | 5–7 » · « Cabbage | 4–10 | 50–85 | 5–7 » · « Celery | 9–21 | 50–65 |
> 10–12 » · « Cucumber | 6–10 | 65–85 | 4 » · « Eggplant | 6–10 | 65–85 | 6–9 » · « Lettuce | 6–8 |
> 50–65 | 3–5 » · « Melons | 6–8 | 65–85 | 3–4 » · « Onion | 7–10 | 65–85 | 8 » · « Pepper | 9–14 |
> 65–85 | 6–8 » · « Squash | 4–6 | 65–85 | 3–4 » · « Tomato | 6–12 | 65–85 | 5–7 »

> « Most of us have tried to start seedlings in a sunny window here in the Northeast, and you may
> have noticed that oftentimes we end up with seedlings that are leggy — thin and angling reaching
> out for more sunlight. That is because our days here in the Northeastern US are still relatively
> short in late winter/early spring and we often have low light days that are cloudy or rainy.
> […] another potential problem with growing seedlings in a sunny window is that we often have
> very cold nighttime temperatures during late winter and early spring, exposing them to
> radiational cooling which ultimately stresses the plants. »

> « it is important to adjust the height of the lights to within 2-4 inches from the canopy of
> the growing seedlings »

> « For seed starting with basic fluorescent bulbs, it is generally recommended to run the lights
> for 16 hours a day. »

> « The ambient temperature for seed starting should range should ideally be about 55°F–70°F, so
> avoid drafty window sills. […] you could drape an inexpensive foil emergency space blanket over
> your frame. […] draping an insulating blanket over the frame will boost the temperature around
> the seedlings by about 5-10°F. »

> « Once the second set of true leaves appears, seedlings should be thinned to at least 1 inch
> apart or transplanted into individual pots or cells. When transplanting seedlings, handle them
> by the leaves only. Their stems are very delicate »

> « The young seedlings will need one to two weeks of hardening off before they go into the
> garden. […] Start by setting them outside for a few hours at a time in a protected, semi-shady
> location […] Outdoor temperatures should be 45°F or warmer, depending on the crop. Gradually
> increase the time outdoors and the exposure to direct sunlight. Transplant your seedlings to
> the garden in the late afternoon after the heat of the day has subsided, or on a cloudy day »

Conversions faites ici : 70 °F ≈ 21 °C ; 50-85 °F = 10 à 29 °C ; 65-85 °F = 18 à 29 °C ;
50-65 °F = 10 à 18 °C ; 2-4 po = 5 à 10 cm ; 55-70 °F = 13 à 21 °C ; 5-10 °F ≈ 3 à 6 °C ;
45 °F ≈ 7 °C ; 1 po ≈ 2,5 cm.

## B. University of Maine Cooperative Extension — *When does "days to maturity" start for plants?*

- **URL :** https://extension.umaine.edu/gardening/2025/09/25/when-does-days-to-maturity-start/
- **Page enregistrée :** `97-umaine-jours-a-maturite.html`
- **Date :** 25 septembre 2025 ; réponse de Jonathan Foster, Home Horticulture Outreach
  Professional

> « for direct-sown seeds in the garden (e.g., corn, peas, radishes), the count starts from
> germination […] For seeds started indoors, the count starts when they are transplanted out and
> begin actively growing the garden. »

> « be aware there aren't any real rules or regulations governing these estimates, and
> different companies handle it differently–it's helpful if the packet specifies »

> « Temperature, day length, precipitation, soil conditions, pollinator presence/absence, etc.,
> all play a huge part in when a plant matures to the harvest point. »

## D. South Dakota State University Extension — *Fall Frost Tolerance of Common Vegetables*

- **URL :** https://extension.sdstate.edu/fall-frost-tolerance-common-vegetables
- **Page enregistrée :** `97-sdsu-tolerance-gel-legumes.html`
- **Date :** « Updated September 07, 2023 » ; « Written by Rhoda Burrows »

> « Crops that came from warmer parts of the world cannot tolerate even a light frost, and may
> even begin to suffer at temperatures below 50 degrees Fahrenheit. »

> « pumpkins can withstand 35 degree Fahrenheit nights if daytime temperatures reach 70 degrees
> Fahrenheit or more. »

> « The following vegetables are not cold tolerant, and they should be protected from frost or
> harvested before even a light frost: Beans · Sweet Corn · Potatoes · Pepper, eggplant ·
> Cucurbits: Cucumbers, summer squash, melons (pumpkins and winter squash may be able to tolerate
> very light frosts, but it is better to protect them if possible). »

> « These vegetables also will not tolerate frost, but they will ripen further after picking […]
> Tomatoes: Must be at least "mature green" (changed from the deep-green to almost turning red).
> […] Pumpkins: If they are starting to turn color, they will continue to turn color. However,
> their keeping quality will generally not be as good »

> « Crops from more-temperate regions can withstand light freezes (28 to 32 degrees Fahrenheit):
> Beets · Carrot · Cauliflower · Lettuce · Onion, Garlic, Chives · Parsley · Peas · Radish ·
> Rhubarb · Spinach · Swiss chard »

> « Root crops develop more sugars when the soil temperature is below 40 degrees Fahrenheit »

> « Among the hardiest vegetables are cole crops, which can tolerate moderate freezes (24 to 28
> degrees Fahrenheit): Broccoli, cauliflower (26 degrees Fahrenheit) · Chinese cabbage, cabbage,
> kale, kohlrabi, turnip · For best quality, harvest after several moderate freezes (24-28
> degrees Fahrenheit): Parsnips, salsify · Brussels sprouts »

Conversions faites ici : 50 °F = 10 °C ; 35 °F ≈ 2 °C ; 70 °F ≈ 21 °C ; 28 à 32 °F ≈ -2 à 0 °C ;
24 à 28 °F ≈ -4 à -2 °C ; 26 °F ≈ -3 °C ; 40 °F ≈ 4 °C.

**Note :** la page classe la « potato » parmi les légumes sans tolérance : il s'agit du feuillage
(la page parle de protéger ou récolter avant le gel). Le tubercule en terre est protégé par la
terre ; le tubercule gelé montre des taches grises sous la pelure (fiche FAO, tableau 4.3).

## E. Iowa State University Extension — *Can I ripen green tomatoes indoors before frost?*

- **URL :** https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/faq/can-i-ripen-green-tomatoes-indoors-frost
- **Page enregistrée :** `97-isu-tomates-vertes.html`

> « Just before the first frost, remove all mature, greenish white fruit from the vines. They
> should be solid, firm, and free of defects. Remove the stems, then clean and dry the fruit.
> Individually wrap each tomato in a piece of newspaper. Store wrapped tomatoes in a cool (60 to
> 65 degrees Fahrenheit), dark location, such as a basement or cellar. […] Inspect the tomatoes
> frequently and discard any damaged or decaying fruit. When the tomatoes begin to color, remove
> the newspaper and place them at room temperature. An alternate method is to leave the green
> fruit on the vine and pull up the entire plant. Hang the tomato plant upside down in a cool,
> dark location. Pick the fruit as they ripen. »

Conversion : 60 à 65 °F ≈ 16 à 18 °C.
