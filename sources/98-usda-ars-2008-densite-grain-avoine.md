## Extraits intégraux

Résumé vulgarisé (« Interpretive Summary ») :

> « We have measured the density of oat kernels from their displacement of sand, in an application
> of Archimedes Principle. We determined the density of oat kernels to be about 1.00 g/cm3. [...]
> We dehulled the oat grains and measured the densities of the oat groats and hulls. Oat groats had
> densities of about 1.29 g/cm3 and hulls had about half that density. »

Résumé technique :

> « Grain envelope volumes ranged from 31 to 38 mm3 and were highly correlated with grain mass.
> Grain densities ranged from 0.96 to 1.03 g/cm3. [...] smaller grains within an oat sample packed
> more efficiently than larger grains and larger grains in a sample were less dense than the smaller
> grains. Analysis of oat grain components indicated groat densities were ≈1.29 g/cm3 and hull
> densities were ≈0.69 g/cm3. »

## Lecture pour le livre

- Le grain d'avoine vêtu, balle comprise (volume d'enveloppe, air de la balle compris), pèse
  **autant que l'eau** : 0,96 à 1,03 g/cm³, les gros grains plus légers que les petits. Une saumure
  est plus lourde que l'eau : l'avoine vêtue y **flotte**, avec l'ergot. La saumure ne trie pas
  l'avoine vêtue.
- L'avoine décortiquée (le gruau, 1,29 g/cm³) coule ; la balle (0,69 g/cm³) flotte.
- L'ergot touche rarement l'avoine (Saskatchewan) : pour elle, le tri se fait au drap blanc.
