## Trailing Wounded Game — extrait intégral

> « You should wait for at least a half-hour to an hour before trailing a deer, unless the downed
> deer is in sight. »

> « Once at the site of the shot, look for signs:
> Blood on the ground or vegetation
> Broken twigs or branches, or scattered leaves
> A "dew" line if early in the morning
> Tracks
> Hair, meat, or bone fragments
> Downhill trails, especially toward water
> If you lose a trail, search in a circular or grid pattern, and try to pick up the trail again. »

> « Use fluorescent orange flagging to mark the blood trail in case darkness or weather forces you
> to quit the search and return the next day. Marking the blood trail also shows where to look for
> more signs if you lose the trail. »

> « Hunter's Tip — A clean kill improves the flavor of game meat. A wounded animal that has to be
> chased down yields strong-flavored meat because waste products, produced by stress, accumulate
> in the flesh. »

## Still Hunting — extrait intégral

> « As the name implies, still hunting is walking stealthily through an animal's habitat, stopping
> frequently—sometimes for long periods—to scan and listen for game. »

> « As a general rule, spend at least 10 times longer being still and observing than walking. Keep
> a low profile; a human silhouette will spook many game species. Use binoculars in open terrain to
> identify movement properly. »

## Stalking — extrait intégral

> « The difference between still hunting and stalking is that when stalking, you follow signs
> leading to a particular type of game or group of animals, or close the distance to game already
> spotted.
> You may follow tracks on trails or a morning "dew" trail through leaves and brush. […]
> Stalking requires total focus because you must remember to keep downwind, stay quiet, stay alert,
> and remain patient. »

## Driving — extrait intégral

> « The purpose of the deer drive is to make the deer move, not run, out of their hiding places.
> Driving involves a group of hunters, some acting as "drivers" and others as "posters."
> Drivers spread out across a field or woods and push game out of cover.
> Posters take positions at the end of the cover to intercept game pushed out by the drivers.
> The success of a drive depends on good organization and being familiar with the terrain.
> It is critical that everyone involved in the drive is aware of the position of other drivers and
> posters. […] never shoot in the direction of another hunter. »

## Approaching Downed Game — extrait

> « A downed deer or other large animal should be approached carefully from above and behind the
> head. If the animal appears to be dead, wait a short distance away for a few minutes. Watch for
> any rise and fall of the chest cavity. »

## Ce que le chapitre en tire

- **Au moins une demi-heure à une heure** (plancher général), sauf bête à terre en vue.
- **La ligne de rosée** du petit matin comme piste sans sang (§5-A, §9).
- **Dix fois plus longtemps immobile qu'en marche** (§5-A, l'approche).
- **Sous le vent, en silence** (§5-A).
- **La pousse : faire bouger, pas courir** ; posteurs au bout du couvert (§5-C, à deux).
- **La bête poussée longtemps donne une viande au goût fort** : c'est le premier maillon du
  mécanisme de §3 (voir `35d-bcrc-coupe-sombre-stress.md` pour le mécanisme complet).
