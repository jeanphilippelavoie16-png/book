## Extraits intégraux

> « Among domestic livestock, pigs are most commonly associated with Trichinella infection, but
> human outbreaks have also resulted from consumption of horsemeat, wild boar, bear, walrus and
> other wild animals. »

> « The International Commission on Trichinellosis recognizes three (3) acceptable means of
> treatment to render potentially Trichinella-infected meats safe for consumption: 1) cooking, 2)
> freezing (for meat from domestic pigs), and 3) irradiation. »

> « Freezing may not eliminate risk posed by species of Trichinella that have higher tolerance to
> cold treatment, including species (Trichinella nativa, Trichinella genotype T-6) commonly found
> in wild animals, and freezing, therefore, is not a reliable method for control of Trichinella in
> these animals. »

> « The successful inactivation of Trichinella larvae by means of curing depends on the type of
> raw meat product (semi-dry and dry sausages, ham) as well as the specific processing factors
> (e.g. salt concentration, ripening conditions) under which the raw meat product undergoes the
> curing procedure (FSIS, 2018). »

> « There are currently no curing models that can be used to correlate the parameters of meat
> chemistry (water activity, brine concentration, pH) with time and temperature conditions that
> can reliably predict inactivation of Trichinella spp. Therefore, ICT does not recommend curing,
> salting and smoking processes for control of Trichinella in pork, horse or game meats, unless the
> specific conditions used in the process has been properly validated to be effective. »

> « Methods that are not reliable for inactivating Trichinella in meats include: • Cooking using
> microwaves • Curing (that has not been validated as described above), salting, drying, or
> smoking Education of hunters and others for proper preparation of game meats should follow the
> same recommendations issued to consumers. »

> « Irradiation is recommended only for sealed packaged foods. »

## Ce que la source établit pour le chapitre 77

- **Trois moyens seulement** sont reconnus : cuisson, gel (porc domestique seulement),
  irradiation (emballages scellés, hors de portée). Pour l'ours et le phoque, il reste **la
  cuisson**.
- La **fermentation n'est pas nommée mot pour mot**, mais elle n'est pas parmi les moyens
  reconnus, et la source dit en toutes lettres qu'**aucun modèle ne relie le pH** (avec
  l'activité de l'eau et la saumure) à l'inactivation du parasite. Les saucissons secs et
  demi-secs — des produits fermentés — sont traités comme des procédés de salaison à valider un
  par un, sur le porc.
- Le gel ne vaut pas pour les espèces des animaux sauvages (*T. nativa*).

## Ce que la source NE dit PAS

- Pas de mention explicite de la fermentation lactique de légumes ou de viande crue « à la
  manière » traditionnelle.
- Les températures de cuisson données (63 à 71 °C) visent le porc ; le livre garde ses 74 °C
  pour le gibier.
