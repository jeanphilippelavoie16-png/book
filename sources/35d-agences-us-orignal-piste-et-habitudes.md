détails de reconnaissance et d'horaire qui se recoupent.

## 1. Alaska Department of Fish and Game — Moose Species Profile

- **URL :** https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/index.cfm?adfg=moose.printerfriendly

> « Moose Tracks — Two crescents shaped halves with two dew claws. The tips leave a deeper
> impression in snow or on soft ground. »

> « Moose Scat — Depends on the season and food source. Pellet shape is directly related to the
> moisture content in the food. In winter, pellets are hard and dry. »

## 2. Alaska Department of Fish and Game — Spotting Alaskan Game

- **URL :** https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/index.cfm?adfg=hunting.spottinggame

> « Game animals are most commonly active in the low light of early morning and late evening. »

> « Binoculars help find game at long distances, but remember you also need them to pick out a
> moose's ear in a willow thicket at 30 yards. »

## 3. Maine Department of Inland Fisheries and Wildlife — Moose

- **URL :** https://www.maine.gov/ifw/fish-wildlife/wildlife/species-information/mammals/moose.html

> « the front hoof width is about 5 inches for a prime bull, just under 4 inches for a cow, and just
> under 3 inches for a calf. »

> « the forest cover also offers shade for bedding and ruminating during summer. The numerous
> wetlands, rivers, ponds, and lakes are valuable areas for moose during the growing season »

## 4. Minnesota Department of Natural Resources — Moose

- **URL :** https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/mammals/moose.html

> « Moose have rather poor eyesight, but acute senses of smell and hearing. »

> « Rutting or mating season occurs from mid-September to mid-October. […] The calves remain with
> their mothers for 12 to 18 months. »

## 5. National Park Service — Isle Royale National Park, Moose

- **URL :** https://www.nps.gov/isro/learn/nature/moose.htm

> « In the summer, it is common to see them feeding in inland lakes and beaver ponds. This helps
> them stay cool, plus they like to eat the aquatic plants that grow beneath the surface. If not in
> water, they tend to stay in the cool shaded forests and bogs, especially during the hottest parts
> of the day. They are most active during dawn and dusk. »
