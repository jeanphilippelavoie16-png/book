Spruce Grouse - Falcipennis canadensis
The male spruce grouse has gray and brown feathers speckled with white, a red comb over his eyes, a black throat, and a black chest with a white border.
The female spruce grouse is a mottled grayish-brown with white bars and spots. She has no comb.
Most of the spruce grouse's diet is made up of the needles and buds of conifer trees like jack pine, juniper, spruce, larch, and lodge-pole pine.
During mating season, the male spruce grouse struts around ruffling his feathers and beating his wings.
The spruce grouse is also known as the "Fool Hen" because it is so tame you can walk right up to it and pick it up. 
Characteristics 
The spruce grouse looks a little bit like a chicken. It is 15-17 inches (38-43 cm) in length with a wingspan of  21.5–22.6 inches (54.6–57.4 cm). Males are a bit larger than females.
The male has gray and brown feathers speckled with white, a red comb over his eyes, a black throat, and a black chest with a white border. The female is a mottled grayish-brown with white bars and spots. She has no comb.
Habitat 
The spruce grouse lives in coniferous forests, especially those with spruce and pine trees.
Diet 
Most of the spruce grouse’s diet is made up of the needles and buds of conifer trees like jack pine, juniper, spruce, larch, and lodge-pole pine. It is often found in trees, plucking needles off branches.
In the summer, it may also eat berries, seeds, mushrooms, and insects. The spruce grouse also eats small stones to help break down food in its gizzard. The gizzard is a strong, muscular organ in a bird’s digestive tract that grinds up food the bird eats.
In the winter, it only eats the needles of conifer trees. To make up for the shift in its winter diet, the spruce grouse’s gizzard becomes larger, and its digestive tract becomes about 40% longer.
Life Cycle 
During mating season, the male struts around ruffling his feathers and beating his wings. Sometimes he flies just above the ground, flapping his wings to attract a female.
The female lays 4-11 eggs in a hollow lined with moss, grass, and leaves. The nest is usually built under low-lying branches of a spruce tree. The chicks hatch after about 3 weeks and fledge (develop flight feathers) at 10 days old.
The spruce grouse has a lifespan of 5-7 years in the wild.
Behavior 
The spruce grouse is also known as the “Fool Hen” because it is so tame you can walk right up to it and pick it up.

The Spruce Grouse in NH 
The spruce grouse was once widely found in New Hampshire, but by 1880, they were rarely seen due to the loss of spruce and fir forests from logging.
They are now found in small numbers in northern New Hampshire.
 IUCN Redlist of Threatened Species
 World Status: Least Concern

Range 
The spruce grouse is found in most parts of Canada, except for the extreme north.
In the United States, it is found in Washington, Idaho, Montana, Wyoming, Michigan, and Northern New England.
