# Stefansson 1936 — *Adventures in Diet* : d'où vient le « 2 à 3 semaines »

- **Source :** Vilhjalmur Stefansson, « Adventures in Diet », *Harper's Monthly Magazine*,
  novembre et décembre 1935, janvier 1936 ; tiré à part avec les déclarations de Karsten
  Andersen et du Dr Clarence W. Lieb. Numérisation de l'Internet Archive.
- **URL :** https://archive.org/details/adventuresindiet00stef_0 (texte intégral :
  https://archive.org/stream/adventuresindiet00stef_0/adventuresindiet00stef_0_djvu.txt)
- **Consultée le :** 24 septembre 2026 (déjà captée le 23 sous
  `sources/51-stefansson-1936-adventures-in-diet.html`)
- **Page enregistrée :** `sources/24-stefansson-1936-adventures-in-diet.html`
- **Nature :** récit de première main d'un explorateur, avec l'expérience de l'hôpital Bellevue
  (New York, 1928) sous supervision médicale. Pas une source clinique moderne ; c'est la source
  d'origine du délai qui circule.
- **Chapitres :** 24 (§8, règle 1 ; §7), 70 (note [^1]).

## Extraits intégraux (coquilles de l'OCR corrigées entre crochets)

> « I had published in 1913, on pages 140-142 of My Life with the Eskimo, an account of how some
> natives and I became ill when we had to go two or three weeks on lean meat, caribou so
> [skinny] that there was no appreciable fat behind the eyes or in the marrow. »

> « But he countered by citing my own experience where illness had not come until after two or
> three weeks, and he now proposed lean for only two or three days. »

> « As said, in the Arctic we had become ill during the second or third fatless week. I now
> became ill on the second day. The time difference between Bellevue and the Arctic was due no
> doubt mainly to the existence of a little fat, here and there, in our northern caribou — we had
> eaten the tissue from behind the eyes, we had broken the bones for marrow, and in doing
> everything we could to get fat we had evidently secured more than we realized. At Bellevue the
> meat, carefully scrutinized, had been as lean as such muscle tissue well can be. »

> « The symptoms brought on at Bellevue by an incomplete meat diet (lean without fat) were exactly
> the same as in the Arctic, except that they came on faster — diarrhoea and a feeling of general
> baffling discomfort. Up north the Eskimos and I had been cured immediately when we got some
> fat. Dr. DuBois now cured me the same way, by giving me fat sirloin steaks, brains fried in
> bacon fat, and things of that sort. In two or three days I was all right but I had lost
> considerable weight. »

Pour la mort, la seule phrase trouvée est d'un autre texte de Stefansson (1944, p. 234), citée
par Speth et Spielmann 1983 (`sources/21-speth-spielmann-1983-stefansson-mal-de-caribou.md`) :

> « Diarrhoea will start in from a week to 10 days and will not be relieved unless you secure
> fat. Death will result after several weeks. »

## Lecture

1. **« 2 à 3 semaines » est le délai avant de tomber malade**, dans l'Arctique, sur du caribou
   qui gardait un peu de gras (derrière les yeux, dans les os). Ce n'est pas un délai de mort.
2. **Sur une viande vraiment maigre, le même homme est tombé malade le deuxième jour.** Plus la
   viande est maigre, plus c'est rapide.
3. Le gras a guéri en deux ou trois jours, avec une perte de poids « considérable ».
4. Aucune source consultée ne chiffre le délai de la mort ; la seule dit « after several weeks ».
