## Préparer (ch. 9, *What to bring*, *Clean and sterilize tools*, *Clean the space*)

- Poche des eaux : « If labor does not start within 6 hours after the bag breaks, there is a risk
  of infection. [...] If labor has not started in 24 hours (1 day and 1 night) after the water
  breaks, bring the woman to a medical center. »
- *What to bring* : « Clean gloves (or very clean plastic bags) to wear whenever you touch the
  mother's genitals, the baby, or any blood or stool » ; « Many very clean cloths or rags » ;
  « Heat to boil water » ; « Bowls for washing and for the placenta » ; « Watch with a second
  hand or timer ». « All of the tools that go inside the vagina or cut the skin must be sterile.
  This includes gloves, razor or scissors for cutting the cord ». « be sure your nails are
  clipped short. »
- Stériliser au four : « This can be used for metal tools and string for tying the cord. Do not
  bake tools made of rubber or plastic » ; « wrap them in 4 layers of clean cloth or heavy paper
  and tie it shut » ; « **Bake at 170ºC (340ºF) for 1 hour or 150ºC (305ºF) for 2½ hours.** »
- Stériliser à la vapeur sous pression : « If you have a pressure-cooking pot, you can sterilize
  your tools in the same way that an autoclave does » ; « cook at 15 to 20 pounds of pressure at
  **121ºC for 20 minutes**. »
- Désinfecter en faisant bouillir : « boil for **20 minutes**. Start counting the 20 minutes when the
  water starts to boil. » La page distingue *disinfecting* (« kills most of the germs ») et
  *sterilizing* (« kills all the germs »). « any tools that will be used to cut the cord at birth
  must be sterilized » ; « Using tools that have been disinfected (but not sterilized) to cut into
  the body puts the person at risk of infection, and should be done only when their life is in
  danger. » Rangement : « A metal box or pot with a tight-fitting lid is best for storage. »
  « germs grow in moisture, and they will come back if the instruments are put away while they
  are wet. »
- Lames : « Try not to reuse razor blades — but if you do, sterilize them first. »
- Gants : « Most plastic gloves can be boiled or steamed » ; retourner le poignet, ne toucher que
  l'intérieur.
- *Clean the space* : « Move animals out of the house » ; sous-matelassage : « Cloth rags or sacks
  can be boiled and then dried. » Literie : sécher au soleil ou au fer ; « Do not store bedding that
  is damp or wet. »

## Le travail (ch. 11, *Care for the mother during labor*, *Signs for the midwife... stage 1*)

- « Help her drink at least 1 cup of liquid each hour » ; « Have the woman urinate at least once
  every 2 hours » ; « Change position every hour ». « A full bladder can also cause pain, problems
  with pushing out the placenta, and bleeding after childbirth. » Si elle a faim : « it is good for
  her to eat ». Boisson de réhydratation : « no saltier than tears » (recettes données).
- Eaux : « they should be clear or a little pink [...] yellow or green waters are a warning sign.
  They mean the baby has probably passed stool inside the womb. » « the baby should be born within
  one day and one night (24 hours) after the waters break. This means that labor should start
  within 12 hours after the waters break. » Température aux 4 heures si les eaux sont rompues.
- Fièvre : « a fever above 38˚C (100.4˚F) is usually a sign of infection. »
- « Labor is too long when strong contractions last more than 12 hours. »
- Cordon procident : sortie du cordon, pouls du bébé qui chute ; « put the mother in the knee-chest
  position with her hips up and hold the baby's head off the cord » ; « try to put it back in so it
  will stay warm and wet (if you cannot get it back in, wrap it in a clean cloth). »
- Éclampsie : « Do not put anything in the mother's mouth » ; « Put the mother on her side, so she
  does not breathe in her spit or vomit. » Tension de 140/90 ou plus = signe d'alerte.

## La poussée (*What happens during stage 2*, *Watch for warning signs*, *Help the mother give birth*)

- Couronnement : « When the baby's head stretches the vaginal opening to about the size of the
  palm of your hand, the head will stay at the opening — even between contractions. »
- « First babies may take a full 2 or even 3 hours of strong contractions and good pushing to be
  born. Second and later babies often take less than 1 hour of pushing. » « If you do not see the
  mother's genitals bulging after 30 minutes of strong pushing [...] the head may not be coming
  down. If the baby is not moving down at all after 1 hour of pushing, the mother needs help. »
  Causes listées : peur, épuisement, vessie pleine, position ; « The position that usually works
  best is squatting » ; « she can hold on to a door knob or a rope tied to the ceiling, and pull
  down as she pushes. »
- « **Never push on the mother's belly to hurry the birth.** Pushing on the belly can make the
  placenta separate from the womb, or tear the womb. »
- Linges chauds : « Boil a pot of water for 2 minutes [...] If you do not have a disinfectant, add
  a little salt to the water. Let the water cool down before you use it. The water should be warm,
  not hot. »
- Ralentir la tête : « tell her to blow hard and fast » ; « Each small push should move the head no
  more than 1 centimeter farther out of the mother. » Ne pas ralentir si : jet de sang avant la
  naissance, cordon procident, cœur du bébé très lent.
- Bouche et nez : essuyer au linge sur le doigt, « You do not need to suction » ; aspirer
  seulement s'il y a du méconium.
- Cordon autour du cou : lâche, le passer par-dessus la tête ; serré, essayer ; sinon « keep the
  head close against the mother's thigh, and let the baby's body somersault out around the
  head ». « It is very rare that a tight cord would prevent a baby from being born. »
- Épaules : attendre la contraction suivante ; tête guidée vers le bas pour l'épaule du dessus,
  vers le haut pour l'autre ; « Guide the head — do not pull it. »
- Épaules coincées : signes (tête qui rentre « like a turtle ») ; « After about 5 minutes, the
  blood vessels in the baby's brain may begin to break » ; quatre méthodes dans l'ordre :
  quatre pattes et traction droite en comptant jusqu'à 30 ; genoux ramenés et « press hard just
  above the mother's pubic bone — not on the mother's belly » ; pousser l'épaule de l'intérieur ;
  sortir le bras arrière. « Never jerk on the baby's neck ».
- Après : « Dry the baby immediately [...] put the baby on her mother's belly, skin to skin » ;
  couvrir, tête couverte ; « a baby should stay skin to skin with her mother for at least an hour ».
- Piège à mucosités : un petit pot, un bouchon percé de deux trous, deux tubes fins ; « It should go
  no more than 10 centimeters (4 inches) down the baby's throat » ; stériliser le tube.
- Cordon : « Now the cord can be cut, usually after about 3 minutes. (Some people wait until the
  placenta is born before cutting the cord. This is a healthy custom.) » Nouer « about 2 finger
  widths from the baby's belly » ; nœud plat (« Right over left, left over right ») ; « Anything
  that is sharp enough to cut the cord will work, as long as it has been sterilized ». « Do not put
  dirt or dung on the cord stump ».

## Le bébé à la naissance (*What to do for the baby: The birth of the placenta*)

- « Babies should start to breathe on their own within 1 or 2 minutes after birth. »
- Signes de détresse : narines qui s'ouvrent, peau entre les côtes qui se creuse, plus de 60 ou
  moins de 30 respirations par minute, grognement. « Never hit or hurt a baby or hold her upside
  down to make her cry. »
- Réanimation : « A baby who is not breathing at all one half minute after birth, even with firm
  back massage [...] needs help immediately. » Sur une surface ferme, au chaud, linge roulé sous les
  épaules, tête droite ; « Put your mouth over the baby's mouth and nose » ; « Breathe into the baby
  using only as much air as you can easily hold in your cheeks. Do not blow. [...] Give 3 to 5 slow
  breaths to start » ; « Breathe about 30 breaths every minute » ; « If the baby does not breathe on
  her own after 20 minutes of rescue breathing, she will probably not be able to. » « cover a baby's
  mouth with gauze or a very thin piece of cloth ».
  **Écart retenu :** le livre suit l'OMS (40 par minute, 30 à 50 acceptables) plutôt que les
  30 par minute de Hesperian.
- Cœur : « between 120 and 160 times a minute » ; compter 6 secondes et multiplier par 10 ; « slower
  than 100 beats a minute [...] give rescue breathing. »
- Couleur : « if a baby's whole body stays pale or blue for more than 5 minutes, there may be a
  problem ».
- Colostrum ; « Breastfeeding makes the womb contract. This helps the placenta come out, and it
  helps prevent heavy bleeding. »

## Le placenta et le saignement (ch. 13, *Check the mother's physical signs*)

- Stade 3 : « usually lasts less than 1 hour ».
- « Most bleeding after birth comes from the place where the placenta was attached. [...] When the
  placenta is born, the womb must contract quickly to prevent heavy bleeding. » « Even a small piece
  of placenta left inside the womb can keep it open and bleeding. »
- Masser : « put your hand on top of the womb and squeeze while you move the same hand in a circle
  [...] Check the womb every 1 or 2 minutes for a while. If it gets soft again, rub it until it
  contracts again. Teach the mother and a family member how to check the womb and rub it ».
- Trois formes d'hémorragie : rapide, filet continu, cachée (« the top of her womb may rise in the
  belly as it fills with blood »).
- Signes de décollement : « A small gush of blood [...] A gush is a handful of blood », « More of the
  cord has come out », « The womb rises ». À 30 minutes sans signe : faire téter, faire uriner.
  « If the placenta does not come by itself after an hour, or if the mother is bleeding heavily,
  help her deliver it » : assise ou accroupie au-dessus d'un bol, pousser ; membranes torsadées.
- « Pulling on the cord is dangerous! If the placenta is still attached to the womb, the cord may
  break or you may pull the woman's womb out of her body. If the womb is pulled out, the mother may
  die. » Inversion utérine : « if someone pulls on the cord before the placenta has separated [...]
  or if someone pushes on the womb to get the placenta out. »
- Examen du placenta : côté bébé lisse, vaisseaux « like tree roots » ; côté mère en lobes à
  rassembler dans les mains ; vaisseaux qui partent du bord « and going nowhere » = morceau
  resté ; cordon à « 3 holes — 1 large hole and 2 small holes » ; « Some babies with 2-vessel cords
  have problems later on. »
- Placenta : l'enterrer profond « to keep animals from digging it up », ou le brûler.
- Utérus mou : vessie pleine, morceau resté, manque d'ocytocine ou de stimulation, infection. Faire
  téter : « When the baby sucks, the mother's body makes its own oxytocin » ; ou masser les
  mamelons.
- Plantes : « If you know of herbs or plants that stop bleeding and are safe, you can give those
  now. Do not put any herbs or plants in the vagina. » (Le livre ne donne aucune plante : ch. 33.)
- Compression dans le vagin (bimanuelle) : main gantée rendue petite, poing au fond du vagin
  au-dessus du col, « Do not put your hand in the womb » ; l'autre main tient l'utérus par le
  ventre, le ramène vers le poing et serre.
- Compression abdominale : « Place your fist just above and next to the mother's bellybutton
  (navel), on the mother's left side. With your left hand, find the pulse in the mother's thigh.
  Straighten your right arm and push down hard [...] until you no longer feel the mother's pulse in
  her thigh. »
- Déchirure : saignement avec utérus dur ; « Roll up 10 to 15 pieces of sterile gauze or another
  small, sterile cloth into a thick pad and push it firmly against the bleeding part ».
- Choc : faible, étourdie, confuse ; peau pâle, sueurs froides ; « fast pulse, over 100 beats a
  minute, that feels thin and faint » ; respiration rapide. Pieds plus hauts que la tête, tête sur
  le côté, au chaud, à boire si consciente, rien par la bouche si inconsciente.
- Misoprostol (encadrés) : avant la sortie du placenta, 400 à 600 µg ; après, 800 µg, sous la langue
  30 minutes ou par voie rectale. **Écart retenu :** le livre prend les doses de l'OMS 2012
  (600 µg par la bouche en prévention, 800 µg sous la langue en traitement).

## Les premières heures (ch. 14)

- « After birth, most women bleed the same amount as a heavy monthly bleeding [...] The blood comes
  out in little spurts when the womb contracts, or when the mother coughs, moves, or stands up. »
- « Check it just after the placenta is born. Then check it every 5 or 10 minutes for 1 hour. For
  the next 1 to 2 hours, check it every 15 to 30 minutes. » « **500 ml (about 2 cups) is too
  much.** »
- Déchirure non cousue : « it can probably heal without sewing. Ask her to rest in bed for 2 weeks
  with her legs together most of the time. She should move her legs regularly, but she should not
  climb up or down steps or steep hills. Someone else should do the cooking and cleaning ».
- Uriner : « Ask her to urinate within the first 2 to 3 hours » ; accroupie sur un bol ou dans un
  linge épais couchée ; « pour clean, warm water over her genitals while she tries. »
- Infection : « a fast pulse or a temperature above 38°C (100.4°F), or her belly feels sore when
  touched. Infection is more likely if her waters broke early in labor, if labor was long ».
- La mère sans intérêt pour son bébé : malade, saigne, épuisée ou déprimée.

## Les premières semaines (ch. 15)

- Utérus : « Just after birth, the top of the womb is about halfway between the pubic bone and the
  bellybutton. The next day, it is at the height of the bellybutton, and should be a bit lower every
  day after that. »
- « Working too much often causes bleeding after a birth. »
- Infection de l'utérus : fièvre 38 °C ou plus, frissons, pouls rapide, saignement abondant, odeur,
  douleur au ventre ; « Womb infections are very dangerous — they can quickly kill a woman. »
  Traitement : antibiotiques (le livre ne les donne pas : ch. 32). « If the woman does not start to
  feel better within 1 or 2 days, she may have pieces of the placenta still in her womb. »
- Caillot dans la jambe : enflure ou chaleur d'une jambe, douleur à la pression ou à la marche,
  zone rouge, bosse dure ; « have the woman lie down with her legs above her hips and try to stay
  still. Put warm cloths on the swollen area, but do not rub or massage it. »

## L'allaitement (ch. 16, *How to breastfeed*, *Common difficulties*, *Alternatives*)

- « The more a baby suckles, the more milk a mother's breasts will make. »
- Crevasses : « the baby is probably not in a good position » ; « The mother can rub breast milk
  into her nipple » ; « leave her breasts open to air » ; « If the pain is too great to
  breastfeed, the mother can remove her milk by hand and feed the baby with a cup and spoon for a
  few days. » « urinates more than 6 times a day ».
- Mastite (Hesperian conseille la chaleur et le massage de la bosse ; **écart retenu** : le livre
  suit l'INSPQ 2025, froid et massage léger seulement).
- Alternatives : « Someone else may be able to breastfeed the baby » ; lait animal : « **mix 100 ml
  fresh milk with 50 ml clean water and 10 g (2 tsp) sugar** » (vache, chèvre) ; « Bring the formula
  to a boil and then remove it from the heat. Let it cool and then feed immediately. » « The cup,
  spoon, bottle [...] should be washed thoroughly and boiled for 20 minutes before each use. »
  « should never be left at room temperature for more than 2 hours. » « Animal milks do not have
  all the vitamins a growing baby needs ». « Dirty bottles and nipples or watered-down formula can
  kill a baby. »

## Siège (*Baby is breech*)

- « Breech births can go well, but they are often dangerous for the baby » ; siège complet ou
  décomplété : ne pas pousser avant que le col soit complètement ouvert ; « The baby's bottom and
  belly will usually be born without any help » ; envelopper le bébé : « If the baby gets cold, she
  may try to take a breath inside the mother » ; tenir « by the hips or below » ; « Do not pull
  hard! Do not bend the neck » ; tête lente, menton fléchi. Siège par les pieds : « very high chance
  of prolapsed cord ».

## Seule (*Emergency care for problems after miscarriage or abortion*)

- « If a woman is bleeding and she is by herself with no one to help her [...] Help the womb
  contract by rubbing the lower belly very hard while lying down or squatting. » La page dit que
  c'est probablement insuffisant, mais que ça peut ralentir.

## Misoprostol (*Alphabetical list of medicines*)

- « **Never use misoprostol to speed up or start a labor. It could cause the womb to tear.** »
- Effets secondaires : « Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, headache. If the woman is breastfeeding,
  misoprostol will cause diarrhea in infants. »

### Le cœur du bébé, la position, les jumeaux (*Check the baby*, *Signs for the midwife... stage 1*)

- « By the last 2 months, you can often hear the baby's heartbeat in a quiet room by putting your
  ear on the mother's belly. [...] You can make a simple fetoscope from wood, clay, or a hollow tube
  of bamboo. »
- « It may sound like a watch ticking under a pillow, only faster. The baby's heartbeat is about
  twice as fast as a healthy adult heartbeat — usually 120 to 160 beats a minute. » « If you hear a
  "swishy" sound [...] you are probably hearing the baby's pulse in the cord [...] they do not help
  you find the baby's position. » « If the heartbeat sounds slow, you are probably hearing the
  mother's pulse [...] feel the mother's pulse in her wrist while you listen. »
- « If you hear the heartbeat loudest below the mother's bellybutton, the baby is probably head
  down. If you hear the heartbeat loudest above the mother's bellybutton, the baby may be breech. »
- « If the whole back moves when you move the top end, the baby is probably in a head-down position.
  If the back stays where it is while you move the upper part of the baby, you may be moving the head
  [...] the baby is breech. » « Do the ends of the baby seem to be in the mother's sides? If so, the
  baby is probably lying sideways. Many babies lie sideways in the first months but most turn head
  down by 8 months or so. »
- Jumeaux, signes : « The womb grows faster or larger than you expect. You can feel 2 heads or 2
  bottoms [...] You can hear 2 heartbeats. » Deux personnes : « Each of you can tap the rhythm of the
  heartbeat with your hand. If the rhythms are the same, you may be listening to the same baby. »
  « Since twins are more likely to be born early, the mother should try to have transportation ready
  at all times after the 6th month. » (Le livre transpose : la préparation de 5-D dès le 6e mois.)
- Pendant le travail : « Listen to the baby's heartbeat about 1 time every hour [...] The best time
  [...] is soon after a contraction stops. » « If you cannot hear the baby's heartbeat during a
  contraction, it usually does not mean the heart has stopped. » « At times, the heart may be as fast
  as 180 [...] or as slow as 100 [...] it should quickly come back [...] at the end of a contraction or
  with a change in the mother's position. » « WARNING SIGNS Baby's heartbeat is slower than 100 [...]
  or faster than 180. » « Listen to the baby's heartbeat right after the waters break. If [it] drops
  below 100 [...] get medical help. » « Finding the place where the heartbeat is loudest can help you
  know if the baby is head down, breech (bottom down), or sideways. »

### Saignements pendant la grossesse et le travail (*Signs... stage 1*, *Watch for warning signs*)

- Placenta prævia : « If a mother is bleeding and has no pain between contractions, she may have
  placenta previa [...] A woman with placenta previa can bleed to death very fast once the cervix is
  open. » « **Never do a vaginal exam if there is unusual bleeding.** You could poke a hole in the
  placenta with your finger and make the bleeding much worse. »
- Décollement : « The mother has pain between contractions [...] The womb is hard between
  contractions, or hard all the time. The mother's belly is sore and tender to the touch [...]
  sometimes no blood comes out [...] The baby's heartbeat can be very fast [...] or very slow. »
- Utérus déchiré : « very bad pain between contractions, then a tearing feeling in her belly, then
  less pain. The mother's contractions stop. [...] The baby feels loose (and sometimes higher) in the
  belly and has no heartbeat. » (*Watch for warning signs* : « her contractions will stop ».)
- Poussée : « a gush of fresh blood can be a sign of a detached placenta or a torn womb » ; décollement
  près de la naissance : « have the mother push as long and as hard as she can ».
- Accroupie : « The position that usually works best is squatting. Squatting opens the pelvis, and
  uses gravity to help the baby move down. » (Sert au § 9-D, étape 4.)

### Épaules (*Help the mother give birth*)

- « After about 5 minutes, the blood vessels in the baby's brain may begin to break and bleed from
  the pressure. » (Confirme l'étape 43.)
- « Cup your hands around the baby's head (do not hold the baby's neck) and gently pull straight
  back while counting to 30. » « **Never jerk on the baby's neck, or bend it too far. You could tear
  the baby's nerves.** »
- Cinquième méthode : « If none of these methods work, and you are far from a hospital, it may be
  better to break the baby's collarbone to help him out than to let him die. [...] reach in with your
  finger, hook the baby's collarbone, pull up toward the baby's head, and break it. You will need to
  use a lot of pressure. » (La page ajoute « only if you have been trained » : écarté, règle « il n'y
  a personne à appeler ».)

### Siège (*Baby is breech*)

- « If the cord gets pinched between the baby's head and the mother's pelvis, lack of air can cause
  brain injury or death. » « If they are not coming, put your fingers inside the mother and gently
  pull down the legs. Do not pull on the baby. » « Loosen the cord a little by gently pulling a bit of
  it out of the vagina. » « Pressure on the baby's back or belly can injure her insides. »
- Bras : « put your fingers inside the mother's vagina to bring the arm out [...] Pull the arm across
  the chest by pulling gently on the elbow. »
- Tête dernière : « The baby must now turn to face the mother's bottom. Hold the baby with your arm,
  and put one finger in the baby's mouth. Put your other hand on the baby's shoulders, with one finger
  on the back of the baby's head to keep it tucked in. » « Lower the baby until you see the hair on the
  back of her head. Do not pull hard! Do not bend the neck — it can break! » « raise the body to deliver
  the face. Let the back of the head stay inside » ; « If it comes too fast, the baby could bleed in the
  brain ».
- Par les pieds : « If you cannot get to a hospital, keep the mother from pushing until you are sure
  that the cervix is fully open. Ask the mother to lie down — the cord may be less likely to prolapse.
  Use the instructions for delivering a frank or complete breech. »

### Jumeaux (*Delivering twins*)

- Dangers : « Twins are more likely to be born early [...] The cord (especially of the second twin) is
  more likely to prolapse [...] The mother is more likely to bleed heavily after the birth. »
- « Cut the first baby's cord, and tightly clamp or tie the end that is coming out of the mother. Twin
  babies sometimes share a placenta, and the second baby could bleed through the cord of the first. »
- « The second baby should be born within 15 to 20 minutes, but might take longer. » « No
  contractions within 2 hours [...] letting the first baby breastfeed [...] massage the mother's
  nipples [...] Do not give medicines to get labor started again. » « Bleeding after the birth of one
  twin and before the second [...] Get the second baby out as fast as you can. Stimulate the nipples
  [...] ask the mother to push very hard. »
- Avant un utérotonique (*Check the mother's physical signs*) : « **Before you give the medicine,
  gently feel the mother's belly to be sure there is not a second baby in the womb.** »

### Placenta, dernier recours (*Check the mother's physical signs*)

- Main dans l'utérus : « Taking out the placenta by hand is very dangerous. It can cause serious
  infection, or tear the cervix, the placenta, or the womb, and cause worse bleeding. [...] very
  painful for the mother and can easily cause her to go into shock. Do not take the placenta out by
  hand unless it is the only way to save a mother's life. » Geste : « follow the cord up into the
  mother's vagina — you will have to fit your whole hand inside [...] keep your fingers and thumb close
  together, making a cone shape [...] Pry the edge of the placenta away from the womb wall using the
  side of your little finger. [...] (It feels a little like peeling the skin off an orange or other
  thick-skinned fruit.) » Après : « keep her head down, and her hips and legs up ».
- Morceau resté : « Fold a piece of sterile gauze over your fingers. The womb is very slippery [...]
  (Or tie a string to a strong piece of woven material like gauze, sterilize it, and keep it in your
  birth kit. The string will stay outside the mother) [...] Be sure to use strong material that will
  not break apart ». (Le livre reprend la ficelle qui reste dehors pour le tampon de l'étape 66 :
  adaptation.)
- Utérus retourné : « This can happen if someone pulls on the cord before the placenta has separated
  [...] or if someone pushes on the womb [...] It can also happen by itself. » « Gently but firmly put
  the womb back through the vagina and cervix [...] If you cannot push it back up, you may have to roll
  it up with your fingers. Push the part of the womb closest to the cervix in first [...] If you cannot
  push the womb back into the right place, put it into the vagina ». « The mother should lie on her back
  with a pillow, blankets, or other padding under her hips. »
- Traction : « If the placenta is still attached to the womb, the cord may break or you may pull the
  woman's womb out of her body. »
