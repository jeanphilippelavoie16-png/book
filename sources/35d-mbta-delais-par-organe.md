- **Organisme :** Missouri Blood Trackers Association (MBTA), association de conducteurs de chiens
  de sang du Missouri. **Association privée, pas un ministère.**
- **Titre de la page :** « After Shot Guide and Wait Times »
- **URL :** https://www.mobloodtrackers.com/general-8
- **Date de consultation :** 2026-09-23 (page téléchargée par le premier rédacteur ; texte extrait et
  relu le 2026-09-24)
- **Langue :** anglais
- **Chapitre qui s'en sert :** 35d (§3, tableaux des étapes 18 et 21, notes 2 et 3)
- **Statut :** seule source trouvée qui donne un **délai par organe** et une **durée avant la

  `35d-hunter-ed-national-pistage-et-approche.md`) ; la MBTA ne fournit que la colonne « Plus sûr »
  et la colonne « Ce que fait la bête ».

## Extraits intégraux

> « 1. Heart Shot […] Time to death: Usually within seconds to a minute. Deer often sprint a short
> distance (30–100 yards) due to adrenaline before collapsing. Wait time before tracking: 30 mins -
> 1 hour. »

> « 2. Double-Lung Shot […] Time to death: Usually 15 seconds to 2 minutes. Deer may run hard, then
> crash within 50–200 yards. Wait time before tracking: 2 hours; Often you can safely start
> earlier, but it's wise to err on the cautious side. »

> « 3. Single-Lung Shot […] The lung hit will present with blood that seems watery and pink with
> very small bubbles mixed usually with bright red blood from the muscular areas surrounding it
> such as the shoulder. Time to death: Variable — can vary from minutes to hours to days. Deer may
> bed and die slowly, or in some most survive indefinitely. Wait time before tracking: 4–6 hours,
> ideally overnight if temperatures allow. »

> « 4. Liver Shot […] Usually the blood will be a much darker if not almost black color due to low
> oxygen saturation. The deer will usually hunch and not run full speed with its tail down. Often
> the deer will walk off slowly. […] Time to death: Usually 30 minutes to 3–4 hours. Deer often bed
> down and die if not pressured. Wait time before tracking: 8-12 hours is recommended. Many hunters
> track liver hits the next morning if shot late in the day. »

> « 5. Lung + Liver Shot […] typically 3-4 hours. Wait time before tracking: 6-8 hours to be safe,
> 8-12 if blood sign is sparse. »

> « 6. Intestinal (Gut) Shot […] Many times there will be little to no bleeding. The intestines
> will pack the wound in most cases and block the flow of blood. The deer will usually hunch and
> walk off slowly while bedding often. The arrow will be slimy and have a sour smell with occasional
> fecal matter. Time to death: Often 12 hours to 7 days. Wait time before tracking: 24-36 hours »

> « 7. Stomach Shot […] Arrow will often have an acidic smell but can sometimes smell sweet. There
> will usually be food matter or grit on it. Time to death: Very slow, often 8 – 24+ hours. […] Wait
> time before tracking: 18–24 hours, almost always overnight. »

> « Bright, frothy red blood if a lung or pulmonary vessel is hit. Steady streams or sprays of
> bright red blood from high-pressure arteries. »

> « Bright, abundant blood trails that begin quickly after impact usually indicate arterial damage.
> Fine mist or spray on vegetation often suggests high-pressure bleeding from an artery rather than
> low-pressure venous bleeding. »

**Transposition à l'orignal : `[À VÉRIFIER]`** — dit dans le texte, sous le tableau de l'étape 18.

**Corrigé le 2026-09-24 (porte 3) :** la source donne la même réaction (« hunch », « walk off
slowly ») pour le **foie** et pour l'**intestin**. La ligne « Bête voûtée, queue basse, qui s'en va au
pas » ne fixe donc plus les durées du foie : elle dit « foie, panse ou intestin », attend toute la
nuit, et prend le délai de l'intestin (24 à 36 h) si le sang ne tranche pas. Les durées du foie sont
passées à la ligne « Sang foncé presque noir ».

**Ce que le livre ne reprend pas :** l'appel au pisteur (« Give your local Tracker a CALL ») et la
carte des pisteurs.
