## La pompe à volets (KAP), annexe B

Le doublage du cadre (p. 196, « The frame », point 1.b) :

> « b. To make the front side smooth and flat so that the flaps will close tightly, fill in the spaces as follows: Cut
> two pieces of 1 X 2-in. boards long enough to fill in the spaces on top of the 36-in. sides of the frame between the
> top and bottom horizontal boards, and nail these filler boards in place. Do the same thing with a 1 X 1-in. board (or a
> board the size of that used for the center brace) as a filler board for the center brace (see Fig. 4).
> If the frame is made of only one thickness of board 3/4 in. to 1 in. thick, it will not be sufficiently heavy to swing
> back far enough on its free-swinging return stroke. »

Le montant du milieu (p. 196, point 1.a) :

> « Next, cut and nail to the frame a piece of 1 X 1-in. lumber 36 in. long, for a center vertical brace. […] Figure 3
> shows the back side of the frame; the flap valves will be attached on the front (the opposite) side. »

Les entailles des volets (p. 198) :

> « After the hem has been made, cut a notch with scissors in each hemmed corner of the flap (Figs. 6 and 8). […] Also
> cut a notch in the center of the flap (along the hem line) extending 1/2 in. downward and extending horizontally 1/4
> in. beyond each of the two sides of the vertical brace (see Fig. 6). The notch MUST be wider than the brace. »

Les ficelles fixées aussi au montant du milieu (p. 199 et 200) :

> « drive three in a horizontal line to attach each flap-stop- one in each of the two vertical 36-in. sides of the frame
> and one in the vertical center brace » ; « This tying should include wrapping each horizontal flap-stop once around
> the vertical center brace. »

Le fil seulement, jamais le plastique (p. 200, « Final assembly ») :

> « Staple, nail, or tie the 9 flap pivot-wires or pivot-strings (each with its flap attached) in their positions […]
> Connect each pivot-wire at both ends to the 36-in. vertical sides of the frame. Also connect it to the vertical brace.
> BE CAREFUL TO NAIL THE PIVOT-WIRES ONLY TO THE FRAME AND THE BRACE. DO NOT NAIL ANY PLASTIC DIRECTLY TO THE WOOD.
> All flaps must turn freely on their pivot-wires. »

La corde au montant du milieu (p. 200 et 205) :

> « For this 3-ft model, tie the pull-cord to the center brace about 12-1/2 in. below the hinge line » (p. 200) ;
> « (Pumps with improvised hinges and large pumps must be pulled from a connection point on their center vertical brace
> to make them swing properly.) » (p. 205, « Narrow KAPs »).

L'air qui revient autour du cadre (p. 205, « Maintenance ») :

> « make sure that there is the minimum practical area of open spaces in and around the KAP through which air can flow
> back around the pump frame, opposite to the pumped direction. »

Le bas de la porte fermé sous la pompe (annexe A, p. 173, « Expedient ventilation and cooling ») :

> « Install a KAP […] near the top of the doorway through which you can feel air naturally flowing into the shelter room
> at that time. […] To enable the KAP to efficiently pump fresh air from the outdoors all the way through the shelter,
> block the lower half of the doorway in which the KAP is installed with a quickly removable covering, such as a
> plastic-covered frame made of sticks. »

## Le desséchant de l'électroscope (KFM), annexe C

> « (Do not use calcium chloride or other salt.) » (p. 215, « What a KFM is and how it works ») ;
> « An excellent drying agent (anhydrite) can be made by heating the gypsum in ordinary gypsum wallboard (sheetrock).
> Do NOT use calcium chloride. » (p. 219, « Make the drying agent »).

**Aucune raison n'est donnée**, ni dans l'annexe C ni ailleurs dans le livre (recherche de « calcium chloride »,
« salt », « desiccant », « drying agent » dans tout le texte). Même constat dans les instructions du KFM tirées à part
(copie consultée le 18 octobre 2026 : `https://www.abomb1.org/pdf/kfm_inst.pdf`, même texte, non enregistrée parce
qu'elle n'appuie aucun fait imprimé) et dans les résultats d'une recherche web (aucune page officielle ne donne la
raison). La phrase du chapitre reste sans mécanisme.

## La neige et la pluie

> « 5. Contaminated water from streams, which would be especially dangerous if the stream is muddy from the first heavy
> rains after fallout is deposited. » ; « 7. Water obtained by melting snow that has fallen through air containing
> fallout particles, or from snow lying on the ground onto which fallout has fallen. Avoid using such water for drinking
> or cooking, if possible. » (chap. 8, p. 71 et 72)

> « Only where such tiny particles are promptly brought to earth by rain- outs or snow-outs in scattered "hot spots" »
> (chap. 1, p. 12) ; « Wet deposition of fallout from both surface and air bursts can result in "hot spots" » (fig. 1.4,
> p. 15).

Ce que la source ne dit pas : où va la poussière quand la neige fond au printemps. Une recherche web (18 octobre 2026)
n'a trouvé que des études sur le césium dans l'eau de fonte de bassins versants (Norvège, Fukushima), sans rien sur
les endroits où l'eau de fonte concentre la poussière autour d'une maison. Rien de la fonte n'est écrit au chapitre.

## Les signes de la phase critique de l'irradiation

> « In the final phase, the victim of serious or fatal radiation sickness will have reduced resistance to infections
> […]. The final phase usually lasts for one to two months. Any available antibiotics should be reserved for this
> critical phase of the illness. » ; « Recovery from most cases of radiation sickness will be more likely for patients
> who receive a well balanced diet, rest, freedom from stress, and clean surroundings. » (chap. 12, p. 110)

La phrase des antibiotiques de l'ORNL n'est plus reprise au ch. 4 : le ch. 32 refuse tout stock d'antibiotiques sans
ordonnance en cours (section 5-B, ligne « Ce que le chapitre refuse de mettre au stock » ; encadré 4). Le fait qui
reste, la résistance plus faible aux infections, vient de la même page.
