# L'écureuil derrière le tronc, et la chasse à deux — quatre pages d'agences d'État

Fiche groupée. Toutes consultées le **24 septembre 2026** ; pages enregistrées en HTML à côté de
cette fiche. Anglais. Les quatre parlent de l'**écureuil gris** (et du renard, au Kentucky), pas de
l'écureuil roux : le comportement décrit est celui d'un écureuil arboricole qui fuit par le tronc,
ce que fait aussi l'écureuil roux, mais aucune de ces pages ne le dit de lui.

- **Sert au chapitre :** 43b — Chasser le petit gibier (§3 « L'écureuil met le tronc entre lui et
  le danger » ; §5-F, étape 27 « Contre le tronc » ; §7, ligne « L'écureuil reste toujours de
  l'autre côté du tronc » ; §9, « Garder un tronc entre soi et elle »).

---

## 1. Georgia Department of Natural Resources, Wildlife Resources Division — « Out My Backdoor: How Squirrels Survive »

- **Auteur :** Terry W. Johnson, ancien gestionnaire du programme des espèces non gibier de la
  Wildlife Resources Division
- **URL :** https://georgiawildlife.com/out-my-backdoor-how-squirrels-survive
- **Copie :** `43b-georgia-dnr-ecureuil-derriere-le-tronc.html` (HTTP 200)
- **Extraits intégraux :**

> « Gray squirrels use camouflage to remain out of the sight of would-be predators. As any
> squirrel hunter can attest, regardless of whether you hunt squirrels in late summer, autumn or
> winter, it's difficult to spot a squirrel in the top of a tree. The animal's grayish to
> yellowish brown pelage blends in with the bark of the trees they most often inhabit. This makes
> one almost impossible to see unless it moves. Often, when a squirrel recognizes it has been
> spotted it will scoot around to the backside of a tree and then peek around the trunk to see if
> a predator is still close by. While hunting I have often seen a squirrel scamper around to the
> backside of the tree to avoid danger. When I would walk around the tree trying to relocate it,
> the squirrel would simply crawl around to the opposite side. »

> « Gray squirrels will also lay motionless on a branch, with only its feet and tail hanging
> down. In some instances, they will flatten their bodies against the tree's trunk and remain
> motionless until a predator moves on. »

## 2. Nebraska Game and Parks Commission — *Nebraskaland Magazine*, « Squirrel Hunting Questions »

- **Auteur :** Jeff Kurrus, rédacteur en chef de *Nebraskaland* (magazine de la Commission) ;
  article du 31 juillet 2020
- **URL :** https://magazine.outdoornebraska.gov/stories/hunting/squirrel-hunting-questions/
- **Captation :** la page en ligne répond **403** (à curl comme à la lecture web). Texte tiré de la
  copie de l'Internet Archive du 7 octobre 2025
  (`web.archive.org/web/20251007221301/https://magazine.outdoornebraska.gov/stories/hunting/squirrel-hunting-questions/`),
  récupérée le 24 septembre 2026. Copie : `43b-nebraska-gpc-ecureuil-chasse-a-deux.html`
- **Extraits intégraux :**

> « Partner or Alone? There's no quieter way to hunt squirrels than hunting alone. Plus, you're
> able to stalk or sit for however long you desire with no one else to cater to. However, hunting
> with a partner is fun. It gives you another set of eyes in the woods and, if a squirrel happens
> to move itself to the opposite side of a tree, you and your partner can take turns playing dog
> by walking to the opposite side of the tree in an attempt to get the squirrel to move again.
> Without a squirrel dog, a partner is the next best option. »

> « However, hunters must always assure there is a safe background due to the extreme distance
> (more than 2,000 yards) that a .22 bullet can travel if unimpeded. »

> « I've always tried to keep my shotgun range at less than 30 yards, while my rifle limit is
> around 40. »

## 3. Kentucky Department of Fish & Wildlife Resources — « Learn to Hunt Squirrel »

- **URL :** https://fw.ky.gov/Education/Pages/Learn-to-Hunt-Squirrel.aspx
- **Copie :** `43b-kentucky-dfwr-ecureuil-cache-cache.html` (HTTP 200)
- **Extraits intégraux :**

> « A squirrel dog is not a necessity for success, but may speed up the hunting. Squirrels will
> often spot a hunter and play a hide and seek game. They will sneak around the base of the tree
> to keep track of a hunter's location and bark warnings to other squirrels. A squirrel dog
> circles the tree and the squirrel now tries to escape the dog. As the dog circles, he brings the
> squirrel into full view of the hunter for a clean shot. »

> « Squirrels are prey for many natural predators and the squirrel uses all areas of the tree to
> escape from them. For ground restricted predators such as most canines (gray fox are limited
> climbers), only a few feet of the trunk is sufficient escape habitat. »

## 4. Missouri Department of Conservation — *Missouri Conservationist*, « Squirrel Hunting Made Easy » (octobre 1997, contenu archivé)

- **URL :** https://mdc.mo.gov/magazines/conservationist/1997-10/squirrel-hunting-made-easy
- **Copie :** `43b-mdc-1997-ecureuil-arbre-entre-soi-et-lui.html` (HTTP 200)
- **Extraits intégraux :**

> « Unless your eyes are a lot better than mine, forget about looking for a squirrel in a tree
> nearby. If you move around, the squirrel will see you and it becomes a game of hide and seek.
> Most of the time you will be the loser with a sore neck. »

> « Never make any quick moves and when walking over leaves go as slow as possible. Put your feet
> down gently, avoiding sticks. »

> « If you are in an opening and see a squirrel in the distance, try to circle until you get a
> large tree between you and the quarry. »

> « I use a shotgun for squirrels and I never shoot one facing away from me. It will put all the
> shot in the hind legs and back. [...] I finally came up with the idea of aiming in front of their
> nose a few inches (depending on the distance) and putting most of the shot in the head. »

## Ce que ces pages ne disent pas

- **Le truc du chasseur seul** (lancer une branche, une pierre ou sa veste de l'autre côté du tronc
  pour faire passer l'écureuil de son côté) : absent des quatre pages. Il n'apparaît, dans la
  recherche du 24 septembre 2026, que sur des forums et des sites commerciaux, non retenus.
  Massachusetts Division of Fisheries and Wildlife (« Squirrel hunting tips », copie d'archive du
  14 juin 2026), Indiana DNR, Virginia DWR, West Virginia DNR et Arkansas GFC, lus le même jour,
  n'en parlent pas non plus. La page de New Hampshire Fish and Game (« Squirrel Hunting 101:
  Outsmarting Ol' Bushytail ») répond toujours **403**, et l'Internet Archive n'en a aucune copie.
- **L'écureuil roux** : aucune des quatre pages ne le vise.
