## Extraits intégraux (domaine public)

### Brouté contre coupé — le principe (p. 11)

> « Browsing refers to the feeding habits of ungulates, including deer, elk, and livestock.
> Browsing on woody vegetation during the dormant season leaves a ragged, splintered break (fig.
> 1), because these animals lack upper incisors. »

> « Clipping refers to the feeding habits of rodents and rabbits, which produce a smooth, oblique
> cut on woody shoots (fig. 1). These animals possess prominent chisellike incisors and must tilt
> their heads to the side to clip a stem. »

### Clé d'identification (p. 16 à 19) — extraits

> « Bark abraded and shredded on upper stem; small lateral branches broken by antler polishing
> . . . BIG GAME »

> « Strips of discarded bark at base of tree...vertical grooves on exposed sapwood . . . BEAR »

> « Tooth marks 1/8 inch wide or wider; sapwood deeply gnawed; 1/2- to 1-inch oblong droppings and
> quills; pieces of outer bark around base of tree . . . PORCUPINE »

> « Browsing-like injury with bud or needle clipping clustered droppings on stumps, logs and rocks
> . . . GROUSE »

> « Dams, ponds, and lodges present...cutting areas with distinct trails leading to water..freshly
> peeled sticks; signs of active beaver pond...wood chips present near stumps . . . BEAVER »

> « Conical top on stumps, with prominent toothmarks . . . BEAVER »

> « Prominent horizontal or diagonal toothmarks on exposed sapwood of upper bole and major
> branches...oblong droppings up to 1 inch long under tree . . . PORCUPINE »

> « Short strips of discarded bark (1/2 inch wide by 2 to 3 inches long) under injured tree...fine
> gnawing visible on exposed sapwood . . . TREE SQUIRREL »

> « Cutting confined to branch tips and twigs...peeled or debudded shoots litter ground under tree
> . . . TREE SQUIRREL »

> « Cutting of moderate-sized branches...no peeled twigs . . . PORCUPINE »

> « Flattened ovoid droppings . . . SNOWSHOE HARE OR COTTONTAIL »

### Ours noir (p. 99)

> « Black bears leave large strips of bark around the bases of trees they peel. Long vertical
> grooves in the sapwood are left by the incisors as the bear strips off the outer layers of
> sapwood (fig. 24), in contrast to rodents that leave short horizontal or diagonal grooves in
> barked stems. »

### Porc-épic (p. 14)

> « Droppings 1 inch long are characteristic of porcupines. »

### Castor (p. 146)

> « Beavers can be identified by the presence of characteristic beaver dams, conical stumps with
> prominent tooth marks, and large wood chips present around stumps. Peeled sticks also may be
> present in or near water »

### Wapiti (p. 71) — même mâchoire que l'orignal

> « Elk, possessing lower incisors only, leave a splintered break on browsed, dormant woody stems.
> Spring browsing may cause the bark to slip from the browsed twig, resulting in a stripped stem
> below the break. »

### Tétras (p. 181, tétras sombre)

> « Buds are neatly plucked from the stem, leaving only the inconspicuous point of attachment.
> Needle clipping may result in the removal of the entire needle or only a portion of it. »

### Souris (p. 14)

> « Droppings about one-eighth of an inch long, chewed paper, and seed hulls are signs of mouse
> activity. »

## Ce que le chapitre en tire

- **Le mécanisme qui départage brouté et coupé** : pas d'incisives en haut chez les cervidés →
  bout effiloché ; incisives en ciseau chez le lièvre et les rongeurs → coupe nette en biais :
  §3 et fiches.
- **La largeur de la marque de dent** comme règle : castor 6 mm, porc-épic 3,6 mm, lièvre 2,3 mm,
  écureuil 1 mm : §3, §9 (se mesure à l'ongle).
- Ours : longs sillons verticaux, lambeaux d'écorce au pied ; porc-épic : marques
  horizontales ou en biais, crottes oblongues jusqu'à 2,5 cm sous l'arbre, rameaux coupés au
  sol ; castor : souche en crayon, gros copeaux ; écureuil : petits lambeaux, pousses pelées au
  sol ; tétras : bourgeons cueillis net, crottes groupées sur souches, billots, roches.
- Écorce effilochée haut sur la tige, petites branches cassées : frottis de bois (cervidé).
