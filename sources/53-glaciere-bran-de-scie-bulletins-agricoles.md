# Ministère de l'Agriculture du Canada (1907) et USDA (1920, révisé 1933) — Garder la glace de l'hiver sous le bran de scie

Deux bulletins agricoles officiels, du domaine public, numérisés par Internet Archive. Ce sont des
documents anciens : ils valent pour le **geste** (empiler, isoler, drainer) et pour les ordres de
grandeur de perte, pas pour un règlement.

## 1. Canada — « The Use of Ice on the Farm »

- **Organisme :** Department of Agriculture (Canada), série du commissaire à l'industrie
  laitière et à l'entreposage frigorifique, Bulletin n° 20 ; auteur J. A. Ruddick ; 1907
- **Notice :** https://archive.org/details/useoficeonfarm20rudd (numérisation financée par
  Agriculture et Agroalimentaire Canada, 2012)
- **Texte intégral :** https://archive.org/download/useoficeonfarm20rudd/useoficeonfarm20rudd_djvu.txt
  — enregistré : `53-agriculture-canada-1907-glace-a-la-ferme-texte.txt`
- **Consulté le :** 24 septembre 2026

> « Every farmer in whose vicinity natural ice is available should store a sufficient quantity
> for use during the summer months. »

> « The crudest kind of building, which will keep out the sun and the rain, or the corner of a
> shed, will serve for the mere storage of ice if dry saw-dust or marsh hay is available in which
> to pack it for protection against the heat. All that is necessary to do is to provide for some
> drainage and cover the ice on all sides, top and bottom, with about 12 inches of saw-dust, or
> 24 inches of hay or cut straw, and protect the covering from the weather. »

> « A consumption of 2 cubic feet (115 pounds) per day for four months would amount to nearly
> 7 tons. Allowing for the waste when such a comparatively small body of ice is stored, a
> building 10 feet square and 10 feet high will afford ample space for that quantity of ice, if
> it is carefully packed. »

> « Fifty pounds per day for four months would amount to 3 tons. Allowing for waste, a solid
> block of ice 6 feet square and 6 feet high should be sufficient if properly stored. »

Instructions (« Extract from Bulletin No. 10 ») :

> « 3. Before putting in the ice cover the stones or gravel in the ice-house with 12 inches of dry
> saw-dust. »

> « 4. Pack the ice directly on the sawdust. Leave a space of 12 inches between the walls and the
> ice. Place the cakes of ice as close together as possible, and fill in all unavoidable spaces
> with crushed ice or snow, well rammed. Never use any saw-dust between the tiers. »

> « 5. Fill the 12-inch space between the ice and the wall with dry saw-dust. Be careful that the
> saw-dust does not contain any ice chips or snow. When no saw-dust is available, cut hay or cut
> straw, or chaff, may be used, but in this case the space between the wall and the ice should be
> twice as large (24 inches instead of 12) »

> « 6. Cover the ice on top with saw-dust or long hay; 12 inches of saw-dust will do. Hay should be
> put on 2 feet thick. […] When saw-dust is used, put on 2 feet thick at first. This will leave
> 12 inches to spare to fill in the sides in the spring, when the saw-dust along the sides has
> settled. »

> « 7. A loft floor over the ice-house does more harm than good, as it prevents circulation of air
> and keeps the covering damp. »

## 2. États-Unis — Farmers' Bulletin 1078, « Harvesting and Storing Ice on the Farm »

- **Organisme :** U.S. Department of Agriculture ; auteur John T. Bowen ; paru en janvier 1920,
  révisé en août 1933
- **Notice :** https://archive.org/details/CAT10306018 (exemplaire de la National Agricultural
  Library)
- **Texte intégral :** https://archive.org/download/CAT10306018/farmbul1078rev1933_djvu.txt —
  enregistré : `53-usda-fb1078-glace-a-la-ferme-texte.txt`
- **Consulté le :** 24 septembre 2026

> « The space used for storing ice should accommodate about 50 percent more than is actually
> needed in order to allow for shrinkage. »

> « […] in a good ice house, where the shrinkage from melting is not more than 30 percent, half a
> ton of ice per cow is sufficient […] »

> « The quantity of ice lost by melting depends partly upon the manner of packing. The cakes
> should be placed close together so that the mass will be as tight and solid as possible, thus
> preventing cracks and openings that will allow air to circulate. »

> « Before ice is placed in the ice house a layer of dry sawdust about a foot thick should be
> placed in the bottom of the house […] If sawdust or mill shavings are used, a space of at least
> 12 inches must be left between the sides of the ice stack and the walls of the building. »

> « Loss by melting is in proportion to the surface area exposed to the air or packing material;
> hence it is advisable to store ice in the form of a cube, or as nearly so as practicable. »

> « Unless adequate drainage is provided the bottom of the pit will be flooded with water from
> melted ice or from seepage. » (section « Ice pits »)

## Ce que la fiche permet d'écrire

- Une réserve de glace **bien empilée et bien couverte passe l'été** : le bulletin canadien la
  calcule pour **quatre mois** d'usage.
- Perte : une bonne glacière perd **jusqu'à 30 %** ; prévoir **la moitié de plus** que ce dont on
  a besoin.
- Le geste : blocs **serrés les uns contre les autres**, en cube, trous bouchés à la glace
  pilée ou à la neige damée, **jamais de bran de scie entre les rangs** ; **30 cm (12 po)** de bran
  de scie sec dessous, autour et dessus (60 cm de foin ou de paille) ; drainage au fond ; bran de
  scie à remettre sur les côtés au printemps quand il s'est tassé.
