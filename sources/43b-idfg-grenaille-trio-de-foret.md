# Idaho Fish and Game — « Idaho's early small game opportunities are a great gateway for new hunters »

- **Organisme :** Idaho Department of Fish and Game (ministère d'État), communiqué de presse du
  **23 août 2019**. Anglais.
- **URL :** https://idfg.idaho.gov/press/idahos-early-small-game-opportunities-are-great-gateway-new-hunters
- **Consultée le :** 24 septembre 2026 (HTTP 200)
- **Captation :** `43b-idfg-trio-foret-ecureuil-lievre-tetras.html` (page complète) et
  `43b-idfg-trio-foret-ecureuil-lievre-tetras-texte.txt` (texte extrait)
- **Pourquoi cette page :** c'est la seule page d'agence trouvée qui donne un numéro de grenaille
  pour le **lièvre d'Amérique**, et elle vise exactement le trio du chapitre : écureuil **roux**,

  ensemble, dans le même bois.
- **Sert au chapitre :** 43b — Chasser le petit gibier (§4, grenaille : « Pour le lièvre, n° 4 à
  7½ »).

## Extraits intégraux

> « Hunting small game is a great way for beginners to get started, and some of the earliest fall
> hunting seasons — including squirrel, snowshoe hare, cottontail, dove and forest grouse — present
> excellent opportunities for new hunters to develop their skills and confidence without breaking
> the bank. »

> « It doesn't take specialized or expensive gear to hunt them: the barebones essentials are a .22
> long rifle and/or any shotgun, and clothes and boots suitable for hiking. »

> « **The Forestland Trifecta.** The list starts out with the small game forestland trifecta — red
> squirrel, snowshoe hare, and forest grouse — which can be hunted at the same time and in the same
> general type of habitat. [...] All three species can be hunted with either a shotgun or small
> caliber rifle, depending on the hunter's preferences. Considering you may be doing a lot of
> walking at high elevation, keep weight in mind when choosing a firearm. **For shotguns, a light
> 20-gauge loaded with No. 6-8 lead shot will work just fine.** »

> « There are three species of forest grouse in Idaho, all of which are native to the state: the
> dusky (blue) grouse, the ruffed grouse, and the spruce grouse. »

> « Doves are hunted with shotguns as they fly [...] Bring No. 6-8 shot shells, and lots of them,
> on your dove hunt. »

## Missouri Department of Conservation — le lapin à queue blanche (même taille de bête)

- **« Basic Hunting: Rabbit »** (brochure, © 2019 Conservation Commission of the State of
  Missouri), https://mdc.mo.gov/sites/default/files/2022-06/E00251_Basic_Hunting_Rabbit.pdf —
  copie `43b-mdc-basic-hunting-rabbit-2019.pdf`, texte `43b-mdc-basic-hunting-rabbit-2019-texte.txt`.
  Extraits intégraux :

> « Gear and Firearms. Most hunters prefer to use shotguns, paired with shot sizes No. 4 through
> No. 6 to effectively and efficiently harvest rabbits. While all gauges are legal for hunting
> rabbits, a 20-gauge with No. 6 shot is what most prefer and start out with. For a real
> challenge, some hunters will use the lighter 28-gauge or .410. »

> « Rabbits have a small home range and are reluctant to leave it. After flushing from cover, a
> rabbit will run away, but eventually circle around back to where it was initially found. »

> « Rabbits typically run a large circle while fleeing a pack of hounds and usually make their way
> back to the original starting point. When the hounds flush a rabbit, move to that spot and wait
> for the dogs to bring them around. »

- **« Rabbit Hunting: Getting Started »** (*Missouri Conservationist*, octobre 2015),
  https://mdc.mo.gov/magazines/conservationist/2015-10/rabbit-hunting-getting-started — copie
  `43b-mdc-2015-rabbit-hunting-getting-started.html`. Extraits intégraux :

> « Most rabbit hunters carry shotguns. Smaller gauges, such as .410 and 28 gauges, full choked and
> loaded with shells holding number 4 shot, are most popular. Heavy shot loads are not necessary
> to cleanly kill rabbits. Larger shot size reduces the number of pellets and, accordingly,
> reduces the amount of meat ruined by pellets. »

> « With beagles, when the dogs jump a rabbit and no shot is offered, hunters spread out in the
> vicinity of where the rabbit jumped and wait for the dogs to trail the rabbit. If the rabbit
> does not duck into a hole or other cover where the dogs can't reach it, the rabbit will likely
> circle back to where it was first jumped. »

Aucune des deux pages ne parle d'un sifflement pour arrêter le lapin, ni de ce qu'il fait au
retour du cercle.
