## 1. NHS — Raynaud's — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/raynauds/

- **Copie :** `sources/24-corps-nhs-raynauds.html` — revue affichée 20 July 2023

> « When you're cold, anxious or stressed, your fingers and toes may change colour. »
> « The skin may turn white or a lighter colour as blood flow is restricted.
> Sometimes the skin turns blue as blood vessels react.
> The skin may turn red as blood flow returns. »
> « The symptoms of Raynaud's may last from a few minutes to a few hours. »
> « Raynaud's is sometimes caused by another health condition, taking certain medicines, or
> working with vibrating tools for a long time. »
> « do not smoke – improve your circulation by quitting smoking »
> « your symptoms are only on 1 side of your body » (signe à faire voir)

## 2. NHS — Chilblains — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/chilblains/

- **Copie :** `sources/24-corps-nhs-chilblains.html` — revue affichée 22 April 2026

> « Chilblains usually appear a few hours after you've been in the cold. »
> « Your fingers or toes may become red or purple. »
> « Chilblains usually go away on their own in 2 to 3 weeks. »
> « do not put your feet or hands on a radiator or under hot water to warm them up »
> « Chilblains happen when the blood vessels deep in your skin stay small, but the vessels closer
> to the surface of your skin get bigger as you warm up. »

*Note de terminologie :* l'anglais *chilblains* est l'**engelure** du français de France (la
lésion rouge et prurigineuse, sans gel). Au Québec, « engelure » se dit couramment pour la
gelure. Le chapitre 24 évite le mot et décrit la lésion.

## 3. NHS — Blisters — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/blisters/

- **Copie :** `sources/24-corps-nhs-blisters.html` — revue affichée 22 November 2023

> « Blisters often heal on their own within a week. »
> « do not peel the skin off a burst blister »
> « Blood blisters may look red or black and are filled with blood instead of clear fluid. »
> « An infected blister can be hot and filled with green or yellow pus. »
> « Blisters develop to protect damaged skin and help it heal. They're mostly caused by friction »
> « wear new shoes for short periods of time, until they're comfortable
> wear thick socks during exercise, such as moisture-wicking sports socks
> change your socks frequently if you get sweaty feet
> wear protective gloves when you exercise or if you use tools at work »

## 4. AAD (American Academy of Dermatology) — How to prevent and treat blisters

- **URL :** https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/injured-skin/burns/prevent-treat-blisters
- **Copie :** `sources/24-corps-aad-ampoules.html` — « Last updated: 7/6/26 »

> « Stop your activity immediately if you experience pain or discomfort, or if your skin turns
> red. Otherwise, you may get a blister. »
> « Most blisters heal on their own in one to two weeks. »
> « Use padding. To protect blisters in pressure areas, such as the bottom of your feet, use
> padding. Cut the padding into a donut shape with a hole in the middle and place it around the
> blister. Then, cover the blister and padding with a bandage. »
> « if your blister is large and very painful, it may be necessary to drain the blister to reduce
> discomfort. To do this, sterilize a small needle using rubbing alcohol. Then, use the needle to
> carefully pierce one edge of the blister, which will allow some of the fluid to drain. »
> « Once your blister has drained, wash the area with soap and water and apply petroleum jelly. Do
> not remove the "roof" of the blister, as this will protect the raw skin underneath as it heals. »
> « Apply powder or petroleum jelly to problem areas. »

## 5. NHS — Ingrown toenail — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/ingrown-toenail/

- **Copie :** `sources/24-corps-nhs-ingrown-toenail.html` — revue affichée 30 June 2025

> « soak your foot in warm, salty water to help soften the skin around your toe and reduce the
> chances of infection
> keep your foot dry for the rest of the day
> wear wide, comfortable shoes or sandals »
> « do not cut your toenail – leave it to grow out
> do not pick at your toe or toenail »
> « do not cut your toenails too short
> cut straight across the nail, not the edges »
> « Signs of an infected toe include: pus coming out of it / you feel hot or shivery »
