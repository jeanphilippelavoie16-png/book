## Extraits intégraux

Résumé, p. 54 :

> « one can easily and effectively boil in perishable containers made of bark, hide, leaves,
> even paper and plastic, placed directly on the fire and without using heated stones »

> « Enlarged hide and bark containers are inherently flimsy, and at some point it would have
> become necessary to support them in a pit. »

Introduction, p. 54–55 :

> « One can readily boil water in perishable containers directly over open flames—in fact,
> even in a paper cup with a blow torch—no hot rocks or boiling pits needed! The container will
> not burn so long as it remains filled with liquid. »

p. 58 (sources ethnographiques) :

> « In an early 20th-century wilderness survival manual, Horace Kephart (1906: 231) provides
> instructions for direct boiling in a birch bark bucket. Alanson Skinner (1911: 557, 1912: 30)
> describes both paunch boiling and cooking in birch bark containers among the Cree, Ojibwa,
> and Blackfeet »

> « Boiling directly in a bark container suspended over a slow fire is also characteristic of
> the Sub-Arctic plus a few tribes in adjacent parts of other bordering areas » *(citation de
> Driver et Massey 1957)*

p. 58–59 (expérience de Holman et Egan 1985) :

> « after heating a birch-bark tray for several hours directly on hot coals, they ended it by
> adding heated stones. What is of particular interest in their experiments is the fact that
> the bark containers maintained their integrity throughout, which lasted for more than five
> hours, even though they were placed directly on the coals (though carefully kept away from
> direct contact with flames to avoid burning off the rims exposed above the level of the
> liquid). »

> « The authors found that direct heating with the bark trays required less fuel and less time
> than applying heat indirectly by stone-boiling. »

> « an expenditure of at least twice as much time and fuel would be required » *(Holman et
> Egan 1985 : 67, à propos de l'ébullition aux pierres chaudes)*

p. 60 :

> « Large bark containers are flimsy without buttressing of some sort around the vessel's mouth
> […] Thus, without structural support the volume that one could effectively heat by direct
> boiling might be limited to comparatively small amounts »

Conclusion, p. 61 :

> « So long as the container is kept filled with liquid it will not ignite, even when heated
> with a blowtorch. The only vulnerable part of the container is the rim itself, the portion of
> the vessel that is not constantly in direct contact with the liquid. »

## Ce que ça change au chapitre

- Un contenant d'écorce de bouleau **va sur les braises** tant qu'il est plein ; seul le bord
  au-dessus de l'eau peut brûler. On le tient à l'écart des flammes, le niveau haut.
- Il tient **des heures** sur les braises (plus de cinq heures dans l'expérience de Holman et
  Egan).
- Chauffer l'écorce directement demande **moins de bois et moins de temps** que les pierres
  chaudes (au moins deux fois moins, selon Holman et Egan).
- Un **grand** contenant d'écorce plie : on le fait petit.
