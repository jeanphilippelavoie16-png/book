## Extraits intégraux (anglais)

> « One of the most pervasive weight loss rules is that a cumulative energy deficit of 3500 kcal is
> required per pound of body weight loss, or equivalently 32.2 MJ per kg. »

> « The origin of this rule can be traced back to a calculation that assumes exclusive loss of
> adipose tissue consisting of 87% fat. However, Forbes has pointed out that lean body mass is lost
> in concert with body fat during weight loss and it is now generally acknowledged that this rule
> of thumb is an oversimplification. »

> « the metabolizable energy densities of body glycogen, protein and fat are 17.6, 19.7, and 39.5
> MJ/kg, respectively »

> « This results in a calculated energy density for lean body mass changes of ρL = 7.6 MJ/kg. »

> « additional weight loss is predicted to be associated with a lower average cumulative energy
> deficit since a greater proportion of the weight loss is predicted to result from loss of lean
> body mass which has a relatively low energy density in comparison with body fat. The rule of
> thumb approximately matches the predicted energy density of lost weight in obese subjects with
> an initial body fat above 30 kg but overestimates the cumulative energy deficit required per unit
> weight loss for people with lower initial body fat. »

## Conversions et lecture

- 32,2 MJ/kg ÷ 4,184 = **environ 7 700 kcal par kilo** de poids perdu (tissu adipeux à 87 % de
  gras). Gras pur : 39,5 MJ/kg = 9 400 kcal/kg. Masse maigre : 7,6 MJ/kg = 1 800 kcal/kg.
- **La règle des 7 700 kcal/kg ne vaut que pour quelqu'un de gros** (plus de 30 kg de gras). Chez
  quelqu'un de mince, une plus grande part du poids perdu est de la masse maigre, qui porte cinq
  fois moins d'énergie par kilo : le même déficit fait perdre **plus** de poids que la règle ne le
  dit.
- Calculs du livre pour le chapitre 26 : 25 000 à 48 000 kcal de déficit ÷ 7 700 = **3,2 à
  6,2 kg**, au minimum.
