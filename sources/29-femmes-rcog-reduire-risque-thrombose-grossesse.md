## Extraits intégraux

p. 1 :

> « A deep vein thrombosis (DVT) is a blood clot that forms in a deep vein of the leg, calf or pelvis. »

> « Pregnancy increases your risk of a DVT, with the highest risk being just after you have had your
> baby. However, venous thrombosis is still uncommon in pregnancy or in the first 6 weeks after birth,
> occurring in only 1–2 in 1000 women. »

p. 1-2 :

> « Venous thrombosis can be serious because the blood clot may break off and travel in the bloodstream
> until it gets lodged in another part of the body, such as the lung. This is called a pulmonary embolism
> (PE) and can be life threatening. »

> « The symptoms of a PE can include: sudden unexplained difficulty in breathing; tightness in the chest
> or chest pain; coughing up blood (haemoptysis); feeling very unwell or collapsing. »

p. 2, facteurs de risque :

> « During pregnancy — If you: [...] become dehydrated or less mobile in pregnancy due to, for example,
> vomiting in early pregnancy, being in hospital with a severe infection [...]; are immobile for long
> periods of time, for example after an operation or when travelling for 4 hours or longer (by air, car
> or train); have pre-eclampsia »

> « After the birth of your baby — If you: have a very long labour (more than 24 hours) or have had a
> caesarean section, lose a lot of blood after you have had your baby or receive a blood transfusion. »

p. 3 :

> « You may be able to reduce your risk, as most DVTs and PEs that occur during pregnancy and after birth
> are preventable. »

p. 5, *What happens after birth?* :

> « It is important to be as mobile as possible after you have had your baby and to avoid becoming
> dehydrated. »

> « You may be advised to have heparin for 7–10 days after birth or sometimes for 6 weeks after birth. »

## Ce que la fiche ne dit pas

Aucune position ni aucun geste sur place pour une embolie pulmonaire : la fiche renvoie à l'aide
médicale immédiate. Recherche du 27 septembre (« pulmonary embolism first aid position ») : seulement des
sites de secourisme non officiels, qui se contredisent sur la position ; rien d'officiel.

- **Consultée le :** 27 septembre 2026 ; **copie :** `29-femmes-rcog-diagnostic-traitement-thrombose.html`

Extrait intégral :

> « What is the treatment for venous thrombosis? If your doctor suspects that you have a venous
> thrombosis, you will be advised to start on treatment with an injection of a drug called heparin to
> thin the blood. [...] For most women, the benefits of heparin are that it: works to prevent the clot
> getting any bigger so your body can gradually dissolve the clot; reduces the risk of a PE; reduces the
> risk of another venous thrombosis developing; lowers the risk of long-term problems developing in the
> leg. »
