# La grenaille d'acier dans les vieux fusils à choke serré

- **Chapitre qui s'en sert :** 35b (§5-A, sous le tableau)
- **Date de consultation :** 2026-09-24 (pages téléchargées, texte extrait)
- **Langue :** anglais
- **Nature :** une page de fabricant (Browning) et une page de NRA Women (association américaine).
  **Aucune source officielle trouvée** ; le vérificateur technique n'avait trouvé que des forums.

---

## 1. Browning — « Can I shoot steel shot in my Browning shotgun? »

- **URL :** https://www.browning.com/support/faq/shoot-steel-shot.html

> « All current Browning shotguns with the Standard Invector, Invector-Plus and DS choke tube
> systems are fully steel shot compatible with current factory loads. However, there are
> limitations to the compatibility of many older Browning shotguns with conventionally choked
> barrels. In certain models, shooting steel shot may cause a slight "ring bulge" just inches
> behind the muzzle or irreversible damage or harm to the shooter depending on the firearm. »

> « However, we do not recommend the use of Invector full or extra full chokes with steel shot.
> They pattern too tightly, and sometimes result in a "blown" pattern. »

## 2. NRA Women — « How Bad Is It … to Shoot Steel Shot Out of a Vintage Shotgun? »

- **Auteure :** Jo Deering
- **URL :** https://www.nrawomen.com/content/how-bad-is-it-to-shoot-steel-shot-out-of-a-vintage-shotgun

> « Generally speaking, old shotguns [...] were built to shoot lead shot, not the much harder steel
> shot popular for certain types of hunting »

> « The ring bulge is a cosmetic problem, of course, but it can also create a safety issue if you
> continue to shoot the gun, as a problem in the barrel is certainly not going to get better. »

> « A major part of the problem with old shotguns and steel shot is the choke. Steel through a
> full-choked gun just doesn't work well, resulting in blown patterns and that ring bulge, which is
> a result of the shot literally pounding out the barrel in the area where it begins to
> constrict. »

> « Many older guns have fixed chokes, and if they're fixed full, you should avoid steel completely.
> Some research out of England, where non-toxic shot is the rule, not the exception, indicates that
> regular steel shot out of older barrels is safe as long as the gun uses a Modified choke or
> something more open than Mod. This applies only to standard steel; loads larger than No. 4, or
> with pressures in excess of 10,733 psi, or with velocity higher than 1,400 fps in a 1-ounce load
> or 1,250 fps in a 1 1/8-ounce load are considered "high-performance" steel loads and should not be
> used in a shotgun that's not specifically marked as proofed for steel shot. »

> « In the U.S., any gun made since the late 1980s is in the clear for steel, as they were made with
> steel in mind. »

> « Alternatively, you can shoot bismuth loads out of an old gun safely, because bismuth is softer
> than steel and won't damage old barrels. »

## Ce que le chapitre 35b en tire

| Fait | Où |
| --- | --- |
| L'acier, bien plus dur que le plomb, **bat le canon là où il se resserre** et le renfle juste derrière le choke | §5-A |
| Choke fixe plein : pas d'acier | §5-A |
| Dans une arme qui n'a pas été faite pour l'acier : acier ordinaire seulement, **choke modifié ou plus ouvert**, **plombs n° 4 ou plus petits** | §5-A |
| Le bismuth, plus mou, n'abîme pas un vieux canon | §5-A |
| Un canon renflé ne s'arrange pas en tirant | Note 1 du §5-G |

## Ce que le chapitre ne reprend pas

- Les seuils de pression et de vitesse des charges « haute performance » (10 733 lb/po², 1 400 et
  1 250 pi/s) : ils ne s'écrivent pas sur la boîte de façon lisible au chalet ; le chapitre dit
  « charge ordinaire ».
- « Faire vérifier par l'armurier ou le fabricant » : il n'y a personne à appeler.
- L'année (fin des années 1980 aux États-Unis) : on ne sait pas toujours l'âge d'un fusil hérité, et
  la date ne vaut pas pour un fusil fait ailleurs.
