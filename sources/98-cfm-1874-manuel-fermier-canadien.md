## Extraits intégraux (OCR corrigé seulement là où il cassait un mot)

**Pomme de terre sur terre neuve (p. 210)**

> « Swamps, when reclaimed and thoroughly relieved of stagnant water, have produced immense crops ;
> while new lands, especially where the timber fallows have been burnt, being richly filled with
> potash, are very superior lands for the potato. Indeed, owing partly to the hoe cultivation that it
> must necessarily receive, and where the top soil is bound in by a hard sod, potatoes are the very
> best crop to grow on new land for the first few years. »

> « No crop that we know of (except the clover) is so certain to leave the land in good heart. [...]
> a good crop of this tuber is certain to be followed by a heavy cereal crop. »

**Comparaison de fumures (p. 211-212, lettre d'un cultivateur)**

> « Recapitulation.—Barn dressing, one ; hen manure and plaster, one and one-fourth ; leached ashes,
> one-half ; nothing, one-third. »

> « My potatoes were but little affected by the rot [...] but where a low place occurred the rot was
> particularly at home. »

**Planter (p. 212-213)**

> « Drills should be twenty-eight inches apart. »

> « A common mode practised in Ireland [...] is that known as the lazy-bed fashion, which consists in
> planting the sets in beds of a few feet in width, covered from trenches formed with the spade. The
> manure is spread upon the land when ready for planting, and the beds are formed of various
> widths—from three to five and seven feet—the trenches being dug to a width of eighteen inches to
> three feet, and to the depth of one foot to twenty inches, according to the nature of the soil. The
> sets are then placed in drills upon the dung, nine or ten inches apart, and at various distances
> between the rows, and covered with soil from the trenches. About three weeks afterwards, as soon as
> the sprouts begin to appear, the beds are covered equally with two or three inches of dirt, dug
> also out of the trenches. This operation [...] has also a tendency to check the growth of, and
> indeed in a great measure to destroy, the couch and other weeds [...] and it is repeated, so far as
> earthing up the plants, without covering them after bloom. »

**Semence (p. 214-219)**

> « It makes no difference how potatoes are cut for planting, The sprouts may be broken off, and they
> will grow again. [...] The eyes may be cut out and planted, and the potato used for food, as is
> often done when they are scarce and dear. »

> « Of one thing we are convinced, that it is a sad error to pick out the small potatoes and plant
> them for seed. »

> « Of one thing we must be careful, not to let sprouts grow too long on potatoes before planting ;
> they weaken the vitality of the seed. »

> « The after-culture consists simply of the free use of the horse hoe and moulding up the potatoes,
> all of which should be done for the last time before the potatoes bloom. »

Tableau (p. 219) « showing amount of seed potatoes required, when cut or uncut, and when set at
different distances apart, in drills twenty-eight inches from crown to crown » (pommes de terre de
semence de 2 onces) : entières à 6, 9, 12, 18, 24 po : **77, 50, 38, 26, 19** boisseaux/acre ;
coupées en deux à 6, 9, 12, 18 po : **38, 25, 19, 13** ; en quatre à 6, 9, 12 po : **19, 13, 10** ;
en cinq à 6, 9 po : **15, 10** ; en six à 6 po : **13**.

> « [...] one hundred and fifty-five bushels, which is probably the average yield in Canada under good
> cultivation. »

**Récolte et garde (p. 216)**

> « All potatoes should be dug as soon as thoroughly ripe , that is, as soon as the tops can be
> detached by pulling from the bulbs. They should be left upon the surface of the ground, if the
> weather be open, until the earth upon them is perfectly dry. Upon lighter lands, two hours will
> often suffice for this purpose. »

**Orge (p. 180-182)**

> « It is a very tender plant, and will not stand the slightest amount of coldness in the soil. [...]
> when once barley has been committed to the earth, it must either grow or rot ; if the soil be too
> cold to allow of its rapid germination and steady growth, then will it most assuredly rot in the
> ground. »

> « Barley is probably the most shallow-rooted crop that we have. [...] cultivation to the depth of
> three or four inches is ample. »

> « The usual position of barley in all rotations is after hoed crops. »

> « [...] the seed-bed of barley can never be in a fit state for reception of the seed until after a
> fall of warm spring rains. »

> « The quantity of seed varies in Canada from two bushels to two and a half bushels per acre when
> sown broadcast, and about one and a half bushels per acre with the drill. »

> « Barley is the most troublesome grain that we have to harvest. A single rain will destroy its
> colour after it has been cut [...] It is well, in harvesting barley loose, to put it into
> moderate-sized cocks in the field, for such as have been so cocked will not sweat in the mow or
> stack. »

**Avoine (p. 183-184)**

> « It is observed, that oats standing thinly are far more liable to rust than when the ground is
> well covered. [...] The best crop of oats that we ever grew was broadcasted, at the rate of three
> bushels per acre. »

**Sarrasin (p. 186-187)**

> « Buckwheat matures very rapidly; nine to ten weeks being the usual length of time between
> germination and maturity. »

> « If, again, the crop mature too late, it may be caught by late frosts and utterly ruined »

> « Where it must be thrashed by hand, a floor may be prepared in the field, by scraping and sweeping
> a piece of ground or by laying down sheets. Lay over this a bed of rails an inch or two apart,
> raised from the ground sufficiently to make room for the grain when it is thrashed. On the rails
> throw the straw as it is brought from the stacks, and thrash out the grain, which falls through
> the openings between the rails. [...] It is absolutely necessary to clean buckwheat as soon as
> thrashed, or the chaff being damp will heat and spoil the grain in a few days. »

**Pois (p. 188)**

> « Peas should be sown heavily [...] From 2 bushels with drill, to 2½ bushels and 3 bushels with the
> hand, are the best quantities of seed. »

> « Early-sown peas will usually bring a heavier yield, but are more subject to bugs. »
