## 1. Huss H.H. (dir.), *Quality and quality changes in fresh fish*, FAO Fisheries Technical Paper 348, 1995 — ch. 5, « Postmortem changes in fish »

- **URL :** https://www.fao.org/4/v7180e/v7180e06.htm
- **Page enregistrée :** `sources/70-fao-huss-1995-changements-post-mortem.htm`

### Extraits intégraux

> « Immediately after death the muscle is totally relaxed and the limp elastic texture usually
> persists for some hours, whereafter the muscle will contract. When it becomes hard and stiff the
> whole body becomes inflexible and the fish is in rigor mortis. This condition usually lasts for a
> day or more and then rigor resolves. »

> « The rate in onset and resolution of rigor varies from species to species and is affected by
> temperature, handling, size and physical condition of the fish (Table 5.1). »

> « Rigor mortis starts immediately or shortly after death if the fish is starved and the glycogen
> reserves are depleted, or if the fish is stressed. »

> « Stunning and killing by hypothermia (the fish is killed in iced water) give the fastest onset of
> rigor, while a blow on the head gives a delay of up to 18 hours »

> « In rigor the fish body will be completely stiff; the filleting yield will be very poor, and
> rough handling can cause gaping. »

## 2. Graham J., Johnston W.A., Nicholson F.J., *Ice in fisheries*, FAO Fisheries Technical Paper 331, 1992

- **URL :** https://www.fao.org/4/t0713e/t0713e00.htm (ch. 4 : …/t0713e04.htm ; ch. 8 : …/t0713e08.htm)
- **Pages enregistrées :** `sources/70-fao-1992-ice-in-fisheries-ch4-vitesse-refroidissement.htm`,
  `sources/70-fao-1992-ice-in-fisheries-ch8-arrimage.htm`

### Extraits intégraux

Ch. 8 :

> « Iced fish should not be in too thick a layer in a box or a fishroom without some intermediate
> support, because the bottom fish will be crushed and damaged, and also lose a significant amount
> of weight. Boxes should be shallow and bulk stowage and stowage in large containers should have
> supporting shelves at intervals of not more than 0.5 m. »

> « The advantage of keeping bulk-stowed fish in shallow layers not more than 0.5 m deep will be
> lost if fish and boards rest […] »

> « Stowage in a box should consist of a bottom layer of ice, about 5 cm deep, layers of fish
> sprinkled with ice, and a final top layer of ice again about 5 cm deep. »

> « For long trips in temperate climates, about one tonne of ice should be used for every 2 tonnes
> of fish »

Ch. 4, tableau 4, « Time taken to cool fish in the centre of a box with ice top and bottom » —
épaisseur de la couche de poisson (cm) / temps pour passer de 10 °C à 2 °C au centre (h) :

> 7,5 cm : 2 h · 10 cm : 4 h · 12,5 cm : 6,5 h · 15 cm : 9 h · 20 cm : 14 h · 60 cm : 120 h

> « it takes about 6 h for fish at the centre of a 10 cm layer to reach 0.5°C »

## Ce que ces pages NE disent PAS

- Aucune épaisseur propre à l'éperlan : le 0,5 m vaut pour le poisson sous glace en général.
