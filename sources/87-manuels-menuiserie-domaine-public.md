## A. William Fairham, *Woodwork Joints: How They Are Set Out, How Made and Where Used*

- **Éditeur :** Evans Brothers, Londres ; le texte cite *The Woodworker* de janvier 1927
- **URL :** https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/21531 (texte : https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/21531/pg21531.txt)

### Proportion du tenon et de la mortaise

> « GENERAL RULE.--In practically all cases where a single tenon is used the thickness of the
> tenon should be one-third the thickness of the timber. This leaves the timber at each side of
> the mortise the same strength as the tenon. »

> « THE TENONS (as already stated) are generally one-third the thickness of the timber, thus
> leaving the same amount of substance at each side of the tenon as the tenon itself is composed
> of. »

### Chevillage à tire (« drawbore pinning »)

> « DRAWBORE PINNING.--At Fig. 162 is seen the method of securing a tenon by drawbore pinning,
> employed when it is not convenient to obtain the necessary pressure by using a cramp. The joint
> is made in the usual manner, and a 3/8-in. twist bit is used to bore a hole through piece A.
> The tenon is driven home and the hole is marked on the side of the tenon (B); the tenon is then
> withdrawn and the hole bored about 1/8 in. nearer to the shoulder than as marked on the
> separate diagram at C. When the tenon is finally inserted the holes will not register
> correctly, and if a hardwood pin be driven into the joint it will draw the shoulders of the
> tenon to a close joint and effectually secure the parts. »

### Perçage de la mortaise avant ciselage

> « The twist bit should be about 1/8 in. less in diameter than the width between the gauge
> lines G. »

## B. William Noyes, *Handwork in Wood* (1910)

- **URL :** https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/20846 (texte : https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/20846/pg20846.txt)

> « This should be of the same width as the thickness of the tenons, which is ordinarily
> one-third of the thickness of the frame. »

> « The mortise should not come too near the end, or the portion of wood outside it will shear
> out. »

> « It is common to loosen up the wood by first boring a series of adjoining holes whose centers
> follow the center-line of the mortise and whose diameter is slightly less than the width of the
> mortise. »

> « The method is to begin at the middle of the mortise, placing the chisel--which should be as
> wide as the mortise--at right angles to the grain of the wood. »

> « Finally test the tenon in the mortise noting carefully where it pinches, if anywhere, and trim
> carefully. The tighter it fits without danger of splitting the mortised member, the stronger
> will be the joint. »

## C. Edwin W. Foster, *Carpentry and Woodwork* (1911)

- **URL :** https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/43574 (texte : https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/43574/pg43574.txt)

> « The hole for the pin or dowel is first bored across the centre of the mortise. The tenon is
> inserted snugly, and the bit again inserted until it makes a mark on the tenon. The tenon is
> withdrawn, and the centre of bit placed 1/16 inch nearer the shoulder than the mark made, and
> the hole bored. »

> « The three holes through which the pin is to pass are not in line when the tenon is again
> placed in mortise. The result is to draw the tenon tight, when the pin is driven home, and to
> make the joint a very snug one, as it should be. »

## D. Charles G. Wheeler, *Wood-working for Beginners: A Manual for Amateurs* (1899)

- **URL :** https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/43604 (texte : https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/43604/pg43604.txt)

### Chevillage à tire et son excès

> « The hole for the pin is not bored through the tenon as just shown, but is bored a trifle
> nearer the shoulder of the tenon than the other holes (in the mortise-piece). The result is
> that when the pin is driven through it draws the tenon-piece down to a snug fit at the
> shoulder. But this has to be done with judgment. If the hole in the tenon is too much out of
> line, driving the pin through tends to split (strictly speaking to _shear_) the end of the
> tenon, and too much strain is put on the pin. »

### La cheville : pointe, taille

> « The pin should be slightly pointed before driving, on the same principle that the end of the
> tenon is bevelled. It is not necessary to round the pin. An eight-sided one is just as good. »

> « Do not use too large pins. [...] For such work as pinning a joint in a chair, you will not
> need anything larger than a 1/4" hardwood pin. »

> « If you put it too far from the edge, its hold on the tenon will be weak and the end of the
> tenon may break out (shear). If you put it too near the edge, the sides of the mortise may tear
> or split out. »

### La filière à chevilles

> « =Dowel-Plate.=--A steel plate with various holes of such sizes that pins made by driving
> blocks of wood through them will drive snugly into the holes made by the corresponding bits. »

### L'équerrage aux diagonales

> « there are many cases in which measuring diagonals is a good test, altering the angles of the
> work until the two opposite diagonals are equal, when the work will, of course, be rectangular.
> This is a good way for large "case" work, using a stick, or fitting two adjustable sticks [...]
> between the angles, when the latter can be altered until the diagonals are equal. »

## Ce que ces quatre livres établissent pour le chapitre 87

| Point du chapitre | Ce que disent les manuels |
| --- | --- |
| Mortaise d'environ le tiers de l'épaisseur (§5.3, étape 11) | **Confirmé** deux fois (Fairham, Noyes), avec la raison : autant de bois de chaque côté que dans le tenon. Noyes : le ciseau « as wide as the mortise » |
| Décalage du chevillage à tire (§5.6, étape 29 ; §6) | **1/16 po (1,6 mm)** chez Foster, **environ 1/8 po (3,2 mm)** chez Fairham. Soit environ **1,5 à 3 mm** |
| Trop de décalage cisaille (§5.6, étape 30) | **Confirmé** (Wheeler) |
| Pointe de la cheville (§5.6, étape 27) | « Slightly pointed » : **confirmé**, sans longueur |
| Diamètre de cheville (§5.6, étape 28) | « Do not use too large pins » ; **6 mm (1/4 po) de bois dur suffit pour une chaise**. **Aucune proportion** au tenon |
| Filière en plaque d'acier (§5.6, étape 26) | **Confirmé** (Wheeler, « dowel-plate ») |
| Diagonales égales (§5.5, étape 24 ; §6) | **Confirmé**, mesurées au bâton. **Aucune tolérance chiffrée** |

## Ce que ces livres NE disent PAS

- Aucune **profondeur supplémentaire** de mortaise borgne par rapport au tenon.
- Aucun **jeu chiffré** dans la largeur du tenon.
- Aucune **longueur** de pointe de cheville.
- Aucune **tolérance** sur l'égalité des diagonales.
- Aucune **profondeur d'entaille** sous un brêlage (vérifié aussi dans Dana et Pearl, *The Use of
  Ropes and Tackle*, 1918, https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/56585, qui décrit les brêlages et les
  frappes sans entaille chiffrée).

## Hors drapeau, signalé en passant

Fairham (« about 1/8 in. less in diameter ») et Noyes (« slightly less than the width of the
mortise ») percent la file de trous **un peu plus petite** que la largeur de la mortaise. Le
chapitre, §5.5 étape 16, écrit « mèche de la largeur de la mortaise ».
