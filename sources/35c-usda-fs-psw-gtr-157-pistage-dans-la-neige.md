## 1. Conditions de neige — extraits intégraux (domaine public)

> « The minimum requirement is snow deep and soft enough for identifiable footprints to register.
> If possible, wait until the second morning after a snowfall to allow tracks to accumulate. »

> « tracking during snowfall or during strong winds is not advised because tracks are quickly
> obscured. »

> « In early spring, the sun melts snow on south-facing slopes, and this can rapidly destroy
> tracks each morning. [...] Later, when the snow freezes, animals may move on top of it without
> leaving detectable tracks. During periods of melting and freezing, tracking must be done early
> in the morning. When recurring melting and freezing prevent tracking on south-facing slopes,
> good tracking may be possible on the north-facing slopes. »

> « The tracks of target species traveling on packed trails made by ungulates or snowshoe hares
> can easily be missed! »

## 2. Morphologie — extraits

> « Canid toes are nearly symmetrical in size and position on the foot [...] The long axes of the
> toes of canids are nearly parallel, a pattern rarely seen in felids and mustelids. Felid toes
> form a shallow, asymmetric arc [...] The toes of mustelids tend to be grouped in a 1-3-1
> spacing; when the small, medial toe does not show, a 1-3 pattern is typical. »

> « A bilobate anterior edge on the interdigital pad positively identifies a print as that of a
> cat. [...] An asymmetric, chevron-shaped interdigital pad is characteristic of the Mustelidae;
> red fox also have a chevron, but it is symmetrical. [...] The feet of all carnivores become
> hairier during the cold season, which obscures detail left in tracks. Lynx feet remain
> relatively hairy in the summer. »

Tableau 1 : les canidés ont l'avant plus grand, quatre doigts, griffes « presque toujours » ; les
félins l'avant plus grand, quatre doigts, griffes « rarement » ; les ursidés l'arrière plus grand,
cinq doigts (quatre dans une empreinte médiocre), talon complet.

## 3. La neige transforme la trace — extraits intégraux

> « the metamorphosed tracks of a bobcat or coyote can easily be misidentified as those of a lynx
> by the inexperienced or unprepared tracker. »

> « Tracks undergoing metamorphism may enlarge and be distorted in one dimension or both.
> Enlargement can be dramatic, with prints increasing up to four times in area. [...] During
> melting, maximum distortion occurs in portions of the track opposite the sun, usually the
> northeast part of the track. Distortion from sublimation occurs mostly on the downwind edge of
> the track [...] toe imprints will join and eventually merge with the interdigital and heel
> pads. »

> « Settling occurs within the snowpack because of gravity. Because snow sticks to vegetation,
> inverted cones around tree trunks indicate settling. The effect of settling is to shrink, and,
> in extreme cases, to destroy a track, often in a matter of hours. »

> « If inverted cones around tree trunks are visible, suspect reduced track sizes, and seek
> sheltered places, such as under the canopy of trees or shrubs, to measure prints. »

> « In sublimated snow, track edges appear well rounded but dry. »

## 4. Allures — extraits intégraux

> « A stride is one cycle of locomotion and is measured as the distance from where a point on a
> foot touches the surface to the next spot where the same point on the same foot touches the
> surface [...] The stride of a walking animal approximates the distance from the hip to the
> shoulder and provides an estimate of the length of the animal. »

> « Walking [...] Tracks generally appear in a line, and hind prints tend to register directly on
> top of front prints. The more the animal relies on stealth, the more often the prints register
> with the hind print directly on top of the front print [...] Lynx, for example, usually show
> direct registry. »

> « Trotting [...] The trail pattern appears the same as that of the walk, but the stride is longer
> and the straddle tends to be narrower in the trot [...] All front prints register on one side of
> the line of travel, and all hind prints register on the opposite side [...] This side trot is
> commonly shown by canids »

> « Misreading a trot for a walk results in overestimating the size of an animal; a bobcat trail
> becomes that of a lynx, or a coyote becomes a wolf. To avoid this mistake, follow the trail and
> look for an area where the animal does not appear to be hurrying. [...] Measure the gait pattern
> where the stride is shortest and the trail relatively straight. »

> « carnivores seldom gallop long distances with consistent track spacing [...] In contrast, a walk
> or trot will continue with the same, even track spacing for long distances. »

> « Red fox (Vulpes vulpes) often use the 2x side trot [...] and leave prints that are about the
> same size as marten tracks. »

> « Often mustelids drag their front feet in the fast gallop, leaving a "dumbbell-shaped" track
> pattern. If the dumbbell pattern is not evident, look for alternating short and long spaces
> between track impressions »

## 5. Mesurer — extraits

> « Track size is influenced by the depth that the foot sinks into the surface; feet leave larger
> footprints in soft substrates than in hard ones. »

> « Center measurements are important because they are easily recorded and change little with
> metamorphosed snow. To obtain center measurements, mark the center of each footprint with a
> small dot [...] Lay a ruler between print centers on one side of the trail to measure the
> stride. »

## 6. Lynx — extraits et chiffres

- Tableau 2 (cm, contour variable « VO ») : **avant 8,7 × 9,0 cm** ; arrière 7,7 × 9,4 cm.
  « The length of the front print is generally less than or equal to the width ».
- > « Although lynx weigh about 10 kg, and mountain lions up to 75 kg, their prints are about the
  > same size. Lynx prints are usually poorly defined because of the densely haired foot. »
- > « Lynx trails are characterized by conspicuous troughs even in soft snow [...] They typically
  > use two gaits, the walk and the bound, although the walk is by far the more common. [...] The
  > walking stride averages 71.9 cm (SD = 8.9, n = 11) [...] Straddle averages 21.2 cm [...] The
  > trough averages 26.9 cm »
- > « Trails through open, mature forest are typically straight [...] Trails through
  > earlier-successional habitat typically meander, possibly indicating searching for prey »
- > « Scats are seldom buried by adults and are often found in the center of trails and at trail
  > intersections [...] Lynx cache remains of kills, which typically appear as mounds of snow or
  > debris »
- Lits : les lits de repos longs sont des cuvettes **nettes, arrondies, à croûte de glace** ; les
  lits de chasse courts sont mal définis, sans croûte.
- > « trackers have reported lynx tracks on top of their own »
- > « Lynx tracks may be distinguished from wolf [...] and dog tracks by their more round shape,
  > their lack of definition because of their densely-haired foot, the usually large hairy
  > interdigital pad, asymmetrically placed and sized toe pads, lack of claws, and the bi-lobed
  > anterior edge of the interdigital pad. Lynx seldom lope or gallop as wolves do. »
- > « try to judge whether the depth of the track is that of a 10-kg or 50-kg animal »

## 7. Mustélidés, pékan, martre, loutre — extraits

- > « Toes typically show a 1-3-1 grouping [...] When only four toes show, the 1-3 grouping is
  > diagnostic. »
- Loutre : > « River otter trails in the snow will often show slide marks of 1 to 5 m in
  > length. »
- Pékan contre martre : le pékan marche davantage, suit le dessus des billots, fait des pistes plus
  droites, creuse un sillon dans la neige molle, traîne les pieds et la queue ; la martre
  **creuse souvent des tunnels sous la neige** près des souches et des chablis ; sa piste est
  **erratique et se recoupe** ; elle marque en frottant son ventre sur ce qui dépasse la neige.
  > « Scats composed entirely of porcupine quills suggest that the trail was produced by a
  > fisher. »
- Martre : empreinte avant d'un adulte d'environ **5 × 4 cm** (« from about 5 x 4 cm (marten) to
  16 x 11 cm (wolverine) »).
- > « Mink tracks [...] tend to be smaller than marten tracks, though the difference can be
  > slight, and mink tend to be restricted to streamcourses. »

## 8. Lire sur le terrain — extraits

> « When a set of tracks is spotted, STOP and THINK! [...] Step back and look at the whole scene.
> Where does the trail originate and lead to? Where can you walk without destroying clues? »

> « S = Setting: geography and habitat? T = Time: year and day? S = Surface? »

> « Because a slip in the snow on a hill can destroy tracks, it is best to approach from the
> downhill side. »

> « It often helps to cast a shadow over a sunlit track that is difficult to see. [...] Tracks that
> are not visible on the way out in the morning may be prominent when you return in the afternoon.
> When possible, track by going out and back on the same route. »

> « Winter light is often "flat" [...] light from a flashlight directed at a low angle across the
> tracks » (aide)

> « The depression must be checked by feeling with bare fingers, using the "pedestal test" to
> reveal the true size of tracks [...] excavate snow from a circle around the track. Blow loose
> snow off the pedestal. Then, with your bare fingers, carefully excavate the remaining snow to
> reveal the original footprint. The compacted footprint on the pedestal will be the best possible
> rendition of the original track. »

> « Try to locate at least three front and three hind prints so measurements may be averaged. »

## 9. Sécurité (p. 116) — ce qui est dit

Éviter de travailler seul l'hiver ; porter de quoi passer la nuit. (Consigne d'institution ; le
chapitre n'en reprend rien.)

## Ce que le chapitre en tire

- Les trois pièges de la neige : l'empreinte **qui grossit** au soleil ou au vent (jusqu'à 4 fois
  la surface), **qui rapetisse** par tassement (cônes inversés au pied des arbres), et le trot pris
  pour un pas qui fait un loup d'un coyote : §3, §5-A, §7.
- Mesurer là où la bête **ne se pressait pas** ; mesurer de centre à centre : §5-A, §9.
- La foulée de pas ≈ distance hanche-épaule : §3, §9.
- Lynx : 8,7 × 9,0 cm, foulée de pas 71,9 cm en moyenne, sillon, crottes non enterrées au milieu
  des pistes, caches, lits à croûte de glace : fiche lynx.
- Martre : tunnels, piste erratique ; loutre : glissades de 1 à 5 m : fiches.
- Le renard au trot de côté fait des empreintes de la taille d'une martre : confusions.
- Le test du piédestal : §9.
- L'aller-retour par la même route pour voir la trace sous deux lumières : §5-A, §9.
