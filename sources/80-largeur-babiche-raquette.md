# Largeur de la babiche : ce que disent Turner (1894) et un fabricant de raquettes de l'Alaska

- **Consultées le :** 24 septembre 2026
- **Capture :** de première main, `curl` HTTP 200 ; texte Gutenberg ; PDF extrait avec `pdftotext`.
- **Sert au chapitre :** 80 — Babiche, tendons, cordage, §5-D étape 18 et §6 (ligne « Babiche, à
  la coupe »).
- **Rang :** aucune source officielle ne donne de largeur de babiche. Les deux documents sont une
  ethnographie publiée par le Bureau of Ethnology de la Smithsonian Institution (1894, domaine
  public) et le témoignage écrit d'un fabricant de raquettes de l'Alaska. Voir aussi
  `sources/20b-babiche-orignal-ou-caribou-sources-divergentes.md`, qui résumait déjà le second.

## 1. Lucien M. Turner, *Ethnology of the Ungava District, Hudson Bay Territory* (1894)

- **Éditeur :** Eleventh Annual Report of the Bureau of Ethnology, Smithsonian Institution,
  Washington, 1894. Domaine public.
- **URL :** https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/39659 (texte : https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/39659/pg39659.txt)
- **Copie :** `sources/80-turner-1894-ethnology-ungava-gutenberg.txt`
- **Peuple décrit :** les Naskapis et Montagnais (Innus) de l'Ungava, que Turner nomme « Nenenot ».

### Extraits intégraux (anglais)

> « The netting is made of deerskin, with the hair removed, and allowed to dry into a condition
> usually known as parchment. This is cut into strips of variable width, depending on the
> particular use for which it is wanted. »

> « A needle of bone, horn, or iron (Fig. 130) is used for netting the snowshoes. […] Various sizes
> of needles are used for the different kinds of netting, of which the meshes differ greatly in
> size. »

> « Each individual varies his work according to fancy, but as the netting between the bars is made
> of coarser line, more compactly woven, there is less difference there than at the toe or heel. »

> « The netting of the toe is of finer line and meshes than the middle or between the bars; while
> that between the heel bar and heel of the snowshoe is finest of all. »

> « the northern stations (Fort Chimo) furnish great numbers of these skins in the parchment
> condition to be purchased by the Mountaineers, who cut them into fine lines for snowshoe netting
> and other purposes. »

> « The thread used in sewing is simply a strip of sinew of the proper size. The fibers are
> separated by splitting off a sufficient amount, and with the finger nail the strip is freed from
> all knots or smaller strands which would prevent drawing through the needle holes. »

## 2. Oliver Cameron avec Ole Wik, « Making Babiche » (2014)

- **Auteur :** Oliver Cameron, fabricant de raquettes de l'Alaska, avec Ole Wik. Illustration
  créditée au Cold Climate Housing Research Center, Fairbanks.
- **URL :** https://www.olivercameron.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/01/Making-Babiche-Oliver-Cameron-010914.pdf
- **Copie :** `sources/80-cameron-wik-2014-making-babiche.pdf` (4 p.)

### Extraits intégraux (anglais)

> « Then I'd cut a hole in the middle of the hide with a good sharp knife. I'd just start going
> round and round inside the hole, cutting a strip maybe 5/32" or 3/16" wide. »

> « There's a way to make a gig or gauge so that you start your string and it stays uniform in
> width all the way along. You cut a slot in the end of a stick and hold your knife over the
> slot. »

> « The hide is dampened enough so it is a little bit flexible. If the skin is damp, the string is
> quite a bit smaller, and then it is pulled during drying, so it's not as hard to cut as if it was
> bone dry. »

> « Sometimes I'll cut a whole hide down the middle and just cut strips along one side, but that
> doesn't give me a long enough piece to do the foot part of a snowshoe. »

> « Next, I cut it round and round for strips. Afterward, since the skins are damp and stretchy, I
> wrap the cord around and around cache legs or trees to dry, pulling until most of the stretch is
> out. »

Sur le coulage (hors drapeau, §5-C) :

> « If I had some extra caribou skins that I wanted to make into string, I would soak them in the
> water when the sun was shining on it to warm it. After a couple of days the hair would begin to
> slip. »

## Ce que ces extraits permettent d'écrire

- **L'ordre des largeurs** (source 1) : la lanière la plus grosse au centre, entre les traverses, là
  où le pied porte ; plus fine à la pointe ; **la plus fine au talon**.
- **Une seule largeur mesurée** (source 2) : environ **5/32 à 3/16 po, soit 4 à 4,8 mm**, coupée en
  spirale dans une peau de caribou **humide**, qui s'amincit encore quand on l'étire au séchage.
  C'est la lanière de la partie du pied de la raquette. Rien pour l'orignal en particulier.
- **Aucune** largeur publiée pour la ligature d'outil ni pour la lanière de gibecière.
- Le gabarit à fente au bout d'un bâton (source 2) est l'équivalent des « deux clous » du chapitre.
