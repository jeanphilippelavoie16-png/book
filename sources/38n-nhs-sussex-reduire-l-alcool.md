- **Organisme :** University Hospitals Sussex NHS Foundation Trust (service public de santé du

- **Titre :** *How to safely reduce your alcohol intake*
- **URL :** https://www.uhsussex.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/How-to-reduce-alcohol-intake.pdf
- **Consulté le :** 2026-09-27
- **Fichier enregistré :** `38n-nhs-sussex-reduire-l-alcool-sans-danger.pdf` (8 pages)
- **Chapitre qui s'en sert :** 38 (nouveau numéro) — Le deuil, le choc et la peur des adultes

## Extraits (mot pour mot)

> « If you start to feel unwell after going several hours without a drink, this means that you
> could be dependent on alcohol and may go into alcohol withdrawal. Signs of alcohol withdrawal
> include: • Shaking • Sweating • Nausea (feeling sick) • Headache. Alcohol withdrawal can have
> serious complications, like confusion, seeing or hearing things (hallucinations) and seizures.
> It can even be fatal if left untreated. »

> « If you are experiencing symptoms do not stop drinking suddenly. Your goal should be to gain
> some control of your drinking and cut down gradually. Try to space out your drinks to manage
> your withdrawal symptoms. »

> « Aim for a stable daily alcohol intake that is planned. Once you have done this for one week,
> start to cut down slowly. Aim to cut down by 10% every four days. If you start to experience
> withdrawal symptoms, this means you are cutting down too quickly. Increase your daily alcohol
> intake to a level where you do not have these symptoms. Aim to keep this steady for one week.
> After that, try to cut down by 10% every week. »

> « If you can, ask another adult to help you: if they can help to measure, monitor and keep the
> alcohol, it will be easier for you. »

> « Be Aware: if you have previously had seizures or experienced hallucinations […] when trying
> to come off alcohol, please get professional help »

La fiche mentionne aussi la thiamine (vitamine B1) : pain et riz complets, comprimés s'ils ont
été prescrits.

## Ce que la fiche ne dit pas

- Rien sur ce qu'on fait quand il n'y a **personne** à appeler : le chapitre garde la
  réduction, et dit franchement que le cas « antécédent de convulsions » n'a pas de solution
  sûre au chalet.
- Le calendrier de réduction est écrit pour un buveur qui veut arrêter ; au chalet, il sert à
  **étaler une réserve qui finit** de façon qu'elle finisse sans sevrage brutal. C'est une
  transposition du livre.
