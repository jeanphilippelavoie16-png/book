## 1. USDA Forest Service — « Spotted water hemlock » (cicutaire maculée)

- **Organisme :** United States Department of Agriculture, Forest Service — *Celebrating
  Wildflowers*, « Plant of the Week »
- **URL :** https://www.fs.usda.gov/wildflowers/plant-of-the-week/cicuta_maculata.shtml
- **Page enregistrée :** `sources/30-usda-fs-cicutaire-maculee.html` (46 756 octets)

> « Ethnobotanist H.D. Harrington once wrote that Water hemlock "has gained the reputation as being
> the most poisonous plant in the North Temperate Zone." Its toxin, called cicutoxin, can cause
> delirium, nausea, convulsions, abdominal pain, seizures, and vomiting within 60 minutes of
> ingestion – frequently leading to death. »

> « All parts of water hemlock are toxic, but the poison is especially virulent from the roots. The
> lower stem and upper roots of Cicuta contain numerous internal partitions or air spaces that can be
> revealed if sliced lengthwise. »

> « The leaflets of Cicuta can be distinguished from similar, non-toxic species in the parsley family
> (Apiaceae or Umbelliferae) by having veins that fork at their tips, with one branch ending at the tip
> of the leaflet and the other in the V-shaped sinus between adjacent leaflet lobes. »

> « True to its common name, water hemlock occurs primarily on wet soils associated with ditches,
> stream banks, pond margins, and marshes. »

## 2. NC State Extension — *Cicuta maculata*

- **Organisme :** North Carolina State University, Cooperative Extension — *Extension Gardener Plant
  Toolbox*
- **URL :** https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/cicuta-maculata/
- **Page enregistrée :** `sources/30-ncsu-cicuta-maculata.html`

> « Water Hemlock is a perennial herb with short tuberous roots and purple-striped or mottled, hollow
> stems with cross-partitions at the nodes and many of these at the base of the stem. »

> « All parts of the plant are poisonous, with the roots more poisonous than the other parts. »

> « Poison Severity: High » — « Poison Symptoms: HIGHLY TOXIC, MAY BE FATAL IF EATEN! Muscle spasms,
> dilated pupils, dizziness, diarrhea, stomach pain, convulsions. Seizures, tremors, fever, bloat,
> respiratory depression » — « Poison Toxic Principle: Cicutoxin and cicutol »

> « Site: Wet meadows, thickets, fresh water swamps, roadside ditches, floodplains »

## 3. NC State Extension — *Actaea rubra* (actée rouge)

- **URL :** https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/actaea-rubra/
- **Page enregistrée :** `sources/30-ncsu-actaea-rubra.html`

> « Problems: Poisonous to Humans | Problem for Cats | Problem for Children | Problem for Dogs »

> « Poison Severity: High » — « Poison Symptoms: Poisonous if ingested. Can cause death in humans and
> may cause death in livestock or pets if eaten in large quantities. Roots and berries are the most
> toxic part of the plant and the berries are very bitter. Symptoms: Gastrointestinal distress and
> burning and blistering of allmucous membranes, lips and mouth. In large quantities heart failure. »
> — « Poison Toxic Principle: cardiotoxic compounds »

> « Fruit Description: The fruit matures to glossy red with a dark spot at the tip. They are about
> 1/3 inch across. »

## 4. NC State Extension — *Heracleum maximum* (berce laineuse)

- **URL :** https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/heracleum-maximum/
- **Page enregistrée :** `sources/30-ncsu-heracleum-maximum.html`

> « Poison Severity: Low » — « Poison Symptoms: Photosensitization (sunburn, dermatitis), eye
> irritation upon exposure to the sap, and subsequent exposure to sunlight. » — « Poison Toxic
> Principle: Furanocoumarins » — « Causes Contact Dermatitis: Yes » — « Poison Part: Sap/Juice »

> « Stem Description: The stem is grooved, hollow, and stout, green with purple splotches. White wooly
> hairs circle the stem at the base of the leaves. They have a strong pungent odor when crushed. »

## 5. Agence européenne des médicaments (EMA) — prêle des champs et prêle des marais

- **Organisme :** European Medicines Agency, Committee on Herbal Medicinal Products (HMPC), agence de
  l'Union européenne
- **Titre :** « Assessment report on *Equisetum arvense* L., herba », EMA/HMPC/278089/2015, 2 février
  2016
- **URL :** https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-report/final-assessment-report-equisetum-arvense-l-herba_en.pdf
- **PDF enregistré :** `sources/30-ema-prele-des-champs-rapport-evaluation.pdf` (392 641 octets)

> « Adulteration with Equisetum palustre, Equisetum hyemale L., Equisetum fluviatile L., Equisetum
> sylvaticum L. is possible. Some Equisetum species such as Equisetum palustre contains potentially
> toxic palustrin (Blaschek et al., 2013). The European Pharmacopoeia 8th edition (2015) contains a
> test of thin-layer chromatography for identification of the herb and specification of the absence
> of Equisetum palustre. Only quality controlled horsetail (from pharmacies) should be consumed by the
> patients, as adulteration with Equisetum palustre is possible, when it is harvested by unqualified
> persons. »

> « A related species Equisetum palustre is toxic, due to the presence of palustrine, hence correct
> plant identification is important. »

(NC State, *Equisetum arvense*, page enregistrée `sources/30-ncsu-equisetum-arvense.html` : la prêle
des champs elle-même contient une thiaminase, « Poison Severity: Low ».)
