## 1. L'outil : pointe au milieu, deux mâchoires qui cèdent et se referment

**Répertoire du patrimoine culturel du Québec — « Harpon à anguille »** (autres noms : fer à fouëne,
nigog), Bonaventure, https://www.patrimoine-culturel.gouv.qc.ca/rpcq/detail.do?methode=consulter&id=213406&type=bien
— copie : `53-foene-rpcq-harpon-a-anguille.html`

> « Il s'agit de la tête d'un harpon en fer de la fin du XIXe siècle. Elle est formée de deux
> mâchoires de 25 centimètres de longueur séparées par une aiguille de fer. Ces pièces sont
> normalement fixées au bout d'un long manche. »
> Dimensions : « Hauteur […] 25 centimètre(s) ; Largeur […] 8 centimètre(s) ».
> « Il est aussi connu sous le nom de nigog, un nom d'origine autochtone. Les Autochtones avaient
> l'habitude de pêcher l'anguille au nigog ou à la nasse. »

→ **25 cm** de mâchoires, **8 cm** de large.

**Le Jeune, *Relation* de 1634 (Montagnais, aujourd'hui Innus)**, traduction anglaise citée par
Musées numériques Canada, « Indigenous Harpoon », https://www.communitystories.ca/v2/la-peche-a-l-anguille_eel-fishing/gallery/1265-2/
— copie : `53-foene-musees-numeriques-le-jeune-1634.html`

> « This harpoon is an instrument composed of a long pole, two or three fingers thick, at the end
> of which they [the Montagnais] fasten a piece of pointed iron, which is provided on both sides
> with two little curved sticks, which almost come together at the end of the iron point. When
> they strike an eel with this harpoon, they impale it upon the iron, the two pieces of stick
> yielding by the force of the blow and allowing the eel to enter; then closing of themselves,
> because they only open through the force of the blow, they prevent the impaled eel from getting
> away. »
> « One of the names attributed to it by French-speakers was nigogue or nigog, which is a word of
> Mi'kmaq origin. »

→ manche de **deux ou trois doigts** ; bouts des bâtons presque joints au bout de la pointe ;
mécanisme : ils cèdent au coup et se referment seuls.

**Tipatshimuna (Nation innue) — fiche « Leister (fish spear) »**, nom français « harpon à
mâchoires », fabricant Pien Penashue, Sheshatshiu, https://tipatshimuna.ca/material-culture/1603-2/
— copie : `53-foene-tipatshimuna-harpon-a-machoires-fiche.html`

> Description : « a fish spear made of wood, metal, string and babiche ».
> « It is also used for fishing. You spear a fish. There is a line that goes with it. The line is
> from a caribou hide. […] This spear is used for fishing for salmon. At the end of the pole, you
> make a gap with a sharpened blade like metal, and it is placed in the middle of the gap of the
> pole. » (Shimun Michel et Manian Ashini Michel)
> « The Innu used this spear like [a] pole with spike and two sticks curved on each side to hold
> the fish when it is speared. » (Joseph Abraham, 1979)
> « […] a long pole and attached at the front were two curved sticks with a spike in the middle
> where the fish were speared. This was done in the night times, using the bark of the birch
> trees as a torch. » (Simeo Penashue, 1979)
> « […] they used to go after the salmon in summer when dusk sets in. They would use a torch
> (using bark of a birch tree) so they could see the salmon swimming along. » (Jerome Pokue, 1978)

→ bout du manche refendu, pointe logée au milieu de la fente ; deux bâtons courbes ; ligne de peau.

**Institut culturel Avataq — « Our tools »**, https://www.avataq.qc.ca/en/Nunavimmiuts/node_1723/node_1762
— copie : `53-foene-avataq-kakivak.html`

> « kakivak: fish spear (a type of harpoon) with a central point flanked by two arched pieces of
> ivory tipped with barbs. Once the fish was impaled on the central point, the arched pieces kept
> it from slipping off. »

→ les mâchoires portent une dent (barbe) ; ce sont elles qui empêchent le poisson de glisser.

**Denys, 1672 — la pointe seule qui lâche** (Mi'kmaq, Acadie), Pictou-Antigonish Regional Library,
http://www.parl.ns.ca/nicolasdenys/pages28_32.htm — copie : `53-foene-denys-1672-flambeau-saumon-truite.html`

> « He who is standing up has in his hand a harpoon, which is the same as that used for Beaver […]
> So soon as he saw a fish passing he speared at it, and rarely missed. But sometimes the spear did
> not take hold, for want of catching on some bone; thus they lost their fish. »

→ ce que les mâchoires corrigent : le harpon simple perd le poisson quand il n'accroche pas d'os.

## 2. Où et quand : eau peu profonde, flambeau d'écorce, canot à deux

**Denys, 1672** (même page) :

> « The Indians used these torches also for fishing the Salmon and the Salmon Trout […] After having
> thus ascended, they disport themselves in these pools. […] To these places of rest the Indians
> went at night with their canoes and their torches. […] Being there, they lighted a torch. The
> Salmon or the Trout, seeing the fire which shines upon the water, come wheeling around the
> canoe. »
> Sur les torches : « torches which they made of Birch bark; these burn more brightly than torches
> of wax ».

**Tipatshimuna — exposition « Leister »**, https://tipatshimuna.ca/exhibits/leister-uashuakanashk%E1%B5%98/
— copie : `53-foene-tipatshimuna-harpon-a-machoires-expo.html`

> « […] a special kind of fish spear or leister that grips the fish over a sharp dart when it is
> struck. […] Innu fishers would paddle out in their canoes after dark and set birch bark torches
> ablaze. Salmon would be attracted to the light and would swim towards the surface enabling the
> fishers to strike them with the spears. »
> Légende : « Innu birch bark torch and […] (leister) used for night fishing in the Pakut-shipu area
> (St. Augustin, Quebec). 1947-57. »

**Répertoire du patrimoine culturel du Québec — « Pêche à l'anguille »**,
https://www.patrimoine-culturel.gouv.qc.ca/rpcq/detail.do?methode=consulter&id=78&type=imma
— copie : `53-foene-rpcq-peche-a-l-anguille.html`

> « La nigogue ou fouëne, semblable au harpon, est l'autre technique de plusieurs nations
> autochtones. Elle est efficace pour divers poissons, dont l'anguille. À la noirceur, un flambeau
> est placé à l'extrémité d'un canot. Sa lumière attire la proie qui est dardée. »
> « Les Madelinots pêchent également l'anguille jaune sur la glace au moyen d'une fouine ou
> fouène. »

**Musées numériques Canada — « Les techniques autochtones et européennes »** (Montpetit, 1867),
https://www.histoiresdecheznous.ca/v2/la-peche-a-l-anguille_eel-fishing/histoire/les-techniques-primitives/
— copie : `53-foene-musees-numeriques-techniques-autochtones.html`

> « Ils capturaient ainsi plusieurs espèces de poissons en eau peu profonde et principalement la
> nuit. »
> « Deux hommes s'embarquaient dans un canot. L'un se tenait à l'arrière avec son aviron et l'autre
> debout à la proue, harpon en main, guettait sa proie. La pince du canot, garnie d'un flambeau
> fait d'écorce de bouleau, éclairait la mer. »

**Dalhousie University, Fish-WIKS — Shelley Denny (UINR), « Mi'kmaq Traditional Knowledge: eels and
the Bras d'Or Lakes »**, https://www.dal.ca/sites/fishwiks/news-events/2014/05/01/mi_kmaq_traditional_knowledge__eels_and_the_bras_d_or_lakes.html
— copie : `53-foene-dalhousie-uinr-anguille-mikmaq.html`

> « There are spears for soft bottom and hard bottom, nikoql. These spears are used when on a boat
> in shallow water usually in the evening but if the conditions are calm and without glare on the
> water, eels can be speared during the day. When spearing at night or during the day, eels are
> selected for their size. Winter spearing takes place through the ice using the winter spear,
> natawemkewe'l, on soft bottom. As eels are not visible, eels that are speared are kept even if
> they are under 45 cm. […] If this does not occur, then the eel may fall off and may die due to
> injuries and the harvester wasted the eel. »
> « Spearing can only take place in shallow depths. »

→ eau peu profonde ; le jour si calme et sans reflet ; on choisit la taille quand on voit ; sous
la glace on garde tout ; un poisson qui se décroche meurt de ses blessures.

**MDC, *Missouri Conservationist*, juin 2015, « Summertime Bow Fishing »**,
https://mdc.mo.gov/magazines/conservationist/2015-06/summertime-bow-fishing
— copie : `53-foene-mdc-visee-sous-le-poisson.html`

> « The trick is to go slow, just lifting your feet enough so they don't drag and disturb the
> bottom. Minimizing movement in your steps reduces vibrations in the water that alert fish to your
> presence. »
> « Bow fishing by boat is best done with two or more people: one to drive, the others to bow fish
> […] Ensuring you have a safe and stable platform is critical. Being at full draw can change your
> center of balance, especially in choppy water. »
> « Polarized sunglasses […] cutting glare on the water, allowing you to see fish more easily. »

## 3. Sous la glace : l'anguille, à l'aveugle

**Atlas des peuples autochtones du Canada (Société géographique royale du Canada) — Gregg White
(Mi'kmaq, Flat Bay), « Seasonal Movement »**, https://indigenouspeoplesatlasofcanada.ca/article/seasonal-movement-2/
— copie : `53-foene-atlas-peuples-autochtones-anguille-sous-glace.html`

> « The process involved cutting a circular hole in the ice, usually around half a metre in
> diameter, with an axe. Once the hole was cut, the fisherman would gently thrust a three to five
> metre "spear" deep into the mud over and over again, until he either impaled an eel on one of the
> prongs or he was sure he had covered the entire diameter of the hole. The tips of the spear were
> curved upward, so when the man pulled the spear back up toward him, the eel became hooked. A
> fisherman could tell if an eel was impaled by sensing the feeling of the spear […] when the
> fisherman was sure he had covered every inch of reach his spear would allow, he would move on
> and cut another hole. »
> « Ice, that because of the flowing river near it, never got too thick, which was both convenient
> for our task and also dangerous. »
> « The thickness of the ice was directly related to the amount of mud you could spear. The thinner
> the ice, the further you could reach into the river mud […] As a result, I have seen men go onto
> the ice when they had no good reason to be there. Well, one good reason I suppose — hunger. »

→ trou d'**un demi-mètre** ; foène de **3 à 5 m** ; dents recourbées vers le haut ; la glace la
plus mince est la meilleure pêche, d'où le danger.

## 4. Viser : le poisson est plus creux qu'il ne paraît

**MDC** (même page) :

> « A general rule of thumb is to aim below where the fish appears in order to compensate for
> refraction. The deeper the target, the lower you aim. […] you can just practice aiming for rocks
> and leaves submerged in the water. »

**Science Learning Hub (Université de Waikato), « Investigating refraction and spearfishing »**,
https://www.sciencelearn.org.nz/resources/59-investigating-refraction-and-spearfishing
— copie : `53-foene-slh-refraction-et-harpon.html`

> « A person above the water sees the apparent position of the fish closer to the surface than the
> real position of the fish. »

**Science Learning Hub, « Refraction of light »**, https://www.sciencelearn.org.nz/resources/49-refraction-of-light
— copie : `53-foene-slh-refraction-verticale.html`

> « Angle of the incident ray – if the light is entering the substance at a greater angle, the
> amount of refraction will also be more noticeable. On the other hand, if the light is entering
> the new substance from straight on (at 90° to the surface), the light will still slow down, but
> it won't change direction at all. »

**HyperPhysics (Georgia State University), « Refraction of Light by Water »**,
http://hyperphysics.phy-astr.gsu.edu/hbase/geoopt/refr2.html — copie : `53-foene-hyperphysics-poisson-plus-creux.html`

> « Submerged objects always appear to be shallower than they are because the light from them
> changes angle at the surface […] »

## Ce qui n'a pas été trouvé

- **Aucune source ne donne la longueur du manche pour l'eau libre.** Le texte dit « plus long que
  l'eau n'est creuse » et ne chiffre que la foène d'hiver (3 à 5 m).
- **Aucune source ne donne l'essence de bois des mâchoires.** Le texte dit « bâtons courbes », comme
  Le Jeune et les aînés innus.
- **Le flambeau d'écorce** : fabrication non décrite par ces sources ; le livre renvoie au ch. 18,
  qui l'a déjà.
- **Le Jeune** n'est vu qu'en traduction anglaise citée par Musées numériques Canada ; le texte
  français de 1634 (*Relation de ce qui s'est passé en la Nouvelle-France en l'année 1634*) n'a pas
  été capté.
