# Le compost au froid — UMaine Bulletin #1159 et UAF « Composting in Alaska »

## A. University of Maine Cooperative Extension, Bulletin #1159, « How Compost Happens »

- **URL :** https://extension.umaine.edu/publications/1159e/
- **Page enregistrée :** `96-umaine-1159-compost.html` (HTTP 200)
- **Consultée le :** 27 septembre 2026
- **Rang :** vulgarisation universitaire, climat du Maine

> « Other items, such as dog or cat manure, contain disease organisms that can survive the compost
> process and may attract other animals. »

> « You should have about 30 parts carbon to one part nitrogen by weight. […] Even C and N ratios of
> 25:1 and 40:1 works well. […] If there is too much carbon, the pile won't decompose very fast. If
> there is too much nitrogen, it will start to form ammonia gas, which can cause odor problems. »

> « Woody materials, such as wood chips, branches, and twigs, may take up to two years to break down
> unless you finely chip or shred them. »

> « Put fresh strips of sod in a pile with the roots up and the grass down. Wet it well, and cover it
> with a tarp to keep the light out. • A large pile may take one to three years to decompose. »

Dépannage : « Ammonia smell — Too much nitrogen; not enough carbon — Add high-carbon material » ;
« Pile not heating up — Pile too small […] Cold weather — Make the pile bigger or insulate the pile
with a layer of straw » ; « Pests […] Meat scraps or fatty food waste in the pile ».

> « For the mesophilic bacteria to flourish, they need water, and with freezing temperatures a small,
> moist pile freezes before the bacteria have a chance to heat the pile up. »

> « In Alaska, the compost pile often freezes after it goes through the thermophylic phase and the
> maturation phase begins in the spring, when the pile thaws »

> « In Alaska, it may be best to plan on using the compost from this year's pile on next year's
> garden. »

> « The minimum dimension for an Alaska summer compost pile is 3 feet by 3 feet by 3 feet. »

> « The goal for the home composter is to get the pile to reach 131°F for three days, which is
> effective for killing many weed seeds and garden diseases. »

> « when fish wastes (very low C:N) are added to these wood wastes, a quality compost can be made in a
> matter of months. » — recette : « 1 part fish waste (carcass) - 1 part saw dust - 1 part green
> materials (lawn clippings) * Best used with bulking agents and frequent turning. »

> « Can I compost fish? Fish are high in nitrogen (C:N of about 10:1). Unfortunately, they can attract
> pests, including bears. Maintain an adequate carbon level to minimize smell and locate the
> composting site to minimize the danger of an occasional bear visit. »

Conversions : 3 pi = 0,91 m, soit un tas d'environ 1 m de côté ; 131 °F = 55 °C.
