## 1. A good smoke sauna — le monoxyde et les braises

Conférence de Pentti Tuohimaa (International Chimneyless Sauna Club), SaunaExpo, avril 2009.

> « Allow at least one hour for the embers to go out and the smoke to escape. »

> « Maintain a low temperature (60–80 °C). »

> « The wood should be cut as small as possible (2–5 cm). »

> « Adding firewood creates carbon monoxide. »

> « The carbon monoxide level will initially be very high when the heater is first lit. »

> « The amounts will slowly reduce, but a new peak will occur every time more firewood is added. »

> « The smallest cuts generate one sixth of the amount of carbon monoxide. »

> « The hatch is closed to smother the embers, causing the carbon monoxide level to rise and
> remain very high. »

> « When throwing water on the hot stones to expel the carbon monoxide, the level will remain
> high if the embers have not been removed. »

> « Once the embers have been removed, little carbon monoxide will be created when water is
> thrown on the stones. »

> « Therefore, the remaining charcoal and ashes should be removed. »

> « Never forget the importance of letting the embers go out and the smoke to escape before
> bathing. »

**Ce que le chapitre en tire :** le mécanisme central du §8 et du §9 — ce qui fait le CO dans un
bain de vapeur, ce ne sont pas les pierres, ce sont les braises et le charbon qu'on laisse avec
elles. D'où la règle du bain sous bâche : les pierres entrent brossées, sans une braise.

## 2. A child in the sauna — Välimäki et Jokinen (pédiatres), Congrès international du sauna, 2006

> « children are bathed in the sauna from the average age of 4.5 months onwards, with 12% having
> their first sauna bath at one month. Babies were taken into the hot room for less than five
> minutes at a time, but more than a quarter of 13-year-olds or older stayed for over 15 minutes. »

> « approximately 19% suffered from mild discomfort, such as headaches, dizziness, feelings of
> being hot and heart palpitations. A total of 43% of those aged 13 or older said that after a
> sauna they tend to sleep better than normal »

> « The Finnish sauna is a hot place, where the high air temperature (80–90 °C) and its rapid
> fluctuation during bathing set high requirements for the body's temperature regulation system. »

> « because of their overall size, babies and toddlers have a disadvantageously large skin area,
> causing them to get hotter than an adult at the same temperature. In addition to that, children
> have little fat tissue underneath their skin to insulate them from the heat, less of an ability
> to sweat and a high daily need for water intake. »

> « children's core (rectal) temperature begins to rise as soon as they enter the sauna and
> continues to do so throughout the session. Therefore, a child cannot maintain a constant body
> temperature in the sauna. »

> « When cooling down, young children's blood pressure may become lower, and as their regulation
> system is slower to respond children may feel dizzy for a while. On the other hand, cooling down
> in cold water raises the blood pressure. »

> « We noticed that a ten-minute heat stress caused 5–10-year-old children to lose 0.8% of their
> bodyweight due to sweating, but their blood plasma volume and sodium levels remained in
> balance. »

> « A sufficient fluid intake must be ensured while in the sauna. A child (and an adult) must be
> allowed to move between the hot room, the washing room and the cooling area according to their
> needs. The ten-minute heat exposure that we used as a standard in our study is atypical for
> real-world sauna bathing – too long for babies and perhaps too short for passionate bathers who
> are past their teenage years. »

> « an acutely ill child who has a fever must not be taken to the sauna. The sauna will not cure
> a cold or an infection. »

> « children require adults' monitoring in the sauna – the younger the child the more vigilant the
> adults must be. Furthermore, the water used for creating steam (löyly) should be lukewarm to
> reduce the risk of burns. »

> « A small child cannot withstand a high heat for an extended period, nor can they communicate
> this fact. Young children, toddlers in particular, are also at risk of suffering burns. »

## 3. Sauna and sleep

> « The test subjects' internal body temperature rose significantly during the roughly 30 minutes
> spent in the sauna. Afterwards, the temperature decreased again and the brain cooled down, and
> around two hours after the sauna the test subjects began feeling tired. »

> « Most test subjects found it easier to fall asleep after a bathing session in the sauna, with
> only around three per cent experiencing increased difficulties. »

> « Over 40% found the sauna to have a positive impact on their sleep quality, although the
> majority (approx. 60%) said that they had not experienced a significant impact. »

(L'étude sur le sommeil profond portait sur **cinq** sujets : le chapitre n'en cite pas les
pourcentages.)

## 4. Fire safety in the sauna

> « Approximately 80% of fires that take place in June and July happen in the sauna, which points
> to carelessness and lack of maintenance at leisure homes when it comes to heaters and chimneys. »

> « Heating them up too quickly with a large fire may cause damage to a heater, the flue and the
> joint that connects them. »

> « Ashes can remain hot for a long time. Therefore, they must be stored in a non-combustible
> container. » — la page donne jusqu'à 24 heures de refroidissement avant de les jeter.

> « Never leave a heater or a fireplace unattended during heating. »

(La fréquence de ramonage de trois ans est une obligation finlandaise : non reprise.)

## 5. Sauna heater

> « A heat-storing heater is heated up before bathing, and the heat stored in a large amount of
> stones will be sufficient to last for the whole bathing session. »

> « The stones of a chimneyless sauna heater, i.e. a heater that lets the smoke into the hot room,
> are heated by the flames, hot air and burning gases from the combustion chamber below. During
> the heating, the smoke initially escapes through an open door, and later a hatch near the
> ceiling or a funnel through the roof. »

> « A so-called ruins heater (rauniokiuas) can be constructed in a traditional way using stones but
> no mortar »

> « The lowest and largest stones warm up first, allowing heat to pass from stone to stone through
> convection as well. »

> « the flames and smoke must not touch the stones » (poêle à chauffe continue : les pierres sont
> séparées du foyer)

## 6. Sauna and health effects — Lasse Viinikka (2009) et Katriina Kukkonen-Harjula (2006)

> « In room temperature, around 10% of the blood pumped by the heart goes to the skin. [...] As much
> as 70% of the blood may circulate via the skin while in the sauna. »

> « Swimming after the sauna makes the skin's blood vessels contract, and the blood pressure
> quickly rises relatively high. A healthy person can usually cope well, but an increase in blood
> pressure may be dangerous to individuals suffering from heart failure »

> « the biggest health benefit of the sauna is the relaxation it offers »

> « Those who die in the sauna are typically inebriated – as many as half of the cases, according
> to some studies. Being drunk may lead a person to slip on the sauna floor or forget to be mindful
> of an underlying heart problem [...] an inebriated person can also fall asleep in the sauna and
> become dehydrated. »

> « The general consensus in Finland is that bathing in the sauna during pregnancy is safe. The
> central nervous system defects described above are rare in Finland, even though nearly all
> pregnant women use the sauna throughout their pregnancy. »

> « anyone allergic to birch trees should avoid using a sauna whisk made from birch »

> « healthy young men stayed in a relatively hot (80 ºC) or hot (100 ºC) sauna without throwing any
> water onto the stones for as long as they felt comfortable doing so. »

## 7. Quality guidelines for sauna whisks — la vihta

> « Made from silver birch. »

> « Length 50 cm +/- 5 cm (the ends of the branches must not be cut off). »

> « A sauna whisk is tied together using a year-old willow bind that has no side shoots. »

> « The branches must not be wet, which means that a sauna whisk cannot be made when it rains. »

> « Sauna whisks can be dried by hanging them up in a cool and dark place with good ventilation. »

> « Whisks are put in a plastic bag immediately after they are made, excess air is squeezed out. »
> (congélation)

> « Before using a dried whisk, immerse it in warm water for approximately 1.5–2 hours. »

> « NEVER put a whisk in hot water or on the hot stones of a heater. »

La page ne dit pas à quel moment de l'été couper les branches.

## Notes de captation

- **Transpositions d'espèces (correction du 2026-09-27).** Le bouleau de la page est le bouleau
  verruqueux européen (*silver birch*, *Betula pendula*) ; le chapitre nomme le bouleau à papier
  (*Betula papyrifera*), l'espèce du glossaire et celle qui pousse au chalet. De même, le tremble
  de la page (« aspen ») est le tremble européen (*Populus tremula*) ; le chapitre nomme le
  peuplier faux-tremble (*Populus tremuloides*). L'aulne (« alder ») est rendu par l'aulne
  rugueux (*Alnus incana*) du glossaire. **Ce sont des transpositions du chapitre, pas des
  espèces éprouvées par la source.** La note précédente disait que le chapitre écrivait
  « branches de bouleau » sans nommer l'espèce : c'était faux, il nomme *Betula papyrifera*.
- **« Environ deux heures » après le sauna (page « Sauna and sleep ») :** ce délai vient de
  Putkonen et Elomaa 1976, la même étude sur **cinq sujets** dont les pourcentages de sommeil
  profond ne sont pas repris. Retiré du chapitre le 2026-09-27 : le délai d'une à deux heures
  avant le coucher repose maintenant sur Haghayegh 2019 (bain chaud, méta-analyse). La même page
  dit « An increase in our internal temperature makes us fall asleep quicker » : la phrase du
  chapitre « un corps chaud s'endort mal », sans source, a été retirée.
- **Feux de sauna :** la page attribue les 80 % à « carelessness and lack of maintenance [...]
  when it comes to heaters and chimneys », puis nomme les erreurs d'installation (« insufficient
  distance to flammable material », poêle et conduit mal assortis) et les cendres chaudes mal
  rangées. Le chapitre reprend ces causes (étape 1, §8 point 5). « Un poêle poussé au maximum
  pendant une heure », qui n'était dans aucune source, a été retiré.
- **Cendres :** la page dit « ashes must be allowed to cool down for up to 24 hours before
  disposal » — un délai d'attente, pas la durée au bout de laquelle la cendre est froide. Le
  chapitre ne reprend plus ce chiffre : il s'aligne sur les ch. 82 et 83 (au moins 7 jours dans le
  contenant fermé, cendres brassées).
- **Sauna à fumée :** le chapitre suit maintenant la source mot pour mot sur deux points : la
  fumée sort d'abord par la porte, puis par la trappe haute (tout ouvert l'été si le vent le
  permet) ; « at least one hour for the embers to go out and the smoke to escape ». La manière de
  franchir le foyer (dalle en linteau) n'est pas dans la source, qui dit seulement « a dome over
  an arched combustion chamber » : elle porte un `[À VÉRIFIER]` au chapitre.
- **Enfants (page « A child in the sauna ») :** « too long for babies » est le jugement des
  pédiatres sur leur durée standard de 10 minutes, pas un effet mesuré ; le chapitre le présente
  comme leur avis (étape 38). « Toddlers [...] are also at risk of suffering burns » : le chapitre
  ne dit plus « plus facilement que quiconque ».
- Les chiffres de ces pages sont ceux de conférenciers (pédiatres, chercheurs) rapportés par
  l'association. Les deux chiffres pédiatriques qui portent une consigne (0,8 % du poids en
  10 minutes ; tension qui baisse à la sortie) sont recoupés par l'article de Jokinen 1990
  (`37-pubmed-jokinen-1990-enfants-au-sauna-resume.txt`).
