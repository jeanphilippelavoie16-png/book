## Extraits intégraux

> « Hardwoods, or deciduous trees, when burned, produce a greater percent of ash than
> softwoods, or conifers. »

> « Potassium is found in wood ash as potassium carbonate and potassium oxide. »

> « Potassium is often called potash because it is found in "pot ashes". […] Originally,
> potassium and other salts were obtained by leaching wood ashes and evaporating the resulting
> solution. »

> « The concentration of elements is usually somewhat higher in ash from hardwoods than ash
> from softwoods. »

> « The fertilizer value of wood ash depends on the type of wood burned. As a general rule,
> hardwoods weigh more per cord, yield more ash per pound of wood, and their ash contains higher
> percentages of nutrients than softwoods. The effects of these factors are illustrated in the
> following table, in which Douglas Fir represents softwood and oak represents hardwood. […]
> Nevertheless, this table indicates that hardwoods produce approximately three times as much
> ash and five times as much nutrients as softwoods. »

> « Ash from a cord of oak would meet the potassium needs of a garden 60 ft. by 70 ft. and ash
> from a cord of Douglas Fir, a garden 30 ft. by 30 ft. »

> « Elements in ash are water soluble. Therefore, ash should be stored in a dry place until
> ready for use. »

## Notes de captation

**Ce qui est confirmé :** à la corde, le bois franc donne environ **trois fois plus de cendre**
que le résineux, et une cendre **plus concentrée** en éléments, dont la potasse — qui est ce que
la lessive va chercher. L'exemple du potager (chêne : 60 × 70 pi ; sapin de Douglas : 30 × 30 pi)
donne un ordre de grandeur de près de cinq fois plus de potassium par corde.

**Ce qui n'est pas dit :** rien sur le bouleau à papier ou l'érable en particulier (chêne et
sapin de Douglas servent d'exemples) ; rien sur la force d'une lessive. La fiche est agricole.

**Utile aussi pour le chapitre :** « Elements in ash are water soluble » — une cendre laissée à
la pluie a perdu sa potasse avant d'arriver au baril. La fiche de l'Alabama Cooperative Extension
(consultée le même jour, https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/lawn-garden/wood-ashes-for-lime-and-potash/,
non enregistrée faute de comparaison feuillu/résineux) le rappelle : les cendres ont été « the
main source of lye for making soap ».
