## Extrait intégral (résumé, anglais)

> « The 1998 ice storm in eastern Quebec provided an opportunity for Canada Mortgage and Housing
> to obtain information about the cooling rates of homes that were without heat and occupants
> for several days. Local authorities were given permission to enter 31 residential homes in the
> Saint-Jean-sur-Richelieu and measure the house air temperature and surface temperatures in the
> basement. House size and insulation levels were estimated and the date of construction was
> recorded. The mean temperature during the testing period was around -8 degrees C, with daytime
> highs of up to 3 degrees C. When a furnace stop operating in the winter, homeowners can expect
> the indoor air temperature to drop in the first several hours from 20 degrees C to 14 degrees
> C. However, from that point on, cooling occurs much more slowly because the temperature
> differential of indoor to outdoor air is now lower. The thermal mass of the house also plays a
> role in stabilizing the cooling rate. The concrete, gypsum, ceramics, wood, and furnishings take
> longer to lose their temperature. In addition, the earth around the basements has a high
> thermal mass which does not change temperatures readily. Incidental gains from the sun also
> increase the house temperature. This data should reassure homeowners who worry about leaving
> their homes in winter. Results show that in the event of a power failure, houses can take days
> or weeks to fall below freezing, depending on the severity of weather outside. »

## Ce que le livre en tire, et ce qu'il ne peut pas en tirer

- **Permis :** les premiers degrés partent vite (de 20 à 14 °C dans les premières heures), puis le
  refroidissement ralentit beaucoup, parce que l'écart avec l'extérieur diminue et que la masse
  de la maison (béton, gypse, céramique, bois, meubles, terre autour du sous-sol) rend sa
  chaleur lentement. Par une moyenne de **-8 °C** dehors, une maison met **des jours ou des
  semaines** à passer sous zéro.
- **Non permis :** un délai à **-35 °C**. Les mesures ont été prises vers -8 °C. Plus l'écart est
  grand, plus la perte est rapide (c'est le mécanisme même du résumé) ; le délai à -35 °C est
  donc plus court, mais aucune source trouvée ne le chiffre.
- Les conduites dans un mur extérieur ou un vide sanitaire ne sont pas à la température de la
  pièce : elles gèlent avant elle (fiche `10-montreal-gel-tuyauterie-filet-d-eau-armoires.md`).
