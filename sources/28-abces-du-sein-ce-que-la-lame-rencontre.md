- **URL :** https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/66230 ; PDF :
  https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/e6088be7-aeff-4c55-988b-8b5d071f1a60/content
- **Consultée le :** 27 septembre 2026 (HTTP 200, 50 pages)
- **Copie :** `sources/28-oms-2000-mastite-causes-et-prise-en-charge.pdf`

### Extraits intégraux

§ 7.7, *Breast Abscess* (p. 17) :

> « A lactating breast, as any other infected tissue, localises infection by forming a barrier of
> granulation tissue around it. This becomes an abscess capsule, which is filled with pus. There is
> a severely painful swollen lump, with redness, heat, and oedema of the overlying skin. In
> neglected cases the lump may be fluctuant, with skin discoloration and necrosis. Fever may or may
> not be present. To confirm the diagnosis, pus can be aspirated with a syringe and wide bore
> needle. The differential diagnosis of breast abscess includes galactocoele (see Section 7.2),
> fibroadenoma, and carcinoma. »

§ 9.3, *Breast Abscess — Surgical treatment* (p. 24) :

> « If an abscess has formed, the pus must be removed. This can be done by incision and drainage,
> which usually requires general anaesthesia, but it can also be done by aspiration, guided by
> ultrasound, if available. Ultrasound is a useful diagnostic tool for breast abscess, and a
> thoroughly performed, ultrasound guided aspiration of the pus may be curative. It is less painful
> and mutilating than incision and drainage, and can be done under local anaesthesia, often as an
> outpatient. »

> « However, antibiotics alone, without removal of pus, are unlikely to be of value. An abscess wall
> creates a barrier that protects the pathogenic bacteria from the body's defences, and makes it
> impossible to achieve effective antibiotic levels in the infected tissue. »

§ 11 (p. 27) :

> « Breast abscess may compromise future lactation in about 10% of affected women. A large abscess
> may require extensive resection of breast tissue which can result in disfigurement and sometimes
> a functional mastectomy. »

## 2. Ghunaim — *Percutaneous ultrasound-guided needle aspiration for management of breast abscesses – a review* (2023)

- **Organisme :** Département de radiologie, Faculté de médecine, Université Taibah (Arabie
  saoudite) ; *Journal of Medical Radiation Sciences* 70(3):327-337, doi:10.1002/jmrs.682 ;
  licence CC BY 4.0
- **URL :** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10500119/ ; texte intégral par Europe PMC :
  https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10500119/fullTextXML
- **Consultée le :** 27 septembre 2026 (HTTP 200)
- **Copie :** `sources/28-europepmc-ghunaim-2023-abces-du-sein-aspiration.xml`

### Extraits intégraux

> « I&D is the traditional, invasive, and frequently used method for larger breast abscesses; it
> requires the use of general anaesthesia, the healing time is prolonged, regular dressings are
> required, restoration of breastfeeding is difficult, patients are unsatisfied with cosmetic
> outcomes as there is a high chance of wound granulation, there is a risk of rupture and
> recurrence of breast abscesses (10%–38%) »

> « An incision is made at the site of maximum fluctuation, the whole of the abscess cavity is
> evacuated, loculi are broken, and, lastly, the wound is packed. »

> « 50% of the patients had complete resolution after one aspiration, multiloculation was
> significantly related to treatment failure (P = 0.002) »

> « Fistula formation was main complication in the I&D group (10%) »

> « Reviews and clinical studies have shown that the risk of fistula formation is lower with
> US-guided NA. A systematic review of milk fistula among lactating mothers shows that milk
> fistulae are more frequent following I&D than after needle aspirations. »

> « Although Eryilmaz et al. had conducted NA without US guidance, NA was successful among all
> patients with abscess diameter smaller than 5 cm; and it was also successful among 25% of
> patients with abscesses larger than 5 cm in diameter »

## 3. Snider — *Management of Mastitis, Abscess, and Fistula* (2022)

- **Organisme :** *Surgical Clinics of North America* 102 (2022) 1103-1116,
  doi:10.1016/j.suc.2022.06.007 (Elsevier) ; PDF diffusé par l'American Association for the
  Surgery of Trauma. Porte surtout sur l'abcès du sein **hors allaitement** ; copie privée, pour
  vérification seulement.
- **URL :** https://www.aast.org/static/bd875d5d-d902-4cf1-8610412343e90b39/Management-of-Benign-Breast-Disease.pdf
- **Consultée le :** 27 septembre 2026 (HTTP 200)
- **Copie :** `sources/28-snider-2022-mastite-abces-fistule.pdf`

### Extraits intégraux

> « If the cavity is not extremely superficial, and relatively normal skin overlies it, aspiration
> frequently allows resolution and has been shown in a randomized trial to allow more rapid healing
> than open drainage. [...] Although aspiration can be conducted with palpation alone, the use of
> ultrasound facilitates complete emptying and irrigation of the cavity in addition to monitoring
> for reaccumulation of pus. It is sometimes necessary to aspirate the cavity several times over a
> 7–10 day period to achieve resolution. »

> « a simple, cost-effective method is to introduce a 16 or 18-gauge needle on a syringe into the
> cavity and aspirate the pus. »

> « If the patient presents with a superficial abscess that has thin, attenuated skin overlying it,
> it is best to proceed with a simple incision and drainage in the office under local anesthesia.
> There is usually no need to make a large incision, insert a drain, or pack the wound. »

> « Unlike lactational abscesses and nonlactational peripheral breast abscesses, subareolar
> abscesses tend to recur or develop duct-cutaneous fistulae. »

## Lecture pour le livre

Ce que la lame rencontre au sein :
- **le lait** : chez la femme qui allaite, l'incision laisse plus souvent qu'une aiguille une
  **fistule de lait** — le lait sort par la plaie — et guérit lentement ; l'allaitement reprend mal ;
- **une poche profonde et cloisonnée** : sous une peau encore normale, l'abcès est profond ; il a
  souvent plusieurs chambres (« loculi »), et c'est ce qui fait échouer le vidage ;
- **pas toujours du pus** : une bosse du sein peut être un kyste de lait (galactocèle), une tumeur
  bénigne ou un cancer ; le pus se confirme à la seringue et à l'aiguille de gros calibre ;
- l'incision d'un abcès du sein se fait d'ordinaire **sous anesthésie générale**.

Hors du drapeau, pour le rédacteur : l'OMS et Snider décrivent l'**aspiration à l'aiguille**
(16 ou 18 G sur une seringue), possible au toucher seul, répétée au besoin sur 7 à 10 jours, moins
mutilante, sans fistule ; sans échographie, elle a réussi pour tous les abcès de moins de 5 cm dans
une série (Ghunaim). Seule la peau mince et amincie au-dessus d'un abcès superficiel se contente d'une
petite incision (Snider).
