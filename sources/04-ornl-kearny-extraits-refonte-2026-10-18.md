## Extraits intégraux (anglais, tels que publiés ; numéros de page du livre)

### L'iode qui n'est pas de l'iodure de potassium (p. 116 et 117)

> « Elemental (free) iodine is poisonous, except in the very small amounts in water disinfected with iodine tablets or
> a few drops of tincture of iodine. Furthermore, elemental iodine supplied by iodine tablets and released by tincture
> of iodine dropped into water is not effective as a blocking agent to prevent thyroid damage. If you do not have any
> potassium iodide, DO NOT TAKE IODINE TABLETS OR TINCTURE OF IODINE. »

> « DO NOT MAKE A FUTILE, HARMFUL ATTEMPT TO EAT ENOUGH IODIZED SALT TO RESULT IN THYROID BLOCKING. Iodized salt
> contains potassium iodide, but in such a low concentration that it is impossible to eat enough iodized salt to be
> helpful as a blocking agent. »

> « Since iodine solutions such as tincture of iodine and povidone-iodine are dangerous poisons if taken orally, these
> experiments have utilized absorption through the skin after topical application on bare skin. All reported
> experimental topical applications on human skin have given less thyroid protection than does proper oral
> administration of potassium iodide. Moreover, undesirable side effects of skin application can be serious. »

> « Potassium iodide has a painfully bad taste »

> « For protection against inhaled radioactive iodine, the FDA Final Recommendations [...] state that the following
> measures "should be considered": "..sheltering [merely staying indoors can significantly reduce inhaled doses]" »

### La viande des bêtes exposées (p. 76)

> « In fallout areas, however, animals that have eaten or drunk fallout-contaminated food or water will have
> concentrated radioactive atoms and molecules in their internal organs. The thyroid gland, kidneys, and liver
> especially should not be eaten. If an animal appears to be sick, it should not be eaten. The animal might be
> suffering from a sickening or fatal radiation dose and might have developed a bacterial infection as a result of
> this dose. Meat contaminated with the toxins produced by some kinds of bacteria could cause severe illness or death
> if eaten, even if thoroughly cooked. »

### Les brûlures de la peau par la poussière fraîche (vers la p. 43 du livre)

> « Fresh fallout particles are no more than a few days old and therefore very radioactive. If fresh particles remain
> for at least several tens of minutes in contact with the skin, beta burns are likely to result. »

> « Prompt washing will prevent beta burns. If water is not available, brushing and rubbing the fallout particles off
> the skin will help. »

> « The fallout on these islanders' scalps, bare necks, and the tops of their bare feet caused itching and burning
> sensations after a time. Days later, beta burns resulted, along with extreme discoloration of the skin. Beta burns
> are not deep burns; however, it took weeks to heal them. »

> « Fallout-contaminated clothing should be removed as soon as practical, or at least brushed and beaten before
> entering a shelter room »

### Les signes de l'irradiation (p. 110 du livre)

> « The first symptoms of radiation sickness are nausea, vomiting, headache, dizziness, and a general feeling of
> illness. These symptoms begin several hours after exposure to acute doses of 100 R to 200 R. and within 30 minutes
> or less after receiving a fatal dose. A source of probable confusion is the fact that one or more of these symptoms
> is experienced by many people when they are first exposed to great danger »

> « The initial symptoms end within a day or two. Then follows the latent phase [...] If the dose received was in the
> non-fatal range, the latent phase may last as long as 2 weeks. In the final phase, the victim of serious or fatal
> radiation sickness will have reduced resistance to infections and is likely to suffer diarrhea, loss of hair, and
> small hemorrhages of the skin, mouth, and/or intestinal tract. [...] hemorrhages and loss of much hair are clear
> indications of having received serious, but not necessarily fatal, radiation exposure. The final phase usually lasts
> for one to two months. Any available antibiotics should be reserved for this critical phase of the illness. »

> « Recovery from most cases of radiation sickness will be more likely for patients who receive a well balanced diet,
> rest, freedom from stress, and clean surroundings. [...] Nursing radiation victims is not hazardous. »

> (chap. 2, p. 20 du livre) « Anxiety, fear, and terror can result in symptoms very similar to those caused by radiation injury: nausea,
> vomiting, extreme trembling, diarrhea. »

### L'air de l'abri (chap. 6)

> « [shelters] hot in a few hours. Unless most of the occupants body heat and water vapor from sweat are removed by
> air circulated through a typical shelter, the heat-humidity conditions will become increasingly dangerous in warm or
> hot weather. »

> « Unlike the punkah, however, this air pump [le KAP] can force air to move in a desired direction and is a true
> pump. » ; « The pendulum-like, flap-valve pump was swung from two cabinet hinges [...] The pull-cord was attached to
> the pump frame below its hinged top »

> « filtered air can be provided by using a homemade KAP to pump air through furnace or air-conditioner filters »

> (Directional Fanning) « With this small two handled fan you quite easily can force about 300 cubic feet per minute
> (300 cfm) of outdoor air through a crowded trench or basement shelter. This is enough air for up to 9 adults crowded
> into a small shelter in extremely hot humid weather, and enough for about 100 people in cold weather. »

> « If no sticks are available, a double thickness of heavy, stiff cardboard 22 inches long by 14 inches wide will
> pump almost as much air if used as a handleless fan. »

> « The air should be fanned out of the shelter in the direction in which the air is naturally flowing. For maximum
> ventilation rate, fan about 40 strokes per minute. »

> « To fan air out through an air-exhaust opening, sit facing the opening with your elbows about 4 inches lower than the
> bottom of the opening. [...] 1) Quickly raise the fan to a vertical position close in front of your face and
> immediately fan (push) a slug of air into the opening - ending the power stroke with your arms fully extended and with
> the fan almost horizontal [...] 2), 3) After a slight pause, leisurely withdraw the almost horizontal fan »

> « (If the air-exhaust opening of your small shelter is much larger than that shown in the sketches, block part of it
> off to reduce it to approximately this 24-inch-high by 20-inch-wide size, for more effective use with this fan.) »

### L'eau (chap. 8)

> « Fortunately, in areas of heavy fallout, less than 2% of the radioactivity of the fallout particles contained in
> the water would become dissolved in water. »

> « Perforate the bottom of a 5-gallon can, a large bucket [...] with about a dozen nail holes. Punch the holes from the
> bottom upward, staying within about 2 inches of the center. » ; « Cover the pebbles with one thickness of terrycloth
> towel, burlap sackcloth, or other quite porous cloth. Cut the cloth in a roughly circular shape about 3 inches larger
> than the diameter of the can. » ; « Completely cover the surface of the soil layer with one thickness of fabric as
> porous as a bath towel. [...] A dozen small stones placed on the cloth near its edges will secure it adequately. » ;
> « Support the filter can on rods or sticks placed across the top of a container that is larger in diameter than the
> filter can. »

> « If the 6 or 7 inches of filtering soil is a sandy clay loam, the filter initially will deliver about 6 quarts of
> clear water per hour. (If the filtration rate is faster than about 1 quart in 10 minutes, remove the upper fabric and
> recompress the soil.) After several hours, the rate will be reduced to about 2 quarts per hour. When the filtering
> rate becomes too slow, it can be increased by removing and rinsing the surface fabric, removing about 1 inch of soil,
> and then replacing the fabric. »

> (Settling) « Fill a bucket or other deep container three quarters full of the contaminated water. Dig pulverized clay
> or clayey soil from a depth of four or more inches below ground surface, and stir it into the water. Use about a
> 1-inch depth of dry clay or dry clayey soil for every 4-inch depth of water. Stir until practically all the clay
> particles are suspended in the water. Let the clay settle for at least 6 hours. The settling clay particles will
> carry most of the suspended fallout particles to the bottom and cover them. Carefully dip out or siphon the clear
> water, and disinfect it. »

### L'électroscope KFM (annexe C)

> « When fallout gamma radiation [...] strikes the air inside the ionization chamber of a KFM, it produces charged ions
> in this enclosed air. These charged ions cause part or all of the electrostatic charge on the aluminum-foil leaves to
> be discharged. As a result of losing charge, the two KFM leaves move closer together. »

> « Take an initial reading. If after 3 hours its reading has decreased by 1 mm, or less, this means that its
> leaf-suspending threads are good insulators »

> (charge) « move the hard plastic rapidly back and forth so that it [...] is rubbed vigorously on the paper » ;
> « Move the electrostatically charged part of the rubbed plastic rather slowly past the upper end of the
> charging-wire »

### La baisse du rayonnement des retombées (chap. 1, p. 12 et 13 du livre ; encadré En plus « La baisse du rayonnement »)

> « Fortunately for all living things, the danger from fallout radiation lessens with time. The radioactive decay, as
> this lessening is called, is rapid at first, then gets slower and slower. »

> « Notice that it takes about seven times as long for the dose rate to decay from 1000 roentgens per hour (1000 R/hr)
> to 10 R/hr (48 hours) as to decay from 1000 R/hr to 100 R/hr (7 hours). »

> « Fig. 1.2. Decay of the dose rate of radiation from fallout, from the time of the explosion, not from the time of
> fallout deposition. »

> « If the dose rate 1 hour after an explosion is 1000 R/hr, it would take about 2 weeks for the dose rate to be
> reduced to 1 R/hr solely as a result of radioactive decay. Weathering effects will reduce the dose rate further, for
> example, rain can wash fallout particles from plants and houses to lower positions on or closer to the ground.
> Surrounding objects would reduce the radiation dose from these low-lying particles. »

Lecture : 1 000 à la 1re heure, 100 à la 7e (dix fois moins), 10 à 48 heures (cent fois moins, écrit « au bout de
2 jours »), 1 au bout d'environ 2 semaines (mille fois moins). Compté depuis l'explosion, pas depuis l'arrivée de la
poussière : l'encadré le dit. « Une fois la poussière tombée » : la phrase de la source parle de la poussière déjà
déposée sur les plantes et les maisons.
