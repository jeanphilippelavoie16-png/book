## Extraits intégraux

> « Ice picks are the second most-important item. Pulling yourself out of cold water onto a
> smooth, wet surface with numb fingers is extremely difficult. A sharp set of ice picks gives you
> the best chance of pulling yourself out of the water. »

> « Two different styles of ice picks. The exposed nails (left) can be stuck into the opposite
> hand grip for safety when not in use. The style on the right has a retractable plastic sheath
> hiding the nail on each hand grip. »

> « After you calm your breathing, you'll have about 10 minutes to climb out of the water before
> you lose the feeling and strength needed to pull yourself out. Follow these steps: »

> « Turn in the direction you came from – that is probably the strongest ice. »

> « Dig the points of the picks into the ice. While vigorously kicking your feet, pull yourself
> onto the surface by sliding forward on the ice. »

> « Roll away from the area of weak ice. Rolling on the ice distributes your weight to help avoid
> breaking through again. »

> « Check that it is at least four inches thick before you walk on it. »

## Ce que la page établit, et ce qu'elle ne dit pas

- Établi : planter les pointes, battre des jambes, **glisser vers l'avant** sur la glace ; se
  retourner vers l'endroit d'où l'on vient ; rouler une fois sorti. Concorde avec
  `20-wisconsin-dnr-pics-a-glace.md` et `20-lakeice-pics-a-glace.md`.
- **Rien sur l'angle de la pointe ni sur le sens de la tenue** (pointe penchée vers le corps ou
  vers l'avant). Pas plus que les deux autres fiches. Les « 45 degrés » qui circulent en ligne
  viennent de sites de vente de matériel de pêche, non retenus.
