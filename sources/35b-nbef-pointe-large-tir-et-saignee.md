## Extraits intégraux

**Sharp Broadheads Are Essential**

> « Sharp broadheads are essential because they: Cut through skin, muscle, and even some bone better
> than dull ones, providing deeper penetration. Cut the tough elastic walls of blood vessels better
> and cause more bleeding than dull broadheads. Are more likely to penetrate an animal completely,
> which leaves both an entrance and an exit wound, making trailing and recovery easier. »

**How a Broadhead Causes Death**

> « Typically, an arrow kills by cutting blood vessels (arteries and veins), producing massive blood
> loss. [...] The time it takes for an animal to die from bleeding can vary from a matter of seconds
> to several hours, depending on the animal's size, how many vessels are cut, and whether they are
> arteries or veins. »

**How Arrows and Bullets Differ**

> « Broadhead-tipped arrows deliver a low-energy impact that kills by cutting vital tissues. Because
> impact alone won't knock down an animal, the arrow must be placed properly for a quick kill and
> easy recovery. »

> « [...] an animal—if mortally wounded—will die relatively close to where it was hit if it isn't
> spooked. »

**Broadheads**

> « All broadheads, regardless of type, must be sharp, durable, and matched to your equipment and the
> size of game being hunted. Blades must be sharpened after each practice and prior to going into the
> field to hunt. A "cut-on-contact" broadhead [...] may be used for all bows but is recommended for
> lower poundage bows to maximize penetration. »

> « To test the sharpness of a broadhead, pass it through rubber bands stretched across a small
> opening. If the bands are cut easily, your broadhead is sharp and ready for the hunt. »

> « If a wrench isn't used, the slightest slip can cause a serious cut. »

**Shot Angles: Broadside**

> « This angle gives the best shot for the largest big game animals: elk, moose, caribou [...]. The
> heavy hair and thick hide of these animals tend to absorb blood and close wounds. As a result, both
> an entry and an exit wound are necessary to cause sufficient blood loss for trailing and death. »

> « For most big game, the aiming spot is straight up from the back side of the front leg, one-third
> of the way up from the bottom of the chest. To avoid hitting the shoulder blade, wait until the
> front leg is forward. »

**Shot Angles: Quartering-Toward** ; **Head-On and Rear-End**

> « Heavy shoulder bones shield the majority of vital organs from penetration. »

> « Heavy bones in front and muscle mass and non-vital organs in back block penetration of the main
> vital areas. »

**Importance of Matching Arrows to Your Bow**

> « Perfect arrow flight can usually be obtained if the balance point on a completed arrow (a fletched
> arrow with the point attached) is located 10% to 16% of the distance forward from the center of the
> arrow toward the point. Arrows that are too lightweight for your bow may cause you to essentially
> "dry fire" your bow. »

**Ethical Hunting and the Risks of Long Shots**

> « Although the average hunter has a maximum effective range of 30 yards, most bowhunters shoot game
> from a much closer range. »

## Conversions et calculs

- 30 verges = **27 m**.
- Point d'équilibre à 10 à 16 % de la longueur **en avant du milieu** : sur une flèche de 61 cm, de
  **6 à 10 cm** ; sur 76 cm, de **8 à 12 cm** (0,10 × 61 = 6,1 ; 0,16 × 61 = 9,8 ; 0,10 × 76 = 7,6 ;
  0,16 × 76 = 12,2).

## Ce que le chapitre 35b en tire

- Une pointe émoussée coupe mal la paroi élastique des vaisseaux : la pointe large s'affûte avant
  chaque sortie ; l'essai de l'élastique tendu sur une ouverture.
- La pointe coupante dès la pointe (« cut-on-contact ») pour les arcs faibles.
- Sur l'orignal : **le poil épais et le cuir boivent le sang et referment la plaie** ; il faut une
  entrée et une sortie. Tir de travers seulement, la patte avant portée en avant, le point au tiers
  inférieur de la poitrine, juste derrière la patte ; jamais de face, de trois quarts avant, ni de
  derrière.
- Bête touchée qu'on n'effraie pas : elle meurt plus près.
- Point d'équilibre de la flèche finie ; la flèche trop légère fait l'effet d'un tir à vide.
- Portée : même un chasseur ordinaire, avec un arc de magasin, ne dépasse pas 27 m.

## Ce que la source ne dit pas

- Rien sur les arcs de fortune ni sur les pointes faites à la main.
