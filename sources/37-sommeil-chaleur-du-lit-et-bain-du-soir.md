## B. Le bain chaud avant le coucher

**Haghayegh S, Khoshnevis S, Smolensky MH, Diller KR, Castriotta RJ. Before-bedtime passive body
heating by warm shower or bath to improve sleep: A systematic review and meta-analysis.**
*Sleep Med Rev* 2019;46:124-135. Résumé PubMed (API E-utilities), fichier
`37-pubmed-haghayegh-2019-bain-avant-coucher-resume.txt` :

> « PBHWB of 40-42.5 °C was associated with both improved self-rated sleep quality and SE, and when
> scheduled 1-2 h before bedtime for little as 10 min significant shortening of SOL. »

> « These findings are consistent with the mechanism of PBHWB effects being the extent of core body
> temperature decline achieved by increased blood perfusion to the palms and soles that augments
> the distal-to-proximal skin temperature gradient to enhance body heat dissipation. »

(SOL : délai d'endormissement ; SE : efficacité du sommeil.)

## C. La température de la chambre et celle du lit

**Okamoto-Mizuno K, Mizuno K. Effects of thermal environment on sleep and circadian rhythm.**
*J Physiol Anthropol* 2012;31:14. Texte intégral en libre accès (Europe PMC, PMC3427038), fichier
`37-europepmc-okamoto-mizuno-2012-temperature-et-sommeil-texte.txt` :

> « The selective vasodilation of distal skin regions promotes the rapid onset of sleep »

> « The bed climate temperature and relative humidity are generally maintained around 32°C to 34°C,
> 40% to 60% relative humidity when normal sleep is obtained »

> « In studies using clothing and/or bedding, no significant difference was observed in sleep in a
> Ta range of 13°C to 23°C [66] and 3°C to 17°C [67]. »

> « Also, no significant difference in sleep quality measured by actigraphy was observed between
> 9°C and 20°C in the elderly »

> « In real-life situations where bed covers and clothing are used, sleep is actually disturbed
> during heat exposure rather than cold exposure »

> « However, our study indicates that cold exposure significantly changes cardiac autonomic
> activity during sleep, without affecting the sleep stages »

> « cold stimulation of the head since sufficient thermal insulation of the body is obtained from
> bedding and clothing; cold air inhalation; and whole body cooling. »

> « It is extremely important to note that Ta in winter should be maintained at a level higher
> than 10°C. However, the most difficult aspect of cold exposure is that sleep is not disturbed. »

(Ta : température de l'air de la pièce.)

**Ce que le chapitre en tire :** pour un adulte bien couvert, le froid de la chambre ne dérange pas
le sommeil ; c'est le lit qui doit être chaud. Mais le froid agit sur le cœur sans réveiller
personne, et la tête reste dehors : d'où la tuque et le seuil de 10 °C que les auteurs donnent.
Pour les enfants, la cible de 19 °C du ch. 39 (étape 9) reste celle du livre ; aucune source de ce
fichier ne la contredit, puisque toutes supposent un dormeur bien couvert, ce qu'un enfant de 4 ans
ne reste pas.
