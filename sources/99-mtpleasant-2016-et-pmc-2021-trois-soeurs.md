# Les Trois Sœurs : rendements (Mt. Pleasant 2016) et aliments, conservation, lessive (revue 2021)

## 1. Mt. Pleasant, J. 2016. « Food Yields and Nutrient Analyses of the Three Sisters: A Haudenosaunee Cropping System »

- **Revue :** *Ethnobiology Letters* 7(1), 2016 — DOI 10.14237/ebl.7.1.2016.721 (accès libre)
- **Autrice :** Jane Mt. Pleasant, Cornell University, School of Integrative Plant Science
- **URL :** https://ojs.ethnobiology.org/index.php/ebl/article/view/721
- **Consultée le :** 27 septembre 2026 — `99-mtpleasant-2016-rendements-trois-soeurs.html` (page du
  résumé et des références)
- **Sert dans :** chapitre 99, §3

> « The Three Sisters yields more energy (12.25 x 10⁶ kcal/ha) and more protein (349 kg/ha) than any
> of the crop monocultures or mixtures of monocultures planted to the same area. The Three Sisters
> supplies 13.42 people/ha/yr. with energy and 15.86 people/ha/yr. with protein. Nutrient contents
> of the crops are further enhanced by nixtamalization, a traditional processing technique where
> maize is cooked in a high alkaline solution. This process increases calcium, protein quality, and
> niacin in maize. »

## 2. Revue 2021 (PMC8001537) — « Historical Indigenous Food Preparation Using Produce of the Three Sisters Intercropping System »

- **URL :** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8001537/ (revue *Foods*, 2021, accès libre)
- **Consultée le :** 27 septembre 2026 — `99-pmc-2021-aliments-trois-soeurs.html` et
  `99-pmc-2021-aliments-trois-soeurs-texte.txt`
- **Sert dans :** chapitre 99, §3, §5-B (butte de Champlain, séchage, conservation) et §9
  (lessive de cendre)

> « Corn provided support for beans, beans provided nitrogen through nitrogen-fixing rhizobia
> bacteria that live on the roots, and squash and pumpkins provided ground cover to suppress weeds
> and inhibit evaporation from the soil »

> « in 1605 Champlain describes seeing corn raised in gardens, with three or four kernels planted in
> mounds three feet apart and into which three or four beans were also planted so that they grew,
> interlacing with the corn and keeping the ground free from weeds »

> « Estimates of crop acreage for the Wendat (Huron) [...] suggest that a third of an acre per person
> provided for sustenance »

> « Heidenreich estimated the Wendat diet was 65% corn, 15% beans, squash and pumpkins, 10–15% fish,
> and 5% meat. »

> « Four husks were usually left on each ear for braiding into a string of five spans in length »

> « It was noted that squash was cut into strips and placed in evaporating trays, or strung on cords
> suspended near the fireplace until dry, then stored. [...] Jesuit Relations testified to their
> hardness saying that it required a hatchet to open them and that they lasted 4–5 months. »

> « Waugh wrote that green beans in the pod were prepared by boiling, drying in evaporating baskets
> or on a flat board, and storing in a bag or barrel. »

Sur la lessive (section 6.1.4, « Hominy ») :

> « nixtamalization of corn involves soaking and cooking the corn in an alkaline solution, usually
> lime water but sometimes wood ash lye (a strong alkaline liquor leached from wood ashes and rich in
> potassium carbonate), washing, and then hulling »

> « shelled flint corn was placed in boiling water, simmered until well swollen, and then sifted
> hardwood ashes were added at a proportion of about one quart of ashes to a gallon of water
> (equivalent to about a 1:5 ratio). The mass was boiled until the black pips of the corn loosened
> and were seen floating about, and the hull slipped easily when the grain was rolled between the
> fingers. [...] then put into fresh water, or a running stream, basket and all, and vigorously
> shaken and stirred until the hulls and pips had floated away [...] The corn was then added to fresh
> boiling water, rapidly boiled until fairly soft, and washed to remove the last traces of lye. »

> « Waugh wrote that it could take up to 90 min for the hulling process, which included about 45 min
> for the first boiling, including the lye, and 30 min for the second »

> « Nixtamalized corn has several benefits over unprocessed corn, being that it is more easily
> ground, has improved nutritional value, flavor and aroma, and reduced levels of mycotoxins. »

## Ce que le livre en tire, et ce qu'il laisse

- Le chapitre dit le mécanisme (tuteur, azote, couvre-sol) et la proportion de la cendre (environ
  une part de cendre pour cinq d'eau, en volume), la durée (environ 45 min puis 30 min) et les
  signes (les germes noirs flottent, la pelure glisse entre les doigts).
- **Non repris :** le test de la lessive « assez forte pour mordre la langue » (Parker, cité par la
  revue) — on ne goûte pas une lessive (ch. 112, encadré). Les récits, prières et cérémonies des
  semailles (cérémoniel, précautions 4.1).
- Les rendements à l'hectare (12,25 × 10⁶ kcal, 349 kg de protéines) ne s'impriment pas : mesurés
  dans l'État de New York, ils ne disent rien de la Côte-Nord. Le chapitre garde la conclusion
  qualitative (plus que chaque culture seule sur la même surface).
