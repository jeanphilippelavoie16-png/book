Clinical Outcomes of ULTRA EZY Bar vs Erich Arch Bar in Conservative Management of Maxillofacial Fractures: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Abstract

Objective

Various techniques have been employed from time to time to achieve maxillomandibular fixation, and arch bars provide an effective and versatile means of maxillomandibular fixation, and however, some of the issues occurring with it have been eliminated with the introduction of Ultralock EZY bar.

The aim of the present study is to compare the advantages and disadvantages of Ultralock Ezy bar over the Erich arch bar in mid-face fracture or maxillary fracture or mandibular fracture or both requiring conservative treatment.

Materials and Methods

A total of 20 patients reported to the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery in Sudha Rustagi Dental College and Hospital, Faridabad, with mid-face fracture/maxillary fracture, mandibular fracture or both. The treatment plan required intermaxillary fixation. As a part of treatment plan, group was selected randomly divided into 20 arches in each group that is test arch group and control arch group.

Test arch group included arches in which Ultralock EZY bar was done. Control arch group included arches in which Erich arch bar was done. The parameters compared in both the groups were surgical time taken, injuries due to wires, arch bar stability, oral hygiene index, patient acceptance and comfort, pulp vitality, and complication (if any).

Results

The average surgical time taken was less, and oral hygiene status and patient acceptance were better in test group. There was not much statistically significant difference in pulp vitality but number of cases with absence of pulp vitality were more in test group.

Conclusion

This study emphasizes the use of Ultralock Ezy bar as a quick and easy method than Erich arch bar. Oral hygiene maintenance was comparatively better in patients with Ultralock Ezy bar than those with Erich arch bar. For the patients who require long-term IMF, Ultralock Ezy bars can be a viable option.

Introduction

Maxillofacial region comprises of soft and hard tissues from frontal bone to mandible. This area occupies a very marked position on the face and is more prone to intentional and unintentional trauma. Henceforth, fractures in this area may occur alone or in combination with fractures of other bones due to motor vehicle accidents, falls, assaults, firearm injuries, sports, and industrial accidents [1].

The main aim of management of fracture is restoration of anatomic form, function with particular attention to reestablishment of occlusion and facial aesthetics [2].

Though various techniques have been employed from time to time to achieve maxilla-mandibular fixation, Erich arch bar is the most common method used for inter-maxillary fixation. The originators of arch bar technique Sauer and Gilmer had used an ordinary round bar to ligate teeth with brass ligature wires [3]. It is indicated in variety of clinical situations such as in management of mandible and mid-face fracture, in maintenance of occlusion during mandible reconstruction, after elective orthognathic surgery, in extended elastic therapy for minor occlusal movements, in sub condylar fractures, for occlusal stability for the placement of internal fixation and in dentoalveolar fractures. In arch bar procedure, semi-rigid support is given by wiring each dental arch; further in the process, arch bars are wired together for securing occlusion.

Some of the disadvantages associated with Erich arch bar are increased surgical time in both removal and placement, needle or wire penetrating injuries to surgeon, injury to periodontium, compromised oral hygiene and movement of teeth in lateral and extrusive direction [4, 5].

In 1989, for eliminating issues related to Erich arch bar, Arthur and Berardo [6] described self-drilling IMF screws, which utilizes four self-tapping titanium screws which are inserted transmucosally, one for each quadrant. Screwing techniques became the more rapidly applied technique as compared to other technique as they were easy to use and less operating time was required in its application.

To eliminate some of the challenges existing with MMF techniques, a new generation MMF system, i.e. Ultralock EZY Bar can be used. It is easy to use as interdental wiring is not required in its application. It is secured by screws so it leads to prevention of bone loss and food lodgement in interdental area.

In our study, the ease and efficacy of Ultralock EZY bar were compared with conventional Erich arch bar in IMF for facial fractures.

Materials and Methods

A total of 20 patients with facial fractures reported to the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery in Sudha Rustagi Dental College and Hospital, Faridabad, who were selected.

The patients between the age group of 18–70 years with mid-face/maxillary or mandibular fractures or both requiring conservative treatment were included in this study. Patients with medically compromised status, patients with disorders affecting bone healing, metabolism and turnover, on bisphosphonates oral or IV, with pathology of mandible or maxilla, gun-shot wounds and comminuted fracture were excluded from the study. Moreover, patients who refused to follow-up and did not give written consent were also excluded from the study. Furthermore, the selection of the arch in the same patient was done by simple random sampling and was designated as test arch and control arch. In test arch, Ultralock EZY bar manufactured by Orthomax was placed, and in control arch, Erich arch bar was placed. Both the arch bars were placed by the same operator in all patients.

For Operative Procedure, in control arch, intra-oral nerve blocks were given and dental arch was reduced conservatively, and then, arch bar was secured with teeth by circumdental 24–26-gauge wire [7]. In test arch, intra-oral nerve blocks were given, dental arch was reduced conservatively, and then, EZY bar was adapted over cervical third of teeth in conjunction with arch shape to avoid damaging of teeth roots and other anatomical structures. Holes were made in the interradicular spaces with a 1.5 mm drill bit, taking care to avoid the damage of roots of teeth. Ezy bar was secured by inserting and locking screws in maxillary arch with 6 mm screws and in mandibular arch with 8 mm screws [8]. After placing both the bars, IMF was done by wire loops or by elastics under aseptic condition (Figs. 1 and 2).

Furthermore, the evaluation was done for both the groups on the basis of surgical time taken, injuries due to wires, oral hygiene status (oral health index), arch bar stability, patient acceptance and comfort, vitality of teeth and complications (if any, such as—screw fracture, loosening of screws, mucosal growth over screw, wire breakage).

Surgical time taken in minutes was observed after giving intra-oral nerve blocks till placement of arch bar was observed individually in both the arches. Wire stick injury was identified by water inflation method in gloves [3]; therefore, for both arches, new gloves were used before placing the arch bars. Vitality of all the teeth present in the arch was checked using electronic pulp tester pre-operatively and post-operatively after removal of Erich arch bar and EZY bar. Oral hygiene index simplified given by Greene and Vermillion [9] was used to evaluate the oral hygiene status of the patient at the time of 3rd day, 1st week, 2nd week, 4th day and 6th weeks. The degree of patient comfort and acceptance were graded as good, fair, and poor during placement of Erich arch bar and EZY bar, after removal of the same. Arch bar stability for test arch and control arch was observed by holding the arch bar by needle holder was checked post-operatively and thus recorded as mobile and non-mobile on 3rd day, 1st week, 2nd week, 4th week, 6th week for the considered arches. Moreover, in the test group, screws fracture, screw loosening and mucosal growth over screw were also noted at patient level. Furthermore, for mucosal growth over screws, number of patient were recorded in which mucosal growth took place over screws in 4th week and 6th week. Number of patients were recorded in which wire breakage was reported in control group.

Result

In total, there were 20 patients with 40 sites distributed in two treatment modalities. The mean age of males was 24.31 ± 6.575, and the mean age of females was 34.00 ± 10.408. The mean surgical time taken was found to be 33.25 min in test arch (ultralock EZY bar) and 47.40 min in control arch (Erich Arch Bar). OHI Score was reported to be more in control group as compared to test group with significant p value of 0.001 (Table 1 and Graph 1).

The difference in the pulp vitality failed to reach the level of significance, although more number of cases where vitality was absent were seen in test arch group after removal of arch bar with p value 0.649 (Table 2 and Graph 2).

For Ultralock EZY bar, no screw fracture was seen, no screw loosening was seen and mucosal growth over screws was seen on 6th week in 3 patients. Moreover, wire breakage in Control arch group was seen intra-operatively in 4 cases and 1 case at 4th week and at 2 cases at 6th week were seen.

Discussion

The management of maxillofacial injuries is one of the most demanding and rewarding aspect of surgery because the patients with maxillofacial injuries not only suffer from physical pain but slight disfigurement can become an emotional distress for whole life [10, 11].

Shorter surgical time definitely translates into fewer complications due to the effect of the time itself and causes less fatigue to surgeon as well. In the present study, surgical time was significantly lower for Ultralock EZY bar group: 33.25 ± 9.695 min as compared to Erich arch bars: 47.40 ± 7.433 min (p < 0.001*). These findings are similar to that reported by Rai et al., Nandini et al. [12] and Qureshi et al. [3]. It reinforces the fact that screws are quick and easy to insert than conventional methods which reduced the operating time. Also Ultralock EZY is a reinvented system that does not need any interdental wire to stabilize its assembly.

Injuries due to wires are routinely experienced complications. The injuries puncture the operator’s finger and dispose them to increase the risk of spread of blood borne diseases like HIV and Hepatitis. Wire stick injuries were found to be significantly lower in the Ultralock ezy bar as compared to control group. (p < 0.001*). This is in correspondence to findings of Nandini et al. [12] study (p = 0.001*), where perforations in arch bars were 13.4 ± 5.13 and IMF screws were 0.60 ± 0.96. Gordon et al. [10] also indicated a 90% penetration rate of the outer glove with the arch bar technique as compared to no glove penetration with the intra-oral cortical bone screw fixation technique.

Moreover, assessing the oral hygiene status of already compromised patients is mandatory as numerous diseases and medications impact the oral cavity, and pathologic conditions of the mouth which further have an impact on the systematic health. Henceforth, this important parameter was also explored in this prospective longitudinal 6 week trial. Significantly lower (p = 0.001*) OHI-S scores for the Ultralock Ezy Bar groups were seen at each and every follow-up visits in relation to control group, i.e. arch bars. Oral hygiene is difficult to maintain in arch bars where interdental wiring is essential. This also causes gingival and periodontal injuries as round wire edges provide site for accumulation of food debris, difficulty practising personal oral hygiene due to arch bars ultimately leading to gingival inflammation.

Treatment of fractures includes restoring anatomy and eliminating deformity, restoring occlusion and masticatory abilities, minimizing morbidity, early return to function [8]. In process of fully satisfying these criteria, arch bar stability was also assessed in the present study. The prevalence of arch bar stability was found to be significantly higher (p = 0.05) in Ultralock Ezy Bar (100%) as compared to Control group: Erich Arch Bar (80%) at sixth week only. With comparison, the other authors have chosen separate patients for stability, but in our study as we did in both the arch bar trial in same patient to compare, thus result could not be correlated. In present study, the arch bar with the help of these screws was securely adapted and provided the necessary stabilization. No incidence of screw loosening was observed in the present study. Also, the improper tightening of wires could have attributed in making the arch bars loosen for patients belonging to control group in the present study.

Patient satisfaction is an important measure of healthcare quality as it offers information on the provider’s success at meeting clients’ expectations and it is a key determinant of patients’ perspective behavioural intention [13]. Although the patient comfort and acceptance showed no significant result among two groups (p > 0.05), on the day of surgery (Intra-operatively), fair satisfaction response was seen among 80% patients of Ultralock EZY bar as compared to Erich arch bars (75%). Longer duration time required in placement of Erich arch bars could have been responsible for minor dissatisfaction among the patients. This is similar to Qureshi et al. [3] who had stated that patient acceptance and comfort were significantly high in patients with IMF screws in relation to arch bars.

The pulp vitality pre- and post-operatively among two treatment groups failed to reach the level of significance, although the post-operative non-vitality characteristics were found more in Ultralock EZY bar than with Erich arch bars. This could have been due to improper angulations of drill bit during drilling of hole for insertion of screws resulting in root impingement. Similar findings were reported by Nandini et al. [12], Roccia et al. [14], and Borah and Ashmead [15] in their respective studies in which more non-vital cases were seen with IMF screws. Nandini et al. [12] also reported that self-tapping screws cause injury to roots of teeth adjacent to screw fixation site. They even further suggested that operator should be confident when he/she feels drop in medullary bone after perforating the buccal cortex as failure to sense change in resistance increases possibility of bur being partly or fully moving in tooth root.

In the present study, no case of screw fracture or screw breakage was encountered. This is in contrast to that reported by Holmes and Hutchison [16] and Coburn et al. [11], who reported a single case of fracture of screw at the junction of screw head and threaded portion. In the present study, only 20 sites were recruited. Fewer number of cases could have been one of the reason for no such complication encountered. The prevalence of complications of screw loosening was not seen. This is similar to that reported by Balaban [17] where incidence of screw loosening was seen in 16.67%. The reason for the same could have been the force of musculature. This force is exerted while the patient is undergoing MMF, or in those patients where the direction of screw placed is not perpendicular to the occlusal plane.

The only complication seen was mucosal growth in test group, i.e. in Ultralock EZY bar at 6th week (Fig. 3) :—15%. Similar findings were reported by Balaban [17] and Chhabra et al. [18] and West GH [19]. Arch bar removal stab incision was given over mucosal growth to expose screw head in the present study.

In this study, in Erich arch bar cases, irritation had been seen due to impingement of wire which has managed itself within a week; thus, it could not be further evaluated. Furthermore, the prevalence of wire breakage was seen in control group: arch bar at 4th week and at 6th week. This was in accordance to the study of Gordon et al. [10] where breakage of wire was observed in 38% cases of arch bar group and new wire was applied to strengthen fixation and no incidence of dimac wire was reported. As the wires were twisted tightly with wire twister to anchor the bar to dental arch, wire fatigue and loosening and breakage do occur and this can only be avoided by preferring alternative treatment modalities such as Ultralock EZY bar.

Within our knowledge, no such prospective and retrospective study had compared these two modalities which makes the present study unique. However, the small sample size could be considered as one of the limitation of this study. Secondly, periodontological assessment could have been done, as damage to the surrounding tissue occurs throughout the complete treatment duration. Gingival inflammation, probing depth, and clinical attachment level could have been assessed for comparing the two treatment procedures, i.e. Ultralock Ezy bar and Erich arch bar. Though both the treatment modalities have been tested in single patient to avoid bias in assessing subjective parameter but for the stability we did not have objective method to evaluate. Pain could also have been assessed using Visual Analogue Scale at pre-operative, intra-operative and post-operative time intervals.

All the procedure performed in this study involving human participants were in accordance with the ethical standards of Sudha Rustagi Dental College and Hospital, Faridabad, under ethical committee.

Conclusion

Thus, Ultralock EZY bar resulted to be quick, safer, and versatile method compared to conventional Erich arch bar in the treatment of fractures. Ultralock EZY bar reduces the operating time and the risk of injuries due to wires to the surgeon and subsequently the transmission of diseases. Due to discrepancy of cleats in Erich arch bar, there could be more retention of microbes in mouth so oral hygiene maintenance was better with Ultralock Ezy bar including enhanced patient acceptance. Thus, it can be postulated that the use of Ultralock EZY bar may lead to a higher quality of life; thus, further more studies are required with large number of sample size.
