## Principe du désossage

> « Boning has several advantages over the conventional methods of cutting up deer. It separates
> the choice pieces of venison from tough connective tissue, tendons, off-grained meat and
> excessive fat. […] The bone dust and marrow from the meat saw is avoided ».

> « the best position for boning a deer is when it is hanging from a gambrel by both hind legs.
> If placed on a flat surface, the animal can be quartered, halved or left whole. »

## Épaule

Fig. 2 : « The first cut is to remove a front shoulder. Lift the front leg up from the chest and
cut the meat attaching it to the side of the ribs. »

Fig. 3 : « Then work the knife back and forth to cut the connective tissue between the leg and the
rib cage. The last cut to be wade [sic] will be at the top of the shoulder blade where it is
attached to the withers. »

Fig. 4 : « Cut the front leg into three parts - shoulder or blade roast, arm roast, and shank. »

Fig. 5 : « Remove the shoulder roast from the leg by severing at the joint. Locate the joint by
moving the shoulder blade up and down and then cut through it. […] For those who prefer steak,
the muscles on each side of the bony ridge on the shoulder blade can be removed. »

Fig. 7 : « Trim out the meat from the heavy tendons and tissue of the shank. This meat is good for
grinding. »

## Longe (« loin or backstrap »)

Fig. 8 : « The loin lies in the groove between the ribs and the vertical spines of the backbone.
Three basic cuts are made. Cut 1: Cut the flank loose just in front of the hind leg and extend the
cut all the way to the backbone. Cut 2: Use the vertical spine of the backbone as a guide and cut
forward along these until you reach the base of the neck. Cut 3: Make the side-of-rib cut directly
over the point where the ribs curve down to join the backbone. »

Fig. 10 : « Remove the loin by cutting it loose from the ribs. Lift the loin and start cutting and
peeling it from the groove, continuing forward until loin meat ends or the base of the neck is
reached. »

Fig. 11 : « Pull and rip the layer of fat and connective tissue from the loin. The loin meat will
still be encased in thin connective tissue. Remove the loosely attached meat near the neck end of
the loin. »

## Flanc, cou, côtes

Fig. 13 : « Remove the flank by cutting it free from the backbone and the adjoining ribs. »

Fig. 14 : « Remove the neck by sawing the backbone off just in front of the point of the
shoulder. »

Fig. 15 : « Remove the side of rib from the backbone. Use the saw and cut the ribs loose at the
point where they curve enroute to connecting with the backbone. »

## Filet

Fig. 19 : « Remove the tenderloin by lifting and cutting it free from the backbone. The two
tenderloins are located on each side of the backbone and just forward of the pelvic area. »

## Cuisse

Fig. 24-25 : « To cut up the hind leg, remove the sirloin roast first. To do this, locate the ball
and socket joint which connects the pelvic bones of the sirloin roast with the large leg bone.
Feel for the joint as you raise and lower the sirloin roast. […] Cut down to the ball-and-socket
joint and work the knife through it […] Separating with a knife instead of sawing avoids spreading
bone marrow and dust. »

Fig. 27-28 : « Next cut the sirloin tip from the remainder of the hind leg. First, set the leg in a
vertical position. Make a cut above the knee cap. Cut directly down to the large leg bone […] Lay
the hind leg flat on the table and remove the sirloin tip by using the leg bone to guide the knife
while cutting off the large chunk of meat. […] Start at the knee cap and keep the knife in a
vertical plane against this bone. »

Fig. 29 : « Once the sirloin tip has been cut from the leg, remove the connective tissue and extra
meat surrounding it. The remaining chunk of sirloin tip meat will be enclosed in a thin connective
tissue and will resemble an undersized football. »

Fig. 31 : « To remove the bone from the round, start by cutting the tendon and connective tissue
that separates the shank from the round and the bone. »

Fig. 35 : « the leg bone has been separated from the large chunk of round. […] The next step is to
separate the round into its individual muscles, each of which is enclosed in its individual
envelope of connective tissue. »

Fig. 36 : « Divide or separate the large leg muscles in the round by cutting the connective tissue
holding them together. A void [sic] cutting into the meat as much as you can. The meat close to the
long tendon does not make good steak meat, it is too tough. »

## Ce que la documentation des planches en tire

- **Chez un cervidé**, la longe est dans la gouttière entre les apophyses épineuses et le haut
  des côtes ; les deux traits qui la bornent sont le long des épines et au coude des côtes.
- La pointe de surlonge se lève **en partant de la rotule, lame à plat contre le fémur** ; sortie,
  elle a la forme d'un ballon (« undersized football ») dans sa membrane.
- La ronde, une fois le fémur sorti, se sépare **muscle par muscle, chacun dans son enveloppe**.
- La cuisse se détache du bassin **à la tête du fémur**, au couteau.
