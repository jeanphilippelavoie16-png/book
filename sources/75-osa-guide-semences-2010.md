## Extraits intégraux

### Séchage à l'air : la température qui abîme (p. 13)

> « Seeds can become damaged when the ambient temperature rises above 95F (35C) and dark colored
> seeds can become overheated when exposed to direct sunlight, especially at temperatures at or
> above 80F (27C). Therefore, take care to dry your seed in a location that is warm, but not too
> warm, and dry them out of the sun if necessary. »

### Au déshydrateur (p. 16)

> « Use a food dehydrator with temperature controls that go down to 85F (29C). Set the
> dehydrator at 85F (29C) and check your seed often to avoid over-drying. »

### Savoir si c'est « très sec », sans instrument (p. 16)

> « Brittleness test — Test small, oblong seeds, or thin ones (such as squash) by bending them.
> If they are "very dry," they will snap instead of just bending. With bigger seeds such as
> corn, beans, or peas; put seeds on a hard surface and hit them with a hammer. If they are
> "very dry," they will shatter like glass when hit. If seeds are inadequately dry, they will
> smash or mush instead of shattering. »

> « Envelope test — Put an envelope (or some other paper item) in your lot of seed overnight.
> The next morning, compare the envelope left with the seed to an envelope that was not with the
> seed. Does it seem moister than the envelope not left with the seed? Is it more malleable, or
> is it as crisp as the other envelope? If the envelope left with the seed seems dry as or drier
> than the envelope left at ambient conditions, then you can consider the seed to be "very
> dry." This test works best where paper tends to stay crisp and dry at ambient conditions. »

> « A general guideline is that small seeds are "very dry" and ready to be stored in containers
> that restrict moisture release at approximately 5% seed moisture. Large seeds are ready at 7%
> seed moisture. »

### Gel de silice (p. 16)

> « Measure out an equal weight of silica gel and place the packet of seed and silica gel in an
> airtight container. […] they should remain in the drying container for 2-7 days. […] Check
> daily for dryness, because seed left with silica gel too long may lose too much moisture and
> become damaged or go dormant. Small seeds should not be dried below 3% moisture, and large
> seeds should not be dried below 5% moisture. »

### Congélateur et insectes (p. 17)

> « Freezer - Protects against insects and rodents. Kills insects. Good for long term storage of
> seeds. A freezer is so cold and dry that moist, unprotected seed will be damaged. Seed should
> be in an air-tight container and must be "very dry." If seeds are taken from frozen storage
> and transferred to room temperate, prevent condensation on the seeds by allowing them reach
> room temperature before opening the container. »

> « Insects such as mites, weevils, beetles, and moths can be present in your seed at harvest,
> and they can find your seed in storage. If you have cause to believe that your seed is
> infested, you can kill the pests by putting your seed in the freezer for a few days. Your seed
> must be "very dry" before you put it in the freezer, and it should be in an air-tight
> container. »

### Taille de population (p. 8-9)

> « Plants on the strong outbreeder side of the mating spectrum are most susceptible to
> inbreeding depression as they are adapted to a constant mixing of genes through
> cross-pollination. while those on the strong inbreeder side of the mating spectrum are the
> least susceptible […]. A notable exception to this rule are the crops of the Cucurbitaceae
> (cucumbers, gourds, melons, squashes, and watermelons); as these crops are largely
> outbreeders, but are not as severly affected by inbreeding. »

> « Please note that the recommendations in our Crop Specific Chart are for the final number of
> plants from which you should harvest seed, not the initial number that you plant in the
> spring. »

> « If you cannot grow as many plants as we recommend, try growing as many plants as possible in
> the space you have available and see what happens. Stay vigilant for signs of inbreeding
> depression such as reduced reproductive capacity (lowered seed yield or fewer flowers),
> reduced vigor and general health of the crop. If you notice signs of inbreeding depression,
> you can try introducing seed of the same variety from an outside source to reinvigorate your
> variety. »

## Lecture pour le livre

- **Chaleur :** au-dessus de **35 °C** d'air ambiant, la semence peut être abîmée ; au soleil
  direct, une graine foncée surchauffe dès **27 °C**. Au déshydrateur : **29 °C**.
- **Nombre de plants :** allogames 80 en général, 200 pour la carotte, l'oignon et le maïs ; les
  cucurbitacées se contentent de 10 à 20. Même les autogames : 10 à 20 pour garder la variété
  en santé.
- **Insectes :** « quelques jours » au congélateur, semence très sèche, contenant étanche, et
  on laisse revenir à la température de la pièce avant d'ouvrir. Pas de durée plus précise.
- **Sans instrument :** le test de l'enveloppe et le test du marteau disent « très sec ».
