## Pourquoi cette source prime, malgré une adresse de la Colombie-Britannique

C'est le **seul document canadien trouvé qui chiffre la fermentation des légumes**. Il n'est pas
provincial au sens ordinaire : il est signé par un groupe de travail national dont la liste des
membres, reproduite en page 2, comprend **« Julie Samson, Caroline Frigault, Yosra Ben Fadhel,
Marie-Eve Rousseau — Quebec Ministère de l'Agriculture, des Pêcheries et de l'Alimentation »**,
c'est-à-dire le MAPAQ, ainsi qu'Agriculture et Agroalimentaire Canada, Santé publique Ontario et
les ministères de la santé de sept autres provinces. Le document précise que le secrétariat
fédéral-provincial-territorial en a fourni la traduction française.

**Réserve de lecture, à ne pas escamoter.** Le document s'adresse aux inspecteurs en santé
publique et aux exploitants d'établissements alimentaires, pas à une cuisine de chalet. Il le dit
lui-même : « it does not replace or supersede federal and provincial guidance or regulatory
requirements ». Le chapitre 77 en retient **les seuils microbiologiques et les points de contrôle
critiques**, qui ne dépendent pas de l'échelle, et laisse de côté tout ce qui relève de
l'inspection, de l'étiquetage et de la vente.

## Extraits intégraux, relevés le 2026-09-20

### Le seuil qui commande tout le chapitre

> « Ensure effective fermentation and end pH of 4.6 is achieved. Ensure a pH of 4.6 or lower is
> maintained during the intended shelf life to control *C. botulinum* risk. »

> « *C. botulinum* spores may germinate in fermented foods when pH rises above 4.6 during
> spoilage. »

> « *C. botulinum* will not grow at pH of 4.6 or below, *L. monocytogenes* control requires a pH
> of lower than 4.4. However, *E. coli* and *Salmonella* are acid-tolerant and may persist in
> fermented foods unless held. »

> « A pH of lower than 4.4 is recommended for unpasteurized pickled vegetables held under
> refrigeration for *L. monocytogenes* control. »

### Ce qu'est un légume fermenté, par opposition à un légume acidifié

> « Fermented vegetables are low acid vegetables that are subjected to activity of acid-producing
> micro-organisms that release organic acids and reduce pH to 4.6 or lower, regardless of whether
> any acid is added. »

> « Acidified vegetables refer to any acid added to preserve nonfermented vegetables that have a
> natural pH above 4.6. »

### Le sel — tableau 3, « Examples of LAB salt/brine concentrations »

| Légume | Sel recommandé (extrait mot à mot) |
| --- | --- |
| **Cabbage (sauerkraut)** | **« 1 to 3%, 2.25% optimal »** |
| Carrots | « 5% » — avec lessivage à la soude préalable |
| Cucumbers (pickles) | « 3 to 15%, depending on variety » |
| Pickled garlic | « 8% » |
| Olives (green Spanish-style) | « 10 to 12% » |
| Mexican peppers | « 18 to 20% » |

> « Vegetables are placed into containers and covered with a brine containing salt between 2 to
> 10% (or higher), depending on the vegetable. »

> « Only pickling or canning salts should be used. Iodized salts, sea salts and other speciality
> salts are not recommended as they may contain heavy metals, anti-caking agents or other agents
> that interfere with fermentation. Problems linked to non food-grade salts include discolouration
> of the fermentation brine, and **masking of spoilage**. »

> « Low salt, generally less than 0.8% can result in poor LAB growth, and soft-textured
> vegetables. »

> « In addition to influencing microbial activity, and adding flavour, salt causes rupture of cell
> membranes during fermentation. As fluids are released from cells, the percentage of salt
> decreases in the fermentation. »

### La température

> « The ideal temperature for a cucumber pickle fermentation is 20-22°C for approximately 3 to 4
> weeks. However, fermentation can occur at higher or lower temperatures, generally taking less
> time at higher temperatures and proceeding more slowly at lower temperatures. At temperatures of
> 15-20°C fermentation periods of 5 to 6 weeks are typical. However, fermentation at temperatures
> higher than 25°C may result in spoilage. »

> « High temperatures >25°C. LAB grows very fast with rapid acid production resulting in poor
> flavour. »

> « Low temperatures <10°C. Delayed start of fermentation, that can lead to spoilage of product
> and off-flavours. »

### Le pH atteint

> « Cucumbers have an initial pH of 5 to 6.4. During fermentation the cucumbers and brine become
> acidified to a final pH of 3.2 to 3.6. Similarly, sauerkraut will have a final pH endpoint of
> 3.2 to 3.4. »

### La règle de rejet — le seul critère de mise au rebut chiffré du dossier

Citant Snyder et coll. (2020), avec le kimchi comme exemple :

> « At room temperature fermentation, test pH of brine every 12 hours and demonstrate pH decrease
> to 4.6 within 48 hours; »

> « At refrigerated temperature fermentation, test pH of brine every day and demonstrate pH
> decrease to 4.6 within 4 days; »

> « **If pH has not dropped to 4.6 within one week consider this a failed fermentation and discard
> product.** (Note: in our guidance for kimchi reviewed in Section 3.3, the critical limit is three
> days for a room temperature fermentation and one week for a refrigerated fermentation). »

> « In a recent review by Snyder et al., (2020), tracking pH to ensure successful fermentation is
> occurring is **the only critical control of significance**, and pH drop is required to establish
> a 5-log reduction in harmful enterohemorrhagic *E. coli*, the pathogen of significance in
> vegetable fermentations. »

> « Our main recommendation is monitoring and recording pH and time as the CCP for a successful
> vegetable fermentation. »

### L'immersion et l'air

> « This is an anaerobic fermentation, so it is important to ensure air is excluded, keep
> vegetables fully submerged in the brine. »

> « **There should be at least 3 cm or more of liquid brine between the fermented vegetables and
> the top of the brine.** »

> « Surface moulds and oxidative yeasts can grow readily on the surface of fermenting vegetables. »

> « During the fermentation, containers should allow gas to vent to avoid containers exploding. »

### Les contenants

> « Containers should be food-grade and acid-resistant. Avoid ceramic or other pottery with glazes
> that may leach. »

> « Metal containers are not suitable due to their reactivity with acid. Plastic can be used but is
> subject to scratching which can harbour bacteria and may leech potential contaminants. Some
> pottery glazes are not acid safe and may leach contaminants such as heavy metals into the food if
> used. »

> « Glass and stone crocks are common and generally well suited. »

### L'huile — la phrase qui ferme une porte

> « Recipes calling for the addition of oil should be examined with caution as when oil is added it
> can create micro-environments of oil bubbles that may allow growth of *C. botulinum*. Use of
> mustard oil is not permitted in Canada. »

### Le délai avant de commencer, et le délai avant de manger

> « Once the vegetables are prepared the fermentation process should be initiated as soon as
> possible, and within a maximum of 24 hours. »

> « Cutting vegetables increases the surface area available for microbial spoilage and pathogen
> growth. »

> « Hold completed fermented vegetables for a minimum of two weeks before distribution,
> particularly short-term fermentations (<1 week), to allow time for acid-tolerant pathogens to
> die-off. »

### La pasteurisation, et ce qu'elle coûte

> « They may be pasteurized to shelf stable conditions (e.g., to 74°C for 15 min) for room
> temperature storage. »

> « If no step is taken to stop fermentation, and the product has not been verified to be shelf
> stable (for example, product maintains a pH of 4.6 or lower to end of shelf life), then the
> fermented vegetables must be stored refrigerated. »

> « Because the fermentation contains live microbes, spoilage and regrowth of microbes may increase
> the pH to levels higher than 4.6, allowing *C. botulinum* spores to grow in the product, creating
> an unacceptable risk. »

### *Listeria* dans un pot réfrigéré

> « The survival of *L. monocytogenes* in fermented refrigerator pickles has been previously
> demonstrated, showing survival of this pathogen for up to **91 days** during refrigerated
> storage. »

> « *Listeria monocytogenes* can persist in refrigerated pickled vegetables for months. »

### *Staphylococcus aureus*

> « *Staphylococcus aureus* may grow at salt concentrations between 7 to 10%, and to a pH of 4.2,
> therefore sanitary handling is important during the process and during handling, such as
> packaging. »

### Éclosions — ce que la statistique dit et ne dit pas

> « There have been no documented foodborne outbreaks involving sauerkraut (covered in the next
> guidance section, 3.2) or fermented vegetables that have been made under good manufacturing
> practices and achieve a pH of 4.6 or lower. »

> « No outbreaks linked to fermented vegetables have occurred in Canada. »

Et l'exception, qui est l'exception instructive :

> « In Italy, however, green olives were associated with an outbreak of *C. botulinum*. The olives
> were soaked in salt water for approximately 35 days, the brine decanted, replaced with fresh
> water and stored in jars at room temperature. **The pH of the olives was measured at 6.5.** The
> recipe used by the restaurant was not standardized, amount of salt or pH through the process used
> was unknown. »

Le tableau 2 chiffre cette éclosion : Italie, 2004, *C. botulinum*, **28 malades, 15 hospitalisés**.

### Les légumes couramment fermentés

> « Vegetables most commonly fermented include cucumbers, beets, olives, peppers, radishes, onions,
> okra, ginger and other vegetables. »

### Le lavage à la soude des carottes — à lire avant de le recopier

> « Carrots receive a lye wash before they are placed into brine. The lye wash is a sodium hydroxide
> (NaOH) solution of 1-2%, and the brine is a NaCl concentration of 5%. »

## Ce que la page ne dit pas, et qu'il ne faut donc pas lui faire dire

1. **Elle ne donne aucune teneur en vitamine C.** Cette donnée vient de la section 3.2 et du
   Fichier canadien sur les éléments nutritifs.
2. **Elle ne donne aucun barème pour la fermentation de la viande ou du poisson dans un cadre
   domestique.** Les sections 3.5 (fesikh) et 3.13 (saucisson) traitent le sujet, et c'est de la
   fabrication sous inspection.
3. **Elle ne dit pas qu'un légume fermenté se garde hors du froid.** Elle dit le contraire :
   sans pasteurisation vérifiée, réfrigération obligatoire.
4. **Le lavage à la soude des carottes est un procédé industriel.** Le document le décrit, il ne le
   recommande pas à qui que ce soit hors d'un établissement. Le chapitre 77 le mentionne uniquement
   pour fermer la porte : une carotte se fermente très bien sans soude, en morceaux, dans une
   saumure — mais **le 5 % de saumure du tableau 3 est couplé au lessivage**, et le chapitre ne
   transpose donc pas ce chiffre tel quel.
5. **Le 2,25 % de sel du chou est en m/m (poids sur poids)**, ce qui correspond exactement à la
   convention du livre : pourcentage calculé sur le poids du légume.
