## Extraits intégraux (section 3, « Operation »)

### Délai de formation de la couche biologique

> « It may take up to 30 days for the biolayer to fully form. During that time, both the removal
> efficiency and the oxygen demand will increase as the biolayer grows. The biolayer is NOT
> visible - it is NOT a green slimy coating on top of the sand. The filtration sand may turn a
> darker colour, but this is due to the suspended solids that have become trapped. »

> « The water from the filter can be used during the first few weeks while the biolayer is being
> established, but disinfection, as always, is recommended during this time. »

> « The process may vary as some filters require a shorter or longer period of time to establish
> the biolayer depending on the amount and source of water being used. »

### Ce que fait la couche biologique

> « The biolayer is the key component of the filter that removes pathogens. Without it, the
> filter removes about 30-70% of the pathogens through mechanical trapping and adsorption. The
> ideal biolayer will increase the treatment efficiency up to 99% removal of pathogens. »

### Changement de source d'eau

> « It may take the biolayer several days to adapt to the new source water, level of
> contamination, and nutrients. It is recommended to consistently use the same source water to
> ensure the highest treatment efficiency. »

### Pause, et mort de la couche

> « The pause period should be a minimum of 1 hour after the water has stopped flowing up to a
> maximum of 48 hours. »

> « If the pause period is extended for too long, the microorganisms will eventually consume all
> of the nutrients and pathogens and then eventually die off. This will reduce the removal
> efficiency of the filter when it is used again. »

### Lame d'eau au-dessus du sable, et assèchement

> « Correct installation and operation of the biosand filter requires a standing water depth of
> approximately 5 cm (2") above the sand during the pause period. »

> « A water depth of greater than 5 cm (2") results in lower oxygen diffusion and consequently a
> thinner biolayer. »

> « A water depth less than 5 cm (2") may evaporate quickly in hot climates and cause the
> biolayer to dry out. »

### Entretien

> « A slower flow rate is not an issue in terms of water quality. In fact, the slower the flow
> rate, the better the water quality. »

### Désinfection après le filtre

> « Although the water may look clear after filtration, it is still necessary to disinfect it »

### Test visuel de turbidité (section 3.1)

> « A simple test to measure the turbidity is to use a 2 litre clear plastic bottle filled with
> the source water. Place this on top of large print such as the CAWST logo on this manual. If
> you can see this logo looking down through the top of the bottle, the water probably has a
> turbidity of less than 50 NTU. »

> « It is recommended to use a sedimentation method if the source water turbidity is greater
> than 50 NTU. »

## Ce que ça change au chapitre

- **Délai :** jusqu'à **30 jours** pour que la couche biologique soit complète — « des semaines »
  est juste. Pendant ce temps, l'eau se boit **désinfectée**.
- La couche **ne se voit pas** : le sable qui fonce, c'est de la saleté prise, pas le film.
- Elle meurt si le filtre reste **plus de 48 heures sans eau neuve** (elle n'a plus rien à
  manger) et si la lame d'eau au-dessus du sable s'évapore. Rien sur le gel dans ce manuel.
- Sans couche biologique, le sable seul retire 30 à 70 % des pathogènes ; avec elle, jusqu'à
  99 %. Jamais 100 % : on désinfecte après.
- Test visuel : lire un gros caractère à travers une bouteille de 2 L ne garantit que
  **moins de 50 UNT** — cent soixante fois le 0,3 UNT du chapitre.

## Ajout du 18 octobre 2026 (vérification technique du ch. 10) : laver le sable, et le débit qui tombe

Tirés du même PDF (`13-cawst-2009-biosand-filter-manual.pdf`, texte extrait avec `pdftotext`).

### Laver le sable (p. 23)

> « Put double the amount of water in the container. Using your hand, swirl the sand around the container 10 times
> very quickly, making sure your fingers touch the bottom of the container and get all of the sand moving. Quickly
> decant the dirty water. »

> « Tip: Do NOT wash the sand until the water in your container is clean. This residual water should still be
> somewhat dirty. »

> « Unlike the gravel, the water in your container should not run clear. »

> « put some sand into a clear container with an equal amount of clear water. Put the lid on and swirl it. Looking
> from the side of the container, 3-4 seconds after you stop swirling, you should be able to see the surface of the
> sand. »

### Sable trop lavé (p. 51)

> « If the flow rate is faster than 0.4 L/minute, the filter may not work efficiently. In this case, the sand has
> been washed too much and needs to be replaced. »

### La couche vivante (p. 3)

> « […] develops at the top 1-2 cm […] »

### « Swirl & dump » quand le débit tombe (p. 56)

> « 4. Using the palm of your hand, lightly touch the very top of the sand and move your hand in a circular motion;
> be careful to not mix the top of the sand deeper into the filter. 5. Scoop out the dirty water with a small
> container. 6. Dump the dirty water outside the house in soak pit or garden. 7. Make certain the sand is smooth and
> level. […] 12. Repeat the swirl & dump steps until the flow rate has been restored. »

> « The biolayer has been disturbed by the swirl and dump, but it will develop again over time. It is recommended to
> disinfect the filtered water during this time. »

Ce que le livre en tire : on lave le gravier jusqu'à l'eau claire, mais pas le sable (on arrête quand l'eau de
lavage est encore un peu sale ; test du pot : on voit le dessus du sable 3 ou 4 secondes après l'avoir secoué).
Quand le débit tombe, on frotte à peine le dessus du sable de la paume, en rond, et on puise l'eau sale ; on ne
gratte pas 2 ou 3 cm, qui enlèveraient toute la couche vivante.
