# Microbiologie du levain — quatre articles scientifiques

- **Type de source :** ⚠ articles scientifiques évalués par les pairs, pas des sources
  officielles. Aucune page gouvernementale ne décrit la microbiologie d'un levain. Ce sont les
  meilleures sources disponibles ; le relecteur humain du chapitre les tranche au besoin.
- **Date de consultation :** 2026-09-23
- **Sert au chapitre :** 14b — Le pain (§3, §5-A, §5-B, §6, §7, §9-A)

## 1. Reese et coll. 2020 — d'où viennent les microbes du levain

- **Référence :** Reese AT, Madden AA, Joossens M, Lacaze G, Dunn RR. « Influences of ingredients
  and bakers on the bacteria and fungi in sourdough starters and bread ». *mSphere*
  2020;5(1):e00950-19. doi:10.1128/mSphere.00950-19
- **URL :** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6968659/

> « When starters are made, the first microorganisms with the opportunity to colonize the
> starter are those in the flour itself. »

> « Here, we show that starters generally resemble the flour used to make them. But,
> interestingly, a large proportion of the bacteria and yeasts in sourdough starters can also be
> found on the hands of bakers. »

> « In short, on day 1 of the protocol, bakers mixed together a 1:1 mix of flour and unchlorinated
> water and maintained it at room temperature covered. On subsequent days, the sourdough starter
> was fed by removing 120 ml of starter then adding 240 ml of flour and 180 ml of water. Feeding
> was repeated daily until the starter doubled overnight. »

> « As expected, the starters were acidic (pH mean, 4.50 ± 0.38 [...]) »

> « Regardless of identity or geography, all bakers successfully made a starter which would later
> be used to bake bread. »

## 2. Oshiro et coll. 2021 — l'ordre d'arrivée : les bactéries d'abord, la levure ensuite

- **Référence :** Oshiro M, Tanaka M, Momoda R, Zendo T, Nakayama J. « Mechanistic insight into
  yeast bloom in a lactic acid bacteria relaying-community in the start of sourdough microbiota
  evolution ». *Microbiol Spectr* 2021;9:e00662-21. doi:10.1128/Spectrum.00662-21
- **URL :** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8528097/

> « The final product quality largely depends on the microbiota functions, but these fluctuate
> sometimes during the initial months of fermentation cycles due to microbiota evolution in which
> three phases of LAB relay occur. »

> « During the first six cycles, the LAB of all sourdoughs increased to >8.6 log CFU/g while
> acidifying until the pH values were <4.0 (Fig. 1A). An increase in the yeast population was
> observed after an increase in LAB. Notably, the time at which the yeast population began to
> increase differed among batches »

> « The maltose-fermenting yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae has been reported as the most common
> yeast species in 68% of 394 sourdoughs (7). »

## 3. Pérez-Alvarado et coll. 2022 — le levain traditionnel (type I)

- **Référence :** Pérez-Alvarado O, Zepeda-Hernández A, Garcia-Amezquita LE, Requena T,
  Vinderola G, et coll. « Role of lactic acid bacteria and yeasts in sourdough fermentation
  during breadmaking ». *Front Microbiol* 2022;13:969460. doi:10.3389/fmicb.2022.969460
- **URL :** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9524358/

> « The type I or traditional SD process depends on the backslopping technique at a low
> incubation temperature (20°C–24°C), which relies on a repeated cyclic of re-inoculation
> (6–24 h) with a new batch of flour and water from a previous one derived from a mother dough
> (Figure 1I). This type of SD is a pure craft, and the dough can be maintained for years. »

> « Baking is carried out at a temperature up to 200°C–250°C and for an average duration of 1 h »

## 4. Li et coll. 2024 — ce que le gel fait au levain

- **Référence :** Li H, Lv Y, Zhang Y, Wang X, Yang X, Qu J. « Fermentation properties and
  functional stability of dough starter Jiaozi and Laomian after frozen storage ». *Front
  Microbiol* 2024;15:1379484. doi:10.3389/fmicb.2024.1379484
- **URL :** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/microbiology/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2024.1379484/full

> « In our previous study, the fermentation ability (FA) of yeast in LM almost decreased to 0,
> while an FA of more than 40% was maintained in JZ after storage at −20°C for 180 days. »
> (LM, *laomian* : levain de pâte de blé, pH initial d'environ 4.)

> « the leavening ability decreased after 4 days of frozen storage (Almeida and Pais, 1996).
> Moreover, the sourdoughs that reactivated after 90 days of storage at −20°C hardly showed
> measurable leavening capacity. Freezing and thawing may cause severe injury to yeast cells and
> then lower the fermentation ability »

> « The yeast (normally referred to as Saccharomyces cerevisiae) is always well-grown between 20
> and 30°C, and its viability would be greatly inhibited below 0°C »

## Note du rédacteur

**Contradiction avec le ch. 12 :** « Le levain, dans l'air » (ch. 12 §9). Reese 2020 montre que
les microbes du levain viennent d'abord de la **farine**, et en partie des **mains** ; l'air
(poussière) n'est pas la source principale. Le ch. 14b écrit « dans la farine » et remonte la
correction dans les décisions pour JP.

La page Parcs Canada de la piste Chilkoot (`14b-parcs-canada-pains-patrimoine.md`) reprend la
croyance des prospecteurs (levain « laissé à découvert pour qu'il soit inoculé ») : c'est un
récit historique, pas une explication.
