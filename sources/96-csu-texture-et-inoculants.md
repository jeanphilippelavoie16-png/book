# Colorado State University Extension — texture du sol à la main ; inoculants de légumineuses

## A. CMG GardenNotes #214, « Estimating Soil Texture: Sandy, Loamy, or Clayey »

- **Organisme :** Colorado State University Extension (D. Whiting, A. Card, C. Wilson, J. Reeder),
  révisé en septembre 2022
- **URL :** https://cmg.extension.colostate.edu/Gardennotes/214.pdf
- **Page enregistrée :** `96-csu-gardennotes-214-texture-du-sol.pdf` (HTTP 200)
- **Consultée le :** 27 septembre 2026
- **Sert dans :** chapitre 96, §5-A, §9
- **Rang :** vulgarisation universitaire. Aucune page québécoise officielle trouvée pour le test à
  la main ; la méthode est la même partout.

Extraits intégraux (le bocal) : « Fill a tall, slender jar (like a quart jar) one-quarter full of
soil. » — « Add water until the jar is three-quarters full. » — « Add a teaspoon of powdered,
non-foaming dishwasher detergent. » — « shake hard for 10 to 15 minutes » — « After 1 minute, mark on
the jar the depth of the sand. » — « After 2 hours, mark on the jar the depth of the silt. » —
« When the water clears, mark on the jar the clay level. This typically takes 1 to 3 days; some soils
may take weeks. »

Extraits intégraux (à la main) : « Sand feels gritty. • Silt feels smooth. • Clays feel sticky. » —
« Ribbons less than 1 inch before breaking: Feels gritty = coarse texture (sandy) soil. […] Ribbons
1 to 2 inches before breaking. Feels gritty = medium texture soil. Not gritty feeling = fine texture
soil. • Ribbons greater than 2 inches = fine texture (clayey) soil. » — « A soil with as little as
20% clay will behave as a clayey soil. »

Conversions : 1 po = 2,5 cm ; 2 po = 5 cm.

## B. « Legume Seed Inoculants »

- **URL :** https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/legume-seed-inoculants/
- **Page enregistrée :** `96-csu-inoculants-legumineuses.html` (HTTP 200)
- **Consultée le :** 27 septembre 2026
- **Sert dans :** chapitre 96, §3, §5-D, §6, §7, §9

Extraits intégraux :

> « If the legume crop was grown in the field previously, there is a good chance that the soil
> already contains the correct rhizobial species for nodulation. However, native rhizobial
> populations found in soil often are less effective in nitrogen fixation potential. »

> « A nodule that is actively fixing nitrogen will be pink to reddish when cut open, rather than tan
> (ineffective) or green (dying). »

> « Acid soils often have poor survival of the rhizobia. »

> « Attempts to supplement the legume nitrogen supply by fertilization usually are counterproductive,
> because plants tend to stop nitrogen fixation when soil nitrogen is high. »

> « Other soils that could be low in molybdenum include those that are strongly weathered or
> leached, sandy soils »
