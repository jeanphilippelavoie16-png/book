## A. Colorado State University Extension — *Frost Protection and Extending the Growing Season*

- **Organisme :** CSU Extension (Colorado State University), fiche Garden & Landscape
- **URL :** https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/frost-protection-and-extending-the-growing-season/
- **Page enregistrée :** `97-csu-protection-gel-allonger-saison.html` (HTTP 200)
- **Consultée le :** 27 septembre 2026
- **Sert au chapitre :** 97 (§3, §5-D, §5-E, §9)

### Extraits intégraux

> « Radiation frosts occur on calm clear nights that lack cloud cover to hold in heat. Radiation
> frosts at the beginning and end of the growing season are typically only a few degrees below
> critical levels, making crop protection worthwhile. »

> « Grandma's old method of covering the garden with blankets and sheets works well as long as
> the fabric remains dry. If the fabric absorbs water, evaporative cooling can lead to colder
> temperatures adjacent to the blanket. To recharge the heat stored in the soil, the blankets and
> sheets must be removed in the daytime. »

> « The covers need to be held off the plants to prevent the plants from freezing where they
> touch the cover and to allow the plants room to grow. They need to be opened or vented on sunny
> days »

> « Two-inch holes cut in the sides of a plastic cover at two to three foot intervals will reduce
> overheating. »

> « Hoops are placed at three to five foot intervals depending on the wind exposure of the site.
> […] Bury the edges of the cover a few inches into the soil or weigh down with rocks, leaving at
> least one side accessible. »

> « In trials, the low and spreading shape was ideal for trapping heat from the soil during a
> frosty night. »

> « During the day, the covering MUST be opened, at least a slit, to prevent overheating. With
> just an hour of sun, temperatures under a closed cover can quickly rise to over 130°F! »

> « On freezing nights, close the cover completely. […] On stormy days with full cloud cover and
> no direct sun, the cover may remain closed. »

> « In trials in Fort Collins, Colorado, a plastic cover on a frame typically provides 3°F to
> over 6°F of frost protection. It works well for cool season crops that are somewhat tolerant of
> frosty nights and adds two to six weeks or more on both ends of the growing season. »

> « With the aluminized side placed down (towards the plants), a space blanket reflects 99% of
> the heat. […] The space blanket must be removed each day to recharge the soil's stored heat. »

> « This device works on the chemistry principle of heat release in a phase change; there is a
> significant amount of heat released as water freezes […] These provide frost protection
> typically down to temperatures in the mid-teens. » (anneau de tubes d'eau autour d'un plant)

> « Both cold air temperatures and cold soil temperatures are limiting factors in early crop
> production. »

- **Pages enregistrées :** `97-umass-couvertures-flottantes.html`, `97-umass-paillis-plastique.html`
  (HTTP 200)
- **Consultées le :** 27 septembre 2026

### Extraits intégraux — couvertures

> « Compared with spun-bonded row covers, clear polyethylene low tunnels provide greater daytime
> heating. »

> « A piece of No. 9 wire cut about 65" long makes a hoop that is about 3' wide at the base and
> 14" tall in the center of the row after inserting each leg of the hoop in the soil. Edges of
> the cover should be secured with soil. »

> « While lightweight row covers do not provide reliable frost protection, they may be helpful
> when temperatures drop 2–3ºF below freezing. Heavyweight covers can provide more frost
> protection, but they block much of the sunlight, resulting in slower growth. »

> « Typically, if outside air temperatures reach around 80°F, it's advisable to consider
> providing additional ventilation. »

> « Medium weight: 0.5–1.0 oz/yd 2 , allow 70%–85% light transmission and give 4–6°F for frost
> protection » ; « Heavyweight: 1.5–2.2 oz/yd 2 , allow 30%–50% light transmission and give up to
> 8°F of frost protection »

> « Those with tender, exposed growing points (tomatoes, peppers, and cucurbit crops) should
> have some support to prevent damage from wind abrasion. »

> « For holding the cover in place, soil is the most secure in high winds »

> « Plastic is removed when plants reach 6–12 inches tall or sooner if temperatures under the
> plastic exceed 100°F, typically at 80–85°F ambient temperature. » (maïs sous plastique clair)

### Extraits intégraux — paillis de plastique (tableau « Effect on soil temperature »)

> « Black | +3°F–5°F | Good » (désherbage) ; « Clear | +8°F–14°F | Poor » ; « Infrared
> Transmitting | +4°F–6°F | Good » ; « White-on-black | -2°F » ; « Silver/metallic | -2°F »

> « When using black mulch, good uniform soil contact is essential, as the soil is warmed by heat
> conduction. »

> « Raised beds offer additional soil drainage and early warming. »
