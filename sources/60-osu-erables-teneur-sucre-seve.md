## Extraits intégraux

### Érable rouge

> « Red Maple (Acer rubrum) […] Habitat: bottomland to upland species. Use for syrup: The downside
> to using red maple is its sap sugar content is usually between 1.5 to 2 percent. This means it
> takes more gallons of sap, more fuel and more time to boil this down to a gallon of syrup. »

### Érable à sucre, pour comparaison

> « Sugar maple is the preferred tree to tap for maple syrup. The sugar content of the sap can run
> 2 percent or higher. »

> « With sugar maple, it takes about 43 gallons of sap to produce 1 gallon of syrup (assuming a 2%
> sap sugar content). The lower the sugar content of the sap, the more gallons of sap that are
> needed to produce a gallon of syrup. Also, the lower the sugar content, the more fuel and time it
> will take to boil down the sap. »

### Nombre d'entailles par diamètre (érables)

> « New tapping guidelines developed at the University of Vermont Proctor Research Center are to use
> one tap for trees 10 to 12 inches in diameter, two taps for trees greater than 24 inches in
> diameter, but no more than two taps per tree. Consider these rates for vigorously growing trees.
> Use reduced rates for trees that are not in excellent health. »

### Érable argenté

> « Silver maple is used to produce syrup, but similar to red, it can be one of the first trees to
> start to bud out in the spring, which shortens the syrup season for the tree. »

## Conversions

10 à 12 po = 25 à 30 cm ; 24 po = 61 cm.

## Limites

- Fiche de l'Ohio, pas de la forêt boréale. La teneur de 1,5 à 2 % est une valeur « usuelle »,
  pas une mesure locale.
- La fiche ne donne ni profondeur ni diamètre de trou pour l'érable : pour ces deux valeurs, voir
  `60-uaf-entaillage-bouleau.md`.
