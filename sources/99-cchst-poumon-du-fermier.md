## Extraits intégraux

> « Farmer's Lung is an allergic disease usually caused by breathing in the dust from mouldy hay.
> However, dust from any mouldy crop - straw, corn, silage, grain, or even tobacco - can also cause
> Farmer's Lung. »

> « Farmer's Lung develops in two steps [...] The first time a person inhales a large amount of dust
> and spores from mouldy hay, the immune system often responds by producing specific antibodies [...]
> further exposure to mouldy dust can generate a hypersensitive type of allergic reaction. »

> « There is less risk when working with loose hay in an open field. However, the risk increases
> when farmers or farm workers are breaking open bales of mouldy hay or straw inside closed barns or
> sheds. A person can inhale an extremely large amount of dust within a very short time while
> working indoors. »

> « Incidents of Farmer's Lung occur most often in late winter or early spring when stored hay or
> grain is used to feed livestock. »

> « Usually, people with Farmer's Lung do not associate their health problems with exposure to mouldy
> hay. »

> « [Acute Farmer's Lung] starts as an intense attack about 4 to 8 hours after the person breathes in
> a large amount of dust from mouldy crops. Signs and symptoms include: shortness of breath, a dry
> irritating cough, a sudden general feeling of sickness, fever and chills, a rapid heart rate, and
> rapid breathing. If the person avoids further exposure to mouldy dust, the signs and symptoms
> usually decrease after 12 hours, but they can last up to two weeks. [...] The symptoms are sometimes
> confused with pneumonia. »

> « [Sub-acute] This condition sometimes resembles a "chest cold" that lingers throughout the winter.
> Some people lose weight over several weeks. »

> « Chronic Farmer's Lung develops after several acute attacks over a period of years. [...] The
> symptoms are accompanied by permanent lung damage and gradually worsen as exposure to mouldy dust
> continues. »

> « For people suffering from acute attacks of Farmer's Lung, the first step in treatment is to avoid
> further contact with mouldy dust. »

> « Once people become hypersensitive, they remain hypersensitive for years, perhaps for life. »

> « During the cleaning of barns or stables, dust from mouldy crops should be wetted down before being
> swept to prevent it from becoming airborne. Approved, properly fitted respirators designed to
> protect against moulds, bacteria, and specks of dust may help protect the lungs »

## Ce que le livre en tire

Une note de bas de page au §3 : les signes (essoufflement, toux sèche, fièvre et frissons, cœur et
souffle rapides), le délai de 4 à 8 heures, la confusion avec la grippe ou la pneumonie, la
sensibilité qui reste des années, l'aggravation à chaque exposition ; et ce qu'on met entre la
poussière et le poumon avec ce qu'on a : travailler dehors plutôt que dans un bâtiment fermé,
mouiller la poussière avant de la brasser, un masque filtrant s'il y en a un.

**Non repris :** « professional advice is required for the selection of the proper mask » et le
recours au médecin (règle « personne à appeler ») ; les examens médicaux ; le mot « approved »
devant le masque (conformité, pas mécanisme). Aucun masque de fortune n'est proposé : aucune source
ne dit ce qu'un foulard ou un linge mouillé arrête des spores.
