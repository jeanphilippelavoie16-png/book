## Extraits intégraux — section 2, « Backslopping », p. 6 à 8

> « Backslopping is the practice of using finished fermented product that contains live
> microorganisms to inoculate a new batch of product substrate. »

> « 1. Is backslopping being used to promote acid fermentation? Backslopping should only be used
> in acid fermentations since acid producing microorganisms and acidified product minimize risks of
> acid-sensitive pathogens from contaminating backslopped culture. However, acid-tolerant spoilage
> agents and pathogens can persist and may amplify in backslopped culture when cross-contamination
> occurs. »

> « 2. Is the backslopped culture compatible with the fermentation performed? In general terms,
> use of backslopped culture will initiate fermentation faster in comparison to spontaneous
> fermentation. The backslopped culture should originate from the same type of food that is being
> made. For example, sauerkraut juice from a fermented batch can be used to initiate fermentation
> in a new batch of sauerkraut. »

> « 3. How is the operator managing the backslopped culture? Is a fresh batch of culture taken
> from the previous batch, or is the same starter maintained in a refrigerator from an older batch
> (for weeks, months, or longer periods). In general terms, a product in process of active
> fermentation should be used as backslopped culture. Any storage is reducing fermentation
> activity, promoting contamination and undesirable microbial activity. […] Some fermentations are
> accompanied by growth of oxidative yeasts that form a film on the top of product; this part
> should be avoided when collecting product for backslopping. »

> « For food safety, the backslopped culture should be free of pathogens and should be able to
> initiate fermentation rapidly, i.e., within 24 hours. For food quality, if the fermented food
> did not result in the desired flavour, colour or texture, that could be an indication that the
> starter culture is not performing well. »

> « The operator should check pH has dropped (or changed) in an established expected time, such as
> within 24 hours of adding backslopped culture. »

## Ce que la source établit pour le chapitre 77

- Le repiquage est une pratique acceptée pour les fermentations acides, **du même aliment**
  (jus de choucroute pour la choucroute).
- Le risque qui justifie de casser la chaîne est écrit : des agents **acidotolérants** peuvent
  **persister et s'amplifier** d'une fournée à l'autre.
- Critères d'arrêt concrets : la saumure donneuse vient d'une fournée **en pleine activité**, pas
  d'un pot gardé des semaines ; **jamais la partie voilée** (levures en film) ; la fournée
  ensemencée doit **partir en 24 heures** (pH qui baisse) ; un goût, une couleur ou une texture
  ratés signalent une culture qui ne fait plus son travail.

## Ce que la source NE dit PAS

- **Aucun nombre maximal de repiquages successifs.**
- Les analyses de laboratoire qu'elle recommande (E. coli, S. aureus) sont hors de portée et ne
  passent pas au livre.
