## 1. Les conditions ordinaires d'un sauna finlandais

**Hussain J, Cohen M. Clinical Effects of Regular Dry Sauna Bathing: A Systematic Review.**
*Evid Based Complement Alternat Med* 2018:1857413. Texte intégral, introduction :

> « Traditional Finnish saunas are the most studied to date and generally involve short exposures
> (5−20 minutes) at temperatures of 80°C–100°C with dry air (relative humidity of 10% to 20%)
> interspersed with periods of increased humidity created by the throwing of water over heated
> rocks »

> « the higher humidity results in water condensation on the skin and reduced evaporation of
> sweat »

**Kukkonen-Harjula K, Kauppinen K. Health effects and risks of sauna bathing.**
*Int J Circumpolar Health* 2006;65(3):195-205 :

> « Experiments of sauna bathing were accepted if they were conducted in a heated room with
> sufficient heat (80 to 90 degrees C), comfortable air humidity and adequate ventilation. The
> sauna exposure for five to 20 minutes was usually repeated one to three times. »

> « Sauna was well tolerated and posed no health risks to healthy people from childhood to old
> age. »

> « Excepting toxemia cases, no adverse effects of bathing during pregnancy were found, and baths
> were not teratogenic. »

> « Medication in general was of no concern during a bath, apart from antihypertensive medication,
> which may predispose to orthostatic hypotension after bathing. »

## 2. Ce que 20 minutes à 80-90 °C font au corps

**Sohar E et al. Effects of exposure to Finnish sauna.** *Isr J Med Sci* 1976;12(11):1275-82.
60 volontaires de 18 à 63 ans :

> « Pathophysiological effects of exposure to Finnish sauna (80 to 90 C, 30 to 40% relative
> humidity) »

> « Marked physiological changes appeared in the first few minutes in the sauna without any
> prodromal warning. At the 20th min the mean heart rate was 143 +/- 25 (SD) beats/min (greater
> than 160 beats/min in 32% of the subjects). Mean rectal temperature was 38.6 +/- 0.6 (SD) C
> (greater than 39 C in 22%): mean skin temperature was 40.4 +/- 1 (SD) C (greater than 40 C in
> 35%) »

> « The mean total sweat loss was 457 +/- 264 (SD) g. Three subjects experienced syncope, and one
> developed an anginal attack »

> « It is concluded that sauna bathing involves dangers to the bather's health, which may appear
> suddenly, without prodromal warning signs. »

## 3. L'humidité compte à part de la température

**Laatikainen-Raussi I et al. Temperature and humidity independently influence thermoregulatory
responses during Finnish sauna bathing.** *Temperature (Austin)* 2026;13(3):289-299. 50 adultes,
quatre séances de 10 minutes :

> « HR increased markedly during sauna exposure (mean +41.6 bpm), while Tcore rose moderately
> (+0.4°C). Temperature and RH were independently associated with increases in HR (β = 1.40
> bpm/°C; β = 0.37 bpm/%RH) and Tcore (β = 0.032°C/°C; β = 0.0079°C/%RH). »

> « These findings demonstrate that humidity, beyond temperature, appears to independently
> contribute to physiological strain during sauna exposure. »

## 4. Les enfants

**Jokinen E, Välimäki I, Antila K, Seppänen A, Tuominen J. Children in sauna: cardiovascular
adjustment.** *Pediatrics* 1990;86(2):282-8. 61 enfants, 20 adolescents et jeunes adultes, 10
minutes en chambre climatique aux conditions d'un sauna finlandais ordinaire :

> « Systolic and diastolic blood pressures remained unchanged in sauna, but the systolic and
> especially the diastolic blood pressure decreased (P less than .001) in children less than 10
> years of age immediately after the heat exposure; two vasovagal collapses developed. »

> « Cardiac output increased in all but the less than 5-year-old children (P less than .001).
> This was due to a significant decrease in stroke volume (32.9%) (P less than .001) in children
> less than 5 years old. »

> « These changes indicate that the Finnish sauna bath puts great demands on a child's
> circulatory regulation. To avoid possible cardiovascular side effects, particularly small
> children should be supervised carefully during sauna bathing. »

## 5. Les contre-indications et l'alcool

**Hannuksela ML, Ellahham S. Benefits and risks of sauna bathing.** *Am J Med* 2001;110(2):118-26 :

> « it is well tolerated by most healthy adults and children. Sauna bathing does not influence
> fertility and is safe during the uncomplicated pregnancies of healthy women. »

> « Although sauna bathing does not cause drying of the skin-and may even benefit patients with
> psoriasis-sweating may increase itching in patients with atopic dermatitis. »

> « Contraindications to sauna bathing include unstable angina pectoris, recent myocardial
> infarction, and severe aortic stenosis. »

> « alcohol consumption during sauna bathing increases the risk of hypotension, arrhythmia, and
> sudden death, and should be avoided. »

**Laukkanen JA, Laukkanen T, Kunutsor SK. Cardiovascular and Other Health Benefits of Sauna
Bathing.** *Mayo Clin Proc* 2018;93(8):1111-21 — résumé seulement (texte intégral refusé, HTTP
403 sur les deux sites) :

> « Sauna bathing, an activity that has been a tradition in Finland for thousands of years and
> mainly used for the purposes of pleasure and relaxation »

## 6. Les morts

**Kenttämies A, Karkola K. Death in sauna.** *J Forensic Sci* 2008;53(3):724-9. Finlande,
1990-2002 :

> « there are approximately 2 million saunas among the population of 5.2 million »

> « The annual rate of death occurring while in a sauna was less than 2 per 100,000 inhabitants.
> Close to half (51%) of the cases were determined to be natural deaths and exposure to heat was
> the cause of death in 25%. Overall, 50% of all cases were under the influence of alcohol. »

> « The prevention of these deaths should focus on less drinking of alcohol and avoid leaving a
> drunken bather alone in the sauna. »

**Rodhe A, Eriksson A. Sauna deaths in Sweden, 1992-2003.** *Am J Forensic Med Pathol*
2008;29(1):27-31. 77 morts :

> « In 69 cases, the blood alcohol concentration was determined; 49 (71%) of these tested
> positive, often with high concentrations. »

> « Other causes of death were drowning, CO poisoning, O2 deprivation, amphetamine intoxication,
> and burn injuries. »

> « Among the cases found dead in a sauna, all but 2 were found alone. Obviously, bathing alone
> is a risk factor that can easily be avoided »

## Notes de captation

- **Ce qui manque :** aucune de ces sources ne donne une durée « maximale » sûre pour un enfant
  de 4 ou 6 ans. Le chapitre n'en invente pas : il donne la durée de l'étude (10 minutes, avec
  deux évanouissements à la sortie chez les moins de 10 ans) et la pratique finlandaise pour les
  bébés (moins de 5 minutes), et laisse la sortie au premier signe.
- **Mayo Clinic Proceedings et Kukkonen-Harjula :** texte intégral bloqué (403, Cloudflare). Le
  chapitre n'utilise que ce qui est dans les résumés.
- Le chiffre d'un demi-litre de sueur par séance de 20 minutes du chapitre est l'arrondi de la
  moyenne de Sohar (457 g ; écart-type 264 g).
