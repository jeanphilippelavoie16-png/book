## Extraits intégraux

> « In historical times, traditional hunters highly prized beaver meat and fat: about 50% of the
> weight of the animal, i.e. 10 kilos of an average beaver of 20 kilos, was consumable (meat, fat,
> innards). The meat was rich in proteins, iron and minerals, while its fat contained 48.8–53% of
> PUFAs. »

> « The beaver's tail is remarkably large in relation to body size, and plays an important part in
> the storage of energy reserves in the form of fat—a key resource for foragers—the content of
> which changes strongly with the seasons, with the volume doubling through grease storage between
> spring and autumn. »

> « Beavers also provided castoreum, produced by their glands, a substance employed as a universal
> medicine at least since Antiquity, and used by traditional hunters as a lure to attract a range
> of prey animals. »

## Ce que la page établit pour le chapitre 46

- **Environ la moitié du poids vif se mange : 10 kg sur un castor de 20 kg**, en comptant la
  viande, le gras **et les abats**. Ce n'est pas un rendement en viande seule.
- La réserve de gras de la queue **double de volume du printemps à l'automne** : un castor
  d'automne vaut plus qu'un castor de printemps pour le gras (ch. 24, 67).

## Ce que la page NE dit PAS

- Aucun rendement en viande seule, ni par morceau (cuisses, épaules, dos).
