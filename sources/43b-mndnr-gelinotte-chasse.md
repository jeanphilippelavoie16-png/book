- **Organisme :** Minnesota Department of Natural Resources (gouvernement de l'État du
  Minnesota). **Pas un ministère québécois.** Statut : source officielle de chasse. Le Minnesota

## Extraits intégraux

### Où

> « Ruffed grouse are commonly found in aspen forests that were clear-cut by loggers 10 to 20
> years ago. Basically, look for aspen stands in which the diameter of the trees are about the size
> of an adult's forearm or calf. »

> « Gray dogwood bushes are commonly found in alder lowlands. [...] The berries are a favorite of
> grouse. »

> « Grouse also frequent edge cover, which is where mature forest converges with younger growth,
> brushy swamp edges and the short grass. Grouse frequently feed on the hazel catkins and the
> catkins of aspen, ironwood and birch trees. »

> « Indeed, if you are walking with ease you're either in the wrong habitat or ambling down a
> logging trail or hunting trail. »

### Tirer

> « Typically, you have two to three seconds to make a shot after you've seen a grouse burst or a
> woodcock helicopter into the air. [...] If you wait for a clear shot a box of shells will last a
> lifetime. »

> « Typically, you'll be shooting a distances of 25 yards or less so an improved- cylinder or skeet
> choke is a good option. The faster your pattern expands the better off you'll be in most
> situations. Shells loaded with 7 1/2 or 8 shoot are good in most situations. You may want to use
> 6 shot later in the season, the time of year when leaves have fallen to the ground and longer
> shots are more common. »

### Marcher

> « A hunter without a dog should walk slowly through cover in an "S" pattern. You will cover more
> ground this way, and you are more likely to flush birds that have moved laterally. Also, make
> brief stops from time to time. This can have the effect of making grouse and woodcock nervous.
> Repeated 10- to 20-second pauses often flush birds that otherwise would stay on the ground. »

> « A common technique in heavy cover is for one hunter to be the designated leader and the other
> hunter to "bounce off" that person »

### Après le coup manqué

> « unlike far-flying ducks and pheasants, ruffed grouse typically fly only 100 yards or so before
> landing again. As such, by paying attention to the direction the bird flew you can possibly flush
> the bird again. »

### Biologie

> « During winter, ruffed grouse spend nearly all of their time in snow burrows to stay warm and
> avoid predators. A ruffed grouse lives most of its life within just a few acres. »

> « Ruffed grouse populations rise and fall at intervals of about 10 years. Many other species of
> wildlife such as snowshoe hares also cycle at 10-year intervals. »

## Ce que la page ne dit pas

- Rien sur la carabine .22 ni sur le tir de l'oiseau perché.
- Rien sur la viande abîmée.
- Rien sur le tétras du Canada, sauf qu'il se chasse à la même saison.
