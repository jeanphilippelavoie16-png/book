### Extraits intégraux

> « Locate the coldframe/hotbed facing south so plants receive full sun most of the day. The
> site should be convenient for the user, protected from the wind, close to a good supply of
> water and located on a well-drained site free from shade. »

> « Avoid the use of boards treated with wood preservatives because of possible injury to
> plants. »

> « a 6 feet by 6 feet size is common with 3 feet by 6 feet as a minimum. »

> « Hotbeds heated with manure are generally dug 16 to 24 inches deep, whereas a coldframe may
> only be dug 6 to 15 inches deep. »

> « Slope the front of the frame lower than the back (1 inch per foot). As an example, the front
> of the coldframe may be 8 inches above the ground and the back 14 inches if the frame is 6 feet
> wide. […] If the sides are not dug into the ground, bank soil around the frame or add
> insulation boards to conserve heat. A coldframe also may be built as a lean-to against a house
> or garage. However, care should be used to prevent damage from snow or icicles falling from the
> roof onto the cover »

> « Construction of the manure or pit hotbed begins by excavating about 24 inches deep. Spread 4
> inches of coarse gravel on the bottom to help drainage and aeration. Then fill the pit with 8
> to 12 inches of fresh manure mixed 5 to 10 percent with straw, tamped evenly and firmly, and
> moistened with lukewarm water. Cover the manure with 3 to 4 inches of good quality loose,
> weed-free soil. […] When the temperature drops to 75°F, the bed is ready to seed. »

> « Temperatures of 70° to 75°F are best for germinating seed. Once germination occurs, maintain
> the temperature in the frame at plant level at 60° to 65°F for cool season crops and 65° to
> 75°F for warm season crops. […] Ventilation is essential on sunny days to maintain correct
> temperatures. As the season advances, remove the sash for most of the day and eventually during
> the night, too, to "harden off" the plants. »

> « overwatering and over-application of nitrogen fertilizers, coupled with high temperatures,
> encourage diseases and leggy growth. […] most annuals will require 30 to 40 days from seeding
> to transplanting. »

### Extraits (tels que rendus)

> « the pit beneath is made 18 to 30 inches deep to hold the manure. »

> « Tamp down the manure so the total depth of manure is 12 to 18 inches. »

> « cover the manure with a layer of good soil 4 to 6 inches deep »

> « collect the manure into a pile about 10 days before the bed is to be started. Compact and
> allow it to remain until heating starts. Then turn the pile, compact it and allow it to remain
> undisturbed until heat is emitted for a second time. »

> « Wait until soil temperature drops to about 85 degrees Fahrenheit before planting seeds or
> moving in plants. »

> « The back wall (generally the north wall) should be at least 18 inches tall » ; « The front
> wall (generally the south wall) should be about 12 inches tall for a 6-foot frame. »

> « raise the sash on the side opposite that from which the wind is blowing »
