# Le couteau croche : la prise, la lame qui vient vers le ventre, et où tenir la pièce

- **Consultées le :** 24 septembre 2026
- **Sert au chapitre :** 20b, §9, « Les outils qu'on se fait », le couteau croche
  (`[À VÉRIFIER : ce qui arrête la course de la lame avant le corps, et où tenir la pièce]`).
- **Rang :** aucune source officielle ne décrit le geste. Un ouvrage de la Smithsonian Institution
  (1964, domaine public aux États-Unis, texte Gutenberg déjà au dossier) et le site de l'Innu Nation.

## 1. Adney et Chapelle, *The Bark Canoes and Skin Boats of North America* (1964)

- **Éditeur :** Smithsonian Institution, United States National Museum Bulletin 230, 1964
- **URL :** https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/50828
- **Copie :** `sources/85-adney-chapelle-smithsonian-canots-d-ecorce.txt` (déjà au dossier)

La prise :

> « The knife, held with the cutting edge toward the user, was grasped fingers-up with the thumb of
> the holding hand laid along the part of the handle projecting away from the user. This steadied
> the knife in cutting. Unlike a jackknife, the crooked knife was not used to whittle but to cut
> toward the user, and was, in effect, a one-hand drawknife. »

Le chevalet à planer (« shaving horse »), qui tient la pièce :

> « Another pioneer tool that became useful to the Indian canoe builder was the "shaving horse." A
> sort of bench and vise, it was used by Indians in a variety of forms, all based on the same
> principle of construction. Usually a seven-foot-long bench made of a large log flattened on top
> was supported by two or four legs, one pair being high enough to raise that end of the bench
> several feet off the ground to provide a seat for the operator. To the top of the bench was
> secured a shorter, wedge-shaped piece flattened top and bottom, with one end beveled and fastened
> to the bench and the other held about 12 inches above it by a support tenoned into the bench
> about thirty inches from the high end. Through the bench and the shorter piece were cut slots,
> about four feet from the high end of the bench and aligned to receive an arm pivoted on the bench
> and extending from the ground to above the upper slot. The arm was shaped to overhang the slot
> on the front, toward the operator's end of the bench, and on each side. The lower portion of the
> arm was squared to fit the slot, and a crosspiece was secured to, or through, its lower end. »

> « The worker sat astraddle the high end of the bench, facing the low end, with his feet on the
> crosspiece of the pivoted arm. Placing a piece of wood on top of the wedge-shaped piece, close to
> the head of the pivoted arm, he pushed forward on the crosspiece with his feet, thus forcing the
> head down hard upon the wood, so that it was held as in a vise. The wood could then be shaved down
> to a required shape with a drawknife or crooked knife without the necessity of holding the work.
> A long piece was canted on top of the bench so that the finished part would pass by the body of
> the worker, and, if it were necessary to shape the full length, it could be reversed. »

## 2. Tipatshimuna (Innu Nation), « Crooked knife – mukutakan »

- **URL :** https://tipatshimuna.ca/exhibits/crooked-knife-mukutakan/
- **Copie :** `sources/20b-tipatshimuna-couteau-croche-mukutakan.html`

> « This multi-purpose knife was used to shave thin planking and ribs for canoes and toboggans, make
> stretching boards to dry furs, carve wooden dolls for the children, fashion snowshoe frames and
> drums, and many other objects out of wood. »

> « One unusual characteristic of the knife is that it must be drawn towards the body when being
> used. Great care must be taken when using the knife to avoid wounding oneself in the stomach. »

## Ce que ces textes permettent d'écrire

- **La prise** : doigts en dessus, pouce couché le long de la branche du manche qui s'écarte de
  soi ; c'est le pouce qui **tient la lame stable** pendant la coupe. On tire vers soi.
- **Le danger nommé** : la lame vient vers **le ventre**.
- **Où tenir la pièce** : **pas à la main**, au chevalet à planer, que les pieds serrent ; une pièce
  longue posée **de biais**, pour que la partie finie passe **à côté du corps**.
- **Ce qui arrête la course de la lame** : aucune des deux sources ne le dit.
