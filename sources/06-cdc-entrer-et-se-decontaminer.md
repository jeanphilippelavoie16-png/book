## Extraits intégraux (anglais, tels que publiés)

### Dehors ou en auto (Get Inside)

> « Get inside a building right away. Brick or concrete multi-story building or basement are best, but being inside
> any building is safer than being outside.
> Carefully remove your outer layer of clothing before entering the building, if you can. Radioactive material can
> settle on your clothing and your body, like dust or mud.
> Once inside, go to the basement or the middle of the building. Radioactive material settles on the outside of
> buildings. The best thing to do is stay as far away from the walls and roof of the building as you can.
> Wash the parts of your body that were uncovered when you were outside. Then put on clean clothing, if you can.
> If you must be outside and cannot get inside immediately, cover your mouth and nose with a mask, cloth, or towel. »

### Dedans (Get Inside)

> « Close and lock all windows and doors. Go to the basement or the middle of the building. [...] If possible, turn
> off fans, air conditioners, and forced-air heating units that bring air in from the outside. Close fireplace
> dampers. »

### Les proches ailleurs (Get Inside)

> « Stay where you are! Going outside to get loved ones could expose you and them to dangerous levels of radiation.
> Schools, daycares, hospitals, nursing homes, and other places have emergency plans in place to keep people safe at
> the facility. »

### Les animaux (Get Inside)

> « Bring pets inside with you, if you can. [...] Wash your pet carefully with shampoo or soap and water and rinse
> completely. Wash your hands and face after washing your pet. »

### Accueillir quelqu'un qui arrive du dehors (Get Inside)

> « Ask them to remove their outer layer of clothing before entering the building or shelter. Once inside, ask them
> to wash the parts of their body that were uncovered when they were outside. »

### Se décontaminer (Self-Decontaminate)

> « Take off your outer layer of clothing. Taking off your outer layer of clothing can remove up to 90% of
> radioactive material. Be very careful in removing your clothing to prevent inhaling radioactive dust released from
> shaking. Put the clothing in a plastic bag or other sealable container and put the bag far away from other people
> and pets. »

> « If you can't take a shower — At a sink or faucet, wash your hands, face, and parts of your body that were not
> covered. Use soap and plenty of water. If you do not have access to a sink or faucet, use: A moist wipe, A clean
> wet cloth, A damp paper towel. Pay special attention to your hands and face. Gently blow your nose and wipe your
> eyelids, eyelashes, and ears with a moist wipe, a clean wet cloth, or a damp paper towel. Put the used wipes, cloth,
> or towel in a plastic bag or other sealable container. »

> « If you can take a shower — Take a warm shower and gently wash yourself with lots of soap. Do not scald, scrub, or
> scratch your skin. Wash your hair with shampoo or soap. Keep cuts and abrasions covered when washing to stop
> radioactive material from getting in open wounds. »

> « You can still use tap water for decontamination. [...] The tap water will be safe to use for washing Skin, Hair,
> Clothing »

> « Use soap and shampoo. Do not use conditioner because it will cause radioactive material to stick to your hair. »

> « If you do not have clean clothes: Take off your outer layer of clothing, shake or brush off your clothes, and put
> your clothes back on. Rewash your hands, face, and exposed skin at a sink or faucet. »
