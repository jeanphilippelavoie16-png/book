## Extraits intégraux

> « Most values are probably less than 2%, and it may not be uncommon for levels to drop below
> 1%. Moreover, many of the principal fat deposits in an animal may disappear completely, and
> others may be largely depleted »

> « Dauphine (1976) observed that 100% of the backfat in the average adult barren-ground caribou
> had been depleted by spring, and that 70% of the abdominal fat had been mobilized. »

> « Franzmann and Arneson (1976) recorded an average fat level of 8.5% ± 4.1 in femora of
> winter-killed moose. Finally, although no actual figures are provided, Peterson (1977) found
> that bone marrow was almost totally fat-depleted in moose from Isle Royale, Michigan that had
> died of malnutrition during the late winter and spring. »

> « Although the major fat deposits most readily accessible to hunter-gatherers (i.e.,
> subcutaneous and visceral fat) may be largely if not entirely depleted at this time of year,
> there may be smaller amounts of fat remaining in places such as the bone marrow which, while
> labor-intensive to extract, will augment the total fat intake. »

## Ce que l'article NE dit PAS

- **Aucun ordre de vidage entre les os** de la patte (canon, tibia, fémur). Le mot « marrow »
  n'apparaît que pour dire que la moelle reste quand le gras de la peau et des viscères est
  parti, et qu'elle peut elle-même se vider.
- Aucun rendement de graisse d'os.
