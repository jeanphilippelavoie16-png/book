## A. Ce qui ne se ramasse pas

### Actée rouge — *Actaea rubra* (`ncsu-actaea-rubra`, `feis-actrub`)

> « Poison Severity: High » — « Poisonous if ingested. Can cause death in humans and may cause
> death in livestock or pets if eaten in large quantities. Roots and berries are the most toxic
> part of the plant and the berries are very bitter. Symptoms: Gastrointestinal distress and
> burning and blistering of allmucous membranes, lips and mouth. In large quantities heart
> failure. » — « Poison Toxic Principle: cardiotoxic compounds »

> « The fruit matures to glossy red with a dark spot at the tip. They are about 1/3 inch across. »

FEIS : « The fruits are showy, poisonous, red or occasionally white berries » ; « a poisonous
essential oil or glycoside (protoanemonin) found in all parts of the plant but most concentrated
in the berries and root. Symptoms of poisoning include gastroenteritis, stomach cramps, headache,
dizziness, vomiting, diarrhea and circulatory failure » ; « Red baneberry is a deciduous,
perennial herb, usually from 1 to 3 feet (4-10 dm) tall ».

### Actée blanche — *Actaea pachypoda* (`ncsu-actaea-pachypoda`)

> « Specific epithet means with a thick stalk. »

> « All parts of this plant are poisonous but mainly the berries and roots. Use caution when
> planting in an area where children and pets have access. »

> « The fruit is a white, sometimes red berry with a dark purple dot. The berries remain on the
> plant until frost. »

> « Poison Severity: High » — « Poisonous if ingested especially the roots and berries can cause
> death in humans, livestock and pets. [...] Burning of mouth and throat, salivation; severe stomach
> cramps, headache, diarrhea; dizziness and hallucinations can occur. »

Recoupement pédicelle : `57-parcdesfalaises-actees-pedicelles.md` (pédicelles fins chez l'actée
rouge, épais et rouge vif chez l'actée blanche).

### If du Canada — *Taxus canadensis* (`ncsu-taxus-canadensis`, `feis-taxcan`)

> « The seed is borne in a cup-shaped, fleshy structure called an aril, up to 3/8 inch long,
> maturing to bright red, open at the end and exposing the single seed in mid- to late-summer. »

FEIS : « All parts of Canada yew, except for the aril, are poisonous to horses and cattle » ;
« The aril of Canada yew can be eaten by humans ».

Toxicité : `25-ontario-if-du-canada-toxicite.md` (reprise) — aiguilles et graines très
toxiques, frais comme secs, toute l'année, sans antidote ; l'arille rouge ne l'est pas.

### Clintonie boréale — *Clintonia borealis* (`ncsu-clintonia-borealis`)

> « The fruit is a bright, light to dark blue berry that is poisonous. »

> « Poison Severity: Low » — « This plant CAUSES ONLY LOW TOXICITY IF EATEN. No cases are known.
> Poisonous through ingestion. Poisonous parts are suspected to be the berries. »

> « Two to four basal, shiny, bright green, oblong leaves »

### Maïanthème du Canada — *Maianthemum canadense* (`ncsu-maianthemum-canadense`, `feis-maican`)

> « Small, spotted berries turn dark red from August to September. »

> « Berries are eaten by small mammals. »

FEIS : « 9 inches (8-20 cm) tall ». **Ni NCSU ni FEIS ne donnent de toxicité ni d'usage
alimentaire humain.** Le « purgatif » du chapitre n'est pas sourcé.

### Chèvrefeuilles — *Lonicera* spp. (`ncsu-lonicera-japonica`, `-tatarica`, `-maackii`)

> *L. japonica* : « Poison Severity: Medium » — « Vomiting, diarrhea, pupil dilation, cold sweat,
> rapid heartbeat, respiratory failure, convulsions, and coma. TOXIC ONLY IF LARGE QUANTITIES
> EATEN. » — « Poison Part: Fruits »

> *L. tatarica* : « Poison Severity: Low » — « Symptoms include diarrhea, vomiting, and abdominal
> pains. No human deaths have been reported from consumption of the berries. »

> *L. maackii* : « Poison Severity: Low » — « The berries cause severe diarrhea when eaten. »

Aucune fiche pour les chèvrefeuilles indigènes de la Côte-Nord (*L. canadensis*, *L. villosa*) :
404 chez NCSU et chez FEIS.

## B. Ce qui se ramasse avec une précaution

### Sureau rouge — *Sambucus racemosa* (`feis-samrac`, `ncsu-sambucus-pubens`, `ncsu-sambucus-canadensis`)

FEIS : « Red elderberry fruits are used to make pies, jelly, and wine. [...] The fruits are sour,
however, and people do not usually eat them raw. The fruits and/or seeds may cause diarrhea and
vomiting in humans, especially if the fruits are not fully ripe. Other parts of the plant may be
poisonous to humans year-round. »

NCSU, sureau rouge de l'Est (« Previously known as: Sambucus pubens ») : « Edibility: Edible, but
very tart; usually cooked ».

NCSU, sureau blanc (*S. canadensis*), même mécanisme : « Unripe fruits are much more toxic than
fully ripe fruits. Cooking helps inactivate the cyanogenic glycoside. »

### Cerise à grappes — *Prunus virginiana* (`ncsu-prunus-virginiana`, `feis-pruvir`)

NCSU : « Stems, leaves, seeds contain cyanide, particularly toxic in the process of wilting » ;
« Poison Part: Seeds » ; « The red fruit turns black when ripe. »

FEIS : « Chokecherries are edible and, although somewhat astringent, are relatively sweet when
fully ripe. Fruits are used to make wines, syrups, jellies, and jams. Indigenous peoples gathered
chokecherries and used them to make pemmican » ; « Research has identified the toxic compound in
chokecherry as the cyanogenic glycoside prunasin. »

### Sorbier d'Amérique — *Sorbus americana* (`feis-sorame`, `ncsu-sorbus-americana`)

FEIS : « The berries are edible for humans but are too acidic to be eaten raw. They can be cooked
with meats or made into jelly. » — « a native, smooth-barked, deciduous shrub or small tree 10 to
30 feet (3-9 m) tall ». **Rien sur l'effet du gel.**

### Viorne trilobée, pimbina — *Viburnum trilobum* (`ncsu-viburnum-trilobum`)

> « found in wet woods, along streams, and on moist wooded hillsides » — « Fruits are edible and
> used in used in sauces, jellies, and juices. »

> « This shrub has hybridized with the European Viburnum opulus and has escaped cultivation [...].
> The best way to tell them apart is by the petiolar glands at the base of the leaf. [...] Also, the
> fruit of the native plant is edible, and that of the European variety is not. »

### Cynorrhodon — *Rosa acicularis* (`feis-rosaci`)

> « Juice is extracted from the hips by boiling and used to make jellies and syrups. Pulp from the
> hips, after seeds and skins are removed, is used to make jams, marmalades, and catsup. » — les
> akènes : « 3.8-5 mm long with stiff hairs along one side » — « a deciduous shrub about 4 feet
> (1.2 m) in height ».
