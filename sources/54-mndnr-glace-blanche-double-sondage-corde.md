## Extraits intégraux (anglais)

### 1. General ice thickness guidelines — la glace blanche

> « Ice thickness guidelines for new, clear ice only. UNDER 4" Stay off 4" Ice fishing or other
> activities on foot 5" - 7" Snowmobile or small ATV »

> « When a layer of snow melts and refreezes on top of lake ice, it creates white ice, only about
> half as strong as new, clear ice. Double the above thickness guidelines when traveling on white
> ice. »

> « Ice is seldom the same thickness over a single body of water; it can be two feet thick in one
> place and one inch thick a few yards away. »

### 2. How to check ice thickness — sonder en avançant

> « Ice chisels are an especially important tool in the early and late-ice season when the ice is
> not as thick as mid-winter. If you are walking on 4 to 6 inches of ice, you likely will find
> areas of ice that are less than 4 inches on the same lake. With the chisel, you can stab the ice
> in front of you with each step you take to ensure the ice has not suddenly become thinner.
> Checking as you walk with an ice chisel is not a substitute for measuring ice frequently with a
> tape measure. »

> « If you are using an auger to check ice thickness, make sure to start by checking thickness
> near shore and to frequently stop and check as you go. »

> « Don't judge ice thickness by how easily a chisel or drill breaks the surface. It happens so
> quickly that it's easy to overestimate the thickness. »

### 3. Ice safety gear — la corde

> « If you see someone else fall through the ice, stay where you are. Do NOT go to them as you
> will likely end up falling through the ice and adding to the number of victims in the water. »

> « Then try throwing one end of a rope to them, and have them tie the rope around themselves
> before they become too exhausted to hold onto it. While the person in the water kicks their
> feet in a horizontal position, pull on the rope to get them out of the water. If they start to
> pull you closer to the opening in the ice, let go of the rope and start again. »

> « If you do not have a rope, look for any item that you can throw or extend out to the person
> in the water. Items to keep in mind for this situation are jumper cables, long stick or tree
> branch, extension ladder, or other similar items. »

> « Always keep a safe distance from the thin ice so you don't become a victim yourself. »

### 4. What if someone else falls in?

> « THROW - Toss one end of a rope or something that will float to the victim. Have them tie the
> rope around themselves before they are too weakened by the cold to grasp it. »

> « ROW - Find a light boat to push across the ice ahead of you. »

## Ce que le livre en tire

- **Glace blanche : environ deux fois moins solide que la glace claire ; on double les
  épaisseurs.** Recoupé par la Croix-Rouge canadienne (`35-croix-rouge-securite-sur-la-glace.md`) :
  « en général, elle est seulement à moitié aussi solide que la glace bleu pâle ». Le « aucun
  facteur de correction chiffré » du chapitre 54 est faux : deux organismes en donnent un.
- **Sonder à chaque pas** au ciseau en début et en fin de saison ; la tarière, s'arrêter souvent.
  Aucune distance chiffrée entre deux mesures au ruban.
- **Aucune longueur de corde** sur aucune des quatre pages. La fonction est décrite : lancer
  depuis une distance sûre, la personne s'attache, on tire ; si elle tire le sauveteur vers le
  trou, on lâche et on recommence.
- **Aucun diamètre de tarière.**
