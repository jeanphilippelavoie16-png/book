1. Pediatrics. 1990 Aug;86(2):282-8.

Children in sauna: cardiovascular adjustment.

Jokinen E(1), Välimäki I, Antila K, Seppänen A, Tuominen J.

Author information:
(1)Cardiorespiratory Research Unit, University of Turku, Finland.

Children's circulatory adjustment to a 10-minute heat stress in a climatic 
chamber was studied in 61 children and 20 adolescents and young adults. The 
thermal conditions corresponded to those of an ordinary Finnish sauna bath. In 
all subjects the rectal temperature and heart rate increased during the heat 
exposure (P less than .001). Systolic and diastolic blood pressures remained 
unchanged in sauna, but the systolic and especially the diastolic blood pressure 
decreased (P less than .001) in children less than 10 years of age immediately 
after the heat exposure; two vasovagal collapses developed. Cardiac output 
increased in all but the less than 5-year-old children (P less than .001). This 
was due to a significant decrease in stroke volume (32.9%) (P less than .001) in 
children less than 5 years old. Stroke volume declined slightly even in the 
older subjects. These changes indicate that the Finnish sauna bath puts great 
demands on a child's circulatory regulation. To avoid possible cardiovascular 
side effects, particularly small children should be supervised carefully during 
sauna bathing.
