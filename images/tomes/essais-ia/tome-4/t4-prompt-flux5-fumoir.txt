Full-bleed book cover illustration in the style of a hand-carved linocut: rough gouge marks and carving texture in every flat area, paper grain, bold hand-cut shapes with slightly irregular edges, Quebec folk art. The dark ink runs off all four edges, no paper border, no margin, unsigned. Only three colours: very dark slate blue, off-white, warm orange; orange only for firelight and lit windows. An evening at the end of autumn in the boreal forest of the Quebec north shore, the first snow on the ground. The upper half is a plain, calm, empty dark slate blue sky with a thin crescent moon and a few tiny stars. Low rounded forested hills near the horizon. In the snow below, exactly three structures: on the left a small smokehouse of dark weathered planks, a thick white plume of smoke curling from its roof, only its half open door glowing orange from the fire inside; in the middle a long horizontal wooden pole resting between two snow-laden black spruces, with three large meat quarters wrapped in white cloth bags hanging from it, in front of a dark spruce thicket; on the right a small plank door set into a low snowy earth mound, the root cellar. Far away, one small log cabin with a glowing orange window. Tall snow-laden black spruces framing both sides. Soulful, quiet and warm. No text, no letters, no signature, no other buildings, no people, no animals, no blood.
