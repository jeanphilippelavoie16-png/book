Vertical linocut print, hand-carved relief print with visible gouge marks and carving texture, printmaking paper grain, full bleed with no border and no margin. Only three inks: very dark slate blue, off-white paper, warm orange. An evening at the end of autumn in the boreal forest of the Quebec north shore, the first snow on the ground. The upper two fifths of the image is a calm, almost empty dark slate blue sky with a few tiny carved stars and a thin crescent moon, left empty for a title. Low rounded forested hills only near the horizon, below the middle of the image, never reaching into the sky. In the lower half: a small wooden plank smokehouse with a pitched roof, smoke rising in a soft curl from its roof, its door half open with a warm orange glow inside; beside it, a long wooden pole lashed between two black spruces, with three meat quarters wrapped in white cloth bags hanging from it; on the right, a small wooden door of a root cellar dug into a snowy mound; further away, a small log cabin with one glowing orange window and chimney smoke. Tall slender black spruces with snow frame both sides, carved drift lines in the snow. Folk art printmaking, soulful, quiet and warm. No text, no letters, no signature, no people, no animals, no blood.
