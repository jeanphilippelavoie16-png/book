Vertical linocut print, hand-carved relief print with visible gouge marks and carving texture, printmaking paper grain, only three inks: very dark forest green, off-white cream paper and warm orange. Full bleed, the print fills the whole image edge to edge, no border, no margin. Autumn dawn on the salt-water estuary of the St. Lawrence river on the Côte-Nord of Quebec, so wide it looks like the sea. The upper half of the image is a huge calm empty sky printed as a flat block of dark forest green ink with faint carving marks, completely empty: no birds, no clouds, no stars, no moon. Near the horizon a few carved horizontal lines of cream and orange. The sun just rising, half above the flat horizon of the water, below the middle of the image, with a long orange path of light on the water. Far away a thin low shore. A rocky point on the right with black spruces and golden orange tamaracks. In the water, a harbour seal resting on a rock, and a traditional fish weir: a line of wooden stakes standing in the tidal water. A low flight of Canada geese skimming just above the water. Lower third: a pale pebble beach at low tide with orange seaweed, one small wooden rowboat pulled up on the beach. Folk art printmaking, soulful, handmade. No text, no letters, no signature, no people.
