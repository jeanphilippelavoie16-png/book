Full-bleed vertical linocut print, rough hand-carved relief print: every shape cut with a gouge, visible gouge strokes and chisel marks in the snow, on the walls and on the hills, uneven inked edges, slight ink speckle, textured printmaking paper, printed edge to edge. Only three inks: dark warm charcoal grey, off-white paper and warm orange. Winter evening in the boreal forest. The upper half is a plain, calm, dark warm charcoal grey sky, almost empty, with only a few tiny stars and no moon, left empty for a title. Low rounded hills carved with fine parallel white lines, a band of small black spruces at the horizon, all below the middle of the image. In the lower half: a plank workshop with a snowy gable roof, both doors swung wide open on the orange glow of a small blacksmith forge with a hood, a black anvil on a log stump, tongs and hammers hanging on the back wall, a few orange sparks, a short puff of smoke from the stovepipe, carved orange light on the snow in front of the door, a small log cabin with a lit window on the left, a woodpile, an axe in a chopping block, snow-laden black spruces carved with white lines on both sides. Folk art printmaking, soulful, handmade. No moon, no text, no letters, no signature, no initials, no animals, no people.
